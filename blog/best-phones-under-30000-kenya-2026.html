4G or 5G: which should you choose?

A 5G phone can connect to a 5G network only where your mobile operator provides coverage and where the device supports the relevant bands. Coverage is location-specific and can change, so check the operator's current coverage map for the places where you work, live and travel. A 5G label by itself does not guarantee faster service in every location.

If most of your daily use is WhatsApp, calls, M-Pesa, browsing and video streaming, a well-matched 4G phone may be entirely adequate. You may get more physical RAM, storage or battery capacity for the same budget by choosing a 4G model. If you regularly download large files, use hotspot or expect to keep the phone for several years, 5G support may be a useful extra.

Physical RAM and storage: don't confuse the numbers

Compare physical RAM separately from memory expansion. Some sellers display a combined figure such as 8GB plus virtual RAM. Virtual RAM borrows from storage and is not the same as 8GB of physical memory. For frequent app switching, physical RAM is the more useful number to compare. For photos, video and apps, internal storage matters; 128GB is more flexible than 64GB, while 256GB offers still more room.

Display, camera and battery trade-offs

AMOLED screens generally offer deep blacks and strong contrast; refresh rates of 90Hz or 120Hz can make scrolling feel smoother. Compare resolution and brightness as well, because refresh rate alone does not tell you how sharp or readable a screen is outdoors. For cameras, look for stabilisation, autofocus, ultra-wide capability and real sample images rather than choosing only by megapixel count.

Battery capacity is measured in mAh, but it is not a direct promise of hours of use. A larger display, high refresh rate, mobile signal strength and gaming can affect battery drain. Check the charging speed and confirm whether the charger is included in the exact package you will receive.

How to make the final choice

  • Choose a 5G model only if the network is useful in your regular locations.
  • Prioritise physical RAM if you keep many apps open.
  • Choose more internal storage if you save lots of photos, video and offline files.
  • Check the exact model code, SIM configuration and network bands.
  • Get warranty provider, return conditions and included accessories in writing.
  • Match the IMEI on the handset, box and receipt when the phone arrives.
