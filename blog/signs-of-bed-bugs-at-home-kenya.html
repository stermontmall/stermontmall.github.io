Signs of bed bugs at home in Kenya and where to check first

Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Look for small rusty or dark spots on sheets, tiny pale shed skins, live flat brown insects about the size of an apple seed, and itchy bites in lines or clusters. Check mattress seams, the bed frame and the headboard first, because bed bugs hide close to where people sleep.

Waking up with itchy marks is unpleasant, and it is natural to wonder whether bed bugs are to blame. The trick is to confirm before you spend money on treatment. Bed bugs are small and secretive, but they leave a trail of clues once you know where to look.

What do bed bugs look like?

Adult bed bugs are flat, oval, reddish-brown insects roughly the size of an apple seed. After feeding they look rounder and darker. Young bed bugs are smaller and paler, and the eggs are tiny and whitish, often hidden in cracks. Because they are so small, you may see their signs long before you see the insects.

What are the first signs of bed bugs at home?

The most common clues show up on bedding and the mattress. Look for small dark or rusty spots on sheets and pillowcases, which can be droppings or crushed bugs. Pale, empty shed skins and tiny eggs along seams are other signs. Some people also notice a sweet, musty smell in heavily affected rooms.

  • Dark or rusty spots on sheets, mattress and nearby walls
  • Shed skins and tiny whitish eggs in seams and crevices
  • Live bugs on seams, tags, headboards or frames
  • New itchy bites after sleeping in the same place

How can I tell bed bug bites from other bites?

Bed bug bites are small, red and itchy, and they often appear on skin exposed at night, such as the face, neck, arms and hands. They may form lines or clusters. However, mosquito, flea and other insect bites can look very similar, and some people barely react to bed bug bites at all. Bites alone are not proof, so look for the other signs too.

Where should I check first?

Start with the bed. Strip the sheets and inspect the mattress seams, piping and tags, then the box base, bed frame joints and headboard. A torch and an old card help you reach cracks. Next, check bedside furniture, sofas, curtain folds, skirting boards and behind picture frames or wall sockets.

What should I do after confirming them?

Wash and dry bedding on a hot setting, vacuum the mattress and bed frame, and avoid moving infested furniture to other rooms. Then decide how to treat. Frequently asked questions

Can I have bed bugs if I never see them?

Yes. Bed bugs are small, hide during the day and come out mostly at night, so many people spot the signs before the insects themselves. Look for dark spots, shed skins and bites that keep appearing after you sleep in the same bed.

Do bed bugs only live in beds?

No. They are found most often near where people sleep, but they can also hide in sofas, curtain folds, wall sockets, skirting boards and behind picture frames, especially once numbers grow. Check the whole room, not only the mattress.

Are bed bugs a sign that my home is dirty?

No. Bed bugs travel on luggage, clothes and second-hand furniture and can appear in clean homes, hotels and hostels alike. What matters is finding them early and responding calmly rather than blaming cleanliness.

Should I treat as soon as I see one bug?

First confirm what you have and where it is hiding. Then follow the product label carefully, or ask a qualified professional. Treating without finding the hiding places often spreads the problem or leaves eggs behind, so a second check after treatment is important.

Catching bed bugs early makes everything easier and cheaper. A careful check of the bed and the surrounding furniture usually tells you what you need to know.
