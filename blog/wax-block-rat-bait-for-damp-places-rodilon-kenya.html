Rat bait for damp places: when Rodilon wax blocks make sense

Updated 30 October 2026 · 2 min read

Quick answer: Use wax blocks where moisture would spoil loose pellets: basements, drains, cellars, stores near the kitchen, farm sheds and poultry houses. Rodilon blocks are made to hold up in damp, humid places. Put them along walls and near holes, above the wet floor where possible, in covered bait stations.

Ordinary baits can soften, grow mould or lose their appeal when they get wet. If your rat problem is in a damp corner, the form of the bait matters as much as the brand name on the pack.

Why do wax blocks suit damp areas?

Wax blocks are made to resist moisture, so they stay in good condition for longer in basements, drains, cellars, stores near the kitchen, farm sheds and poultry houses.

Rats and mice can gnaw the blocks, and because a block is solid it is also useful where pellets might be carried off or scattered.

Where should I put the blocks?

Put blocks along walls, near holes and burrows, behind appliances and in dark corners where you see droppings or gnaw marks. In a damp area, keep them above the wet floor where you can.

Use covered bait stations that keep out children, pets and livestock, check them often and replace any block that has been eaten or damaged. The 10 g pack is a small size for kitchens, drains, small stores and sheds, so you can bait one trouble spot without buying a large pack.

Keep the blocks away from children, pets and livestock, and check the PCPB registration number on the label before use. Collect dead rodents with gloves and dispose of them safely.

What fixes the damp problem itself?

Bait is only half of the answer, because rodents come for water as well as food. Deal with the conditions that bring them in:

  • Fix leaking pipes that give rodents water
  • Store grain and feed in sealed containers
  • Block gaps around pipes, doors and roofs
  • Keep rubbish covered and take it out regularly

Frequently asked questions

Why choose a wax block instead of pellets?

Blocks are made to resist moisture, so they hold up in damp places where loose pellets can go mouldy or spoil quickly.

Where can I use Rodilon blocks?

They are sold for rats and mice in damp areas, stores and farms, including kitchens, drains, small stores and sheds.

How do I handle and store the blocks?

Wear gloves to handle them and wash your hands afterwards. Keep unused blocks in their pack, dry and out of reach, away from food and animal feed.
