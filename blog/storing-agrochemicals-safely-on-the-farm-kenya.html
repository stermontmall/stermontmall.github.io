How to Store Agrochemicals Safely on Your Farm in Kenya

Updated 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: To store agrochemicals safely on your farm in Kenya, keep all products in their original labelled containers inside a dedicated, well-ventilated, locked storage shed. Ensure the space is strictly separated from human food, animal feed, water sources, seeds, and living quarters to prevent accidental poisoning and cross-contamination.

Proper farm chemical safety protects your family, livestock, workers, and crops from accidental exposure or contamination. Whether you manage a smallholding or a large commercial acre, setting up an organized storage area for your agricultural supplies is a fundamental part of good farming practice. When you buy farm inputs from our Agrovet category, maintaining strict storage protocols ensures those inputs remain effective and safe until application.

Why is safe pesticide storage important on the farm?

Proper pesticide storage prevents tragic accidents involving children, farmhands, and livestock. Unsecured chemicals left in living areas, kitchens, or cattle sheds present an immediate hazard to human and animal health. Storing your farm products correctly also preserves their shelf life, preventing extreme temperature changes or moisture from ruining valuable inputs before you get to use them.

When farm inputs are kept disorganized, there is a serious risk of mixing up products or applying the wrong formula by mistake. Dedicated storage protects your financial investment and ensures your farm complies with standard agricultural safety practices. If you also store grain or operate storage facilities on site, combining proper chemical management with rodent management strategies—as discussed in our guide on keeping rats out of a store—keeps your entire facility safe and clean.

How should you set up a dedicated agrochemical storage space?

A safe chemical storage area should ideally be a standalone shed or a lockable room completely detached from house kitchens, grain stores, and livestock housing. The structure needs to be cool, dry, well-ventilated, and protected from direct sunlight and rain. High temperatures and direct sun can degrade chemical formulations or cause liquid containers to swell and leak due to pressure buildup.

Concrete floors are preferred over bare soil or timber because concrete is non-absorbent and much easier to clean if a liquid spill occurs. Install sturdy metal or sealed wooden shelves so products stay raised off the ground. Keep liquid formulations on lower shelves and dry powders or dusts on upper shelves. This simple arrangement prevents a leaking bottle from dripping onto and ruining dry packages stored underneath.

Always keep the storage area locked key-in-hand, with access restricted strictly to trained, authorized adult farm workers. Clearly mark the outside of the door with visible warning signs indicating that hazardous agricultural chemicals are stored inside.

Why must agrochemicals remain in their original labelled containers?

Never transfer any crop protection product or livestock treatment into soda bottles, drinking water jerrycans, cooking oil tins, or unlabelled jars. Decanting chemicals into everyday beverage or food containers is one of the leading causes of accidental poisoning on farms, especially among young children who mistake the liquid for juice or water.

Original containers carry critical manufacturer labels detailing active ingredients, regulatory registration numbers, safety warnings, emergency first-aid measures, and handling precautions. If someone accidentally ingests or comes into contact with a product, medical responders require the original label to determine proper medical treatment.

Furthermore, original packaging is specifically designed by manufacturers to withstand the chemical properties of the product. Using non-approved containers can cause plastic to dissolve or corrode, leading to dangerous leaks. If a label becomes torn or unreadable over time, secure a duplicate label or write the exact product name and main safety notes clearly on the bottle with a permanent marker.

How do you handle spills and dispose of empty containers safely?

Accidental spills require immediate and calm handling. Keep a simple spill response kit near your storage shed containing dry sand, sawdust, a broom, a dustpan, heavy-duty rubber gloves, and sturdy plastic waste bags. If a liquid chemical spills, quickly cover it with dry sand or sawdust to absorb the fluid. Never wash chemical spills away with a water hose, as the runoff can contaminate nearby water wells, streams, or agricultural soil.

Sweep the saturated absorbent material carefully into a heavy-duty disposal bag, seal it tightly, and mark it clearly for safe disposal according to local environmental rules. Wash your protective gloves and hands thoroughly with soap and clean water immediately after finishing the cleanup.

Empty chemical containers must never be reused for storing domestic water, food, or animal feed, regardless of how thoroughly you wash them. Rinse empty liquid containers three times with clean water, pouring the rinse water directly into your sprayer tank while preparing a spray mixture. Once triple-rinsed, puncture, crush, or break the plastic containers so they cannot be reused, and dispose of them through approved collection points or local agricultural disposal programs.

Frequently asked questions

Can I store farm chemicals inside my family home or kitchen?

No, farm chemicals should never be stored in living quarters, kitchens, or bedrooms. Vapors from volatile products can cause health issues, and accidental spills or leaks can contaminate food, cooking utensils, and drinking water, creating severe health risks for your family.

Where can I find correct mixing ratios and application safety rules?

Always read the product label on the original container carefully before opening or using any chemical. Manufacturer labels provide exact instructions, safety warnings, and protective gear requirements. For specific agricultural advice, consult your local extension officer or a qualified professional.

What should I do if an agrochemical label falls off or gets damaged?

If a label is damaged or missing, immediately identify the product and write its full name and key safety warnings clearly on the container using a permanent marker. If you cannot identify the contents with complete certainty, do not use it; arrange for safe disposal.

How far away from water wells should agrochemical storage be located?

Chemical storage sheds should be situated far down-slope from water wells, rivers, boreholes, and rain harvest tanks. Maintaining a safe distance prevents accidental chemical spills or heavy rainfall runoff from leaching into your farm's drinking and irrigation water sources.
