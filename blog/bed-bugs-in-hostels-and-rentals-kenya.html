Bed bugs in hostels, hotels and rentals: prevention for landlords

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Inspect beds at every changeover, launder bedding hot, seal cracks and gaps, respond fast to guest reports, and treat affected rooms carefully following the product label. Check neighbouring rooms, re-inspect after treatment, and ask a qualified professional if the problem is large or keeps returning.

For landlords, hostel managers and Airbnb hosts, bed bugs are as much a business risk as a pest problem. They travel in with luggage and second-hand furniture, they spread between rooms, and one bad review can empty your bookings. A routine, rather than panic, is what protects you.

Why are hostels and rentals so exposed?

Many guests means many bags, and bed bugs ride in luggage, clothes and bedding. In shared dormitories, neighbouring bunks and adjoining rooms make it easy for them to spread. Rentals with turnover of tenants and second-hand furniture face the same risk. This is not a sign of poor cleaning, so avoid blaming guests or staff.

What should I check at every changeover?

Strip the bed and inspect mattress seams, tags, the box base, frame joints and headboard using a torch. Look for dark spots, shed skins and live bugs. Spend a minute on bedside tables and nearby furniture. Our guide to the signs of bed bugs shows exactly what to look for.

  • Mattress seams, piping and tags
  • Bed frame joints and headboard
  • Bedside tables, sofas and curtain folds
  • Skirting boards, sockets and picture frames

How do I reduce the risk between guests?

Use mattress and pillow protectors with smooth surfaces, which remove hiding places and make inspections quicker. Keep beds slightly away from walls, avoid bedding that touches the floor, and wash and hot-dry linen at each changeover. Seal cracks in walls, skirting and furniture, and limit clutter. Ask guests to keep luggage off beds, and provide a luggage rack or hard surface if you can.

What do I do if a guest reports bites?

Respond quickly and politely, offer another room, and then inspect the reported room and the rooms next to it, above and below. Do not assume a bite is a bed bug bite, because other insects look similar, but take every report seriously. Take the room out of use until you have checked it.

How should I treat a confirmed room?

Bag and hot-wash bedding, vacuum the bed and surroundings, seal and dispose of the vacuum contents, then apply a suitable product only as its label directs. Frequently asked questions

How often should I inspect rooms between guests?

Check mattress seams, bed frames and headboards at every changeover, and do a deeper inspection of furniture, curtains and skirting boards on a regular schedule, such as monthly. Frequent short checks find a small problem before guests notice it.

What should I do if a guest reports bites?

Respond promptly and politely, offer to move the guest to another room, then inspect the original room and its neighbours. Do not blame the guest. Bites alone do not prove bed bugs, so look for the other signs before you treat.

Can I wash my way out of a bed bug problem?

Hot washing and drying kills bed bugs on bedding and clothes, but it will not reach those hiding in the bed frame, walls or furniture. Combine laundering with careful cleaning and a suitable room treatment used strictly according to the label.

Should I treat one room or the whole building?

Bed bugs move between adjoining rooms through gaps, pipes and shared walls, so inspect the rooms next door, above and below. In large or repeated cases, ask a licensed operator to plan a building-wide approach.

How do I check that the treatment worked?

Inspect the room again after a week or two, and then at intervals, looking for new spots, live bugs and bites. Many labels call for a repeat treatment, so follow the label and keep notes of every check.

Quick checks, clear records and a calm response protect both your guests and your reputation.
