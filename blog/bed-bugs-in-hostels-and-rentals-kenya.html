Bed bugs in hostels and rentals: a prevention guide for landlords

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Landlords and hostel managers can prevent bed bugs by inspecting rooms between tenants, checking beds regularly, sealing cracks, reducing clutter and acting fast on every report. If bugs appear, isolate the room, treat as the label directs and follow up. For several rooms, use a licensed operator.

Hostels, hotels, Airbnbs and rental houses see many people come and go, which makes them an easy route for bed bugs to travel. A single suitcase or second-hand mattress can bring them in, and one complaint can quickly turn into several. This guide gives landlords and managers in Kenya a practical routine for prevention, response and follow-up.

Why are hostels and rentals at higher risk?

Bed bugs travel on luggage, clothes, bags and furniture, so places with frequent turnover and shared walls are exposed. In a hostel, bugs can move through gaps along pipes, sockets and skirting boards from one room to the next. Second-hand beds and mattresses, which are common in rentals, can also carry them in.

Bed bugs are not a sign of poor cleaning, but clutter and cracks give them more hiding places and make them harder to find.

How often should I inspect rooms?

Inspect every room between tenants or guests, and keep a short written checklist so the job is done the same way each time. In hostels, check beds and mattresses in occupied rooms at regular intervals, such as monthly, and check again whenever a complaint comes in. Look at mattress seams, bed frames, headboards, bedside furniture and the wall behind the bed. Our guide to the signs of bed bugs shows what to look for.

Train cleaning staff to report dark spots on sheets, shed skins or live bugs immediately, and not to move bedding from a suspicious room into others.

What should I do between tenants or guests?

Strip all bedding and wash it on a hot cycle if the fabric allows it, then dry it well. Vacuum mattress seams, bed frames and the floor edges, and empty the vacuum into a sealed bag outside. Seal cracks along skirting boards and around sockets and pipes, and repair loose wallpaper where bugs can hide.

Use zip-up mattress covers designed for bed bugs and avoid accepting second-hand mattresses or furniture without inspecting them carefully. Keep rooms uncluttered so inspections are quick and thorough.

What do I do when a tenant reports bed bugs?

Respond quickly and calmly. Thank the tenant for reporting, inspect the room and the rooms next to, above and below it, and record what you find. Avoid moving the tenant's belongings into other rooms until they have been checked.

Then treat the affected rooms. Follow the label of any insecticide exactly and check our guide on how to get rid of bed bugs in Kenya for the full process. You can compare options in the bed bug killers category. Plan a second inspection about two weeks later, because eggs can hatch after the first treatment.

When should I call a licensed operator?

Call a licensed operator if bed bugs appear in more than one room, return after treatment, or affect a hostel or hotel with many guests. A professional can inspect the whole building, treat all affected areas together and arrange follow-up visits. Keep a record of dates, rooms and products used, as it helps show that you acted responsibly.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I inspect rooms for bed bugs?

Inspect each room between tenants or guests, and check mattresses and beds in occupied rooms regularly, for example monthly in a hostel. Increase checks if a complaint arrives, because bed bugs can spread to neighbouring rooms quickly.

Who is responsible for bed bug treatment in a rental?

Responsibility depends on your tenancy agreement and local rules, so check what your contract says. In practice, acting fast on a report and treating the affected rooms yourself or through an operator limits cost and protects your reputation.

Do I need to treat the whole building?

Not always, but you should inspect the rooms next to, above and below the affected one. If bugs are found in several rooms, treat all affected areas together, since treating one room alone can push the bugs next door.

Can I use mattress covers to help?

Zip-up mattress covers made for bed bugs remove many hiding places and make inspection easier, because bugs cannot get into the mattress seams. They help, but they do not replace inspecting the frame, walls and furniture.

When should I hire a licensed operator?

Hire a licensed operator when bed bugs appear in several rooms, when they return after your treatment, or when you manage a hostel or hotel with many guests. A planned programme with follow-up visits is usually more effective.
