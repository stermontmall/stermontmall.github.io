How do I tell fleas, ticks and lice apart?

Spotting the difference between these three common pests helps you choose the right approach for your home and animals. Many people looking into fleas Kenya problems notice tiny, dark insects that jump high onto carpets, sofas or ankles.

Ticks are usually round, flat or swollen, and attach themselves firmly to animal skin or human skin while feeding on blood. Lice, on the other hand, are smaller, flat insects that stay close to the skin or hair roots of animals, often causing intense itching and visible nits attached to hair strands.

  • Fleas: Small, dark brown, and known for powerful jumping ability.
  • Ticks: Slow-moving or stationary arachnids that firmly attach to skin.
  • Lice: Small, wingless insects that crawl slowly through hair and fur.

Why must I treat the home and the animal separately?

A common mistake is treating only the animal while ignoring the surrounding environment. Adult parasites often spend a significant portion of their lifecycle off the host, hiding in cracks in the floor, pet bedding, carpets, and dark corners.

When dealing with ticks home infestations or fleas hiding in indoor spaces, you need a dual strategy. Treat your pets using products meant specifically for animals, and treat your floors, walls, and premises with appropriate structural treatments. Doing both prevents the pests from immediately jumping back onto your pets or family members.

How should I handle lice control around livestock and pets?

Effective lice control requires checking your animals regularly, especially during cooler or wetter seasons when livestock and pets tend to crowd together indoors or in sheds.

Look for signs of constant scratching, patches of missing hair, or thickened skin. Because different species affect cattle, sheep, goats, dogs, and poultry, always identify the specific animal affected before applying any product.

  • Inspect animals regularly around the neck, ears, and base of the tail.
  • Isolate new animals introduced to your herd or pack until checked.
  • Keep animal housing clean, dry, and well-ventilated.

Frequently asked questions

Can fleas and ticks live in my house without pets?

Yes, pests can survive and hide in carpets, furniture, and floor cracks even if pets are removed. Eggs and larvae can remain dormant until conditions become favorable, which is why treating both the animals and the indoor environment is essential.

How do I know if my livestock have lice?

Animals with lice often rub against fences, walls, or trees constantly. You may notice rough coats, thinning hair, and restless behavior. Part the hair in bright daylight to look closely at the skin.

Can I use household sprays directly on my pets?

No, you should never use general household surface sprays directly on animals. Always use products formulated and registered specifically for animals, and consult a vet for safe usage.
