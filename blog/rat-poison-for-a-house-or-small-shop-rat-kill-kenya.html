Rat poison for a house or small shop: starting with Rat-Kill

Updated 30 October 2026 · 2 min read

Quick answer: Rat-Kill pellets are a ready-to-use bait for rats and mice in homes, shops, stores and farms. The small 25 g pack suits a single room, a kitchen or a small shop. Place the pellets close to droppings, along walls and near nesting holes, out of reach of children, pets and livestock.

Few people want a large pack of poison for one kitchen. Rat-Kill is sold in a small 25 g pack, sized for a single room, a kitchen, a small shop or a first treatment, so you can start without buying more than you need.

What does the 25 g pack cover?

Think of it as a first treatment for a small space. There is no mixing and no sprayer, so you place the pellets directly where you see rodent activity.

If the problem turns out to be bigger than one room, a store or a farm will need a larger rodent bait. That is the sign to plan more bait points.

Where do I place the pellets?

Look for droppings, gnaw marks, greasy rub marks along walls and holes where rodents live and travel. Place the bait close to those signs, along walls and near nesting holes, in places that children, pets and livestock cannot reach.

Pick your bait spots before you open the pack, so the pellets go down quickly and stay out of reach. Check the bait often, replace bait that has been eaten and remove dead rodents safely, wearing gloves.

Close the pack after each use and keep it out of reach, in a cool, dry cupboard or store that can be locked.

What should I fix at the same time?

Baiting and hygiene work together. Rats and mice eat and spoil stored food, gnaw electrical wires and spread disease through droppings and urine, and they breed quickly, so a few can become a large problem within weeks.

  • Cover food and keep grain and feed in sealed containers
  • Block holes and gaps around pipes, doors and floors
  • Keep the area clean and free of crumbs and spilled feed
