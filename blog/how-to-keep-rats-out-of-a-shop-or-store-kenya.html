How to keep rats out of a shop or store in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: To keep rats out of a shop or store, block every gap with strong materials, keep goods off the floor and away from walls, store food in closed containers, remove waste daily and inspect regularly. Baits and traps help, but proofing and cleanliness stop rats coming back.

Shops and stores are ideal homes for rats because they offer food, shelter and quiet corners. One rat can chew through sacks, packaging and cables and leave droppings that spoil stock and worry customers. This guide shows a simple routine for shop owners: how to close entry points, store goods, manage waste and use baits or traps safely as one part of the plan.

Why do rats target shops and stores?

Rats go where they find food, water and safe hiding places close together. A shop with grain, flour, snacks, animal feed or leftover food, plus piles of boxes and sacks, offers all three. Back rooms that are rarely cleaned and goods stacked against the wall give them the cover they like.

Shops near markets, open drains, rubbish heaps or restaurants face extra pressure, because rats already live nearby and will test every gap in your building.

How do I block the ways rats get in?

Walk around the building, inside and out, in daylight and again with a torch. Look at the base of doors, gaps around pipes and cables, drain covers, air bricks, the roof edge and any cracks in walls or floors. Rats can squeeze through gaps about the width of a thumb.

Use materials rats cannot chew. Cement, metal mesh, metal plates and fine metal sheeting work well, while wood, plastic and foam are quickly gnawed through. Fit a strong strip at the bottom of doors, keep doors closed after deliveries and repair broken drain grates. Trim plants and branches that touch the roof.

How should I store goods so rats stay away?

Lift goods off the floor and keep them away from walls so you can see underneath and clean around them. Use shelves, pallets or racks and leave a clear gap. Store loose foods, grain, flour and animal feed in strong closed containers instead of open sacks, and use stock in the order it arrived so nothing sits for months.

  • Keep stock off the floor and away from walls
  • Use closed, chew-resistant containers for loose food and feed
  • Do not leave empty boxes and sacks piled in corners
  • Clean spills straight away
  • Leave a clear walkway so you can inspect all areas

How should I handle waste and cleaning?

Waste is a free meal for rats. Use bins with tight lids, empty them daily and keep the waste area away from the shop door. Do not leave rubbish bags on the ground overnight. Sweep and wipe floors, shelves and the back room often, and fix leaking taps or pipes that give rats water.

Cleaning also helps you notice early signs. Our guide to the signs and control of rats and mice explains what to look for during your inspections.

Where do baits and traps fit in?

Baits and traps remove the rats that are already inside, but they work best after you have blocked entry points and removed easy food. Choose the method carefully, because baits are poisonous and glue boards and traps raise safety and welfare issues. Always follow the product label, use tamper-resistant stations where directed, keep baits away from food, children and pets, and check and remove carcasses safely. If you run a food business or a large store, ask a licensed operator for a planned programme.

Frequently asked questions

How high should I stack goods off the floor?

Keep goods on shelves or pallets, ideally with a clear gap under them and away from the wall, so you can see and clean underneath. This removes hiding places and makes droppings and gnaw marks easier to spot during checks.

Can a shop cat keep rats away?

A cat may catch the odd rat, but it cannot control an infestation and does not replace proofing, good storage and hygiene. Rats can also carry germs and leave droppings in stock. Treat a cat as a small extra, not a plan.

How often should I inspect my store for rats?

Do a quick check every day when opening, looking for droppings and chewed packs, and a full inspection of corners, roofs and entry points once a week. Write down what you find so you can see whether the problem is growing.

Is it safe to use rat bait in a shop?

Baits are poisonous, so use them only as the product label directs, in closed tamper-resistant stations placed out of reach of children and pets and away from food. For food premises or large stores, ask a licensed operator.

Why do rats keep coming back after I treat?

Rats return when food, shelter or entry points remain. Treatment alone removes the rats you have today, while open gaps, loose waste and goods stored on the floor invite new ones. Proofing and hygiene are what keep them out.
