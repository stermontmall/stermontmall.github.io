Solar system for your home in Kenya: what you need

Updated 15 October 2026 · 5 min read

Solar can give you power during outages and lower your bills over time. Before you buy, it helps to know what each part does and how to work out the size you need.

The main parts of a solar system

Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity. A charge controller protects the battery from overcharging, and comes in simpler PWM types and more efficient MPPT types. A battery stores power for the evening and night. An inverter changes the battery's DC power into the AC power your sockets use. Cables, fuses or breakers and a proper mounting frame complete the system.

Step 1: List what you want to power

Write down each appliance, its power in watts and how many hours a day you use it. Lights, a phone charger, a TV and a laptop use little power. Fridges run all day, and heaters, irons and cookers use a lot, so they are usually left off a small system.

Step 2: Work out daily energy

Multiply watts by hours for each item and add them up. This gives your daily energy in watt-hours. For example, a 50 watt TV used for 4 hours uses about 200 watt-hours a day.

Step 3: Size the panels, battery and inverter

Kenya gets plenty of sun, and many places see roughly 4 to 6 hours of strong sunshine a day. Panels must produce more than you use, because some energy is lost in the system. The battery should hold enough for the evening and night, and the inverter must handle the total watts of everything running at the same time, with some spare room.

Battery types

Deep-cycle lead-acid and gel batteries are common and cost less at first. Lithium batteries usually cost more but last longer and are lighter. Use batteries made for solar, not ordinary car batteries.

Safety and installation

Use the right cable size, fuses and good connections, and keep batteries in a ventilated place. For larger systems or roof mounting, use a qualified installer.
