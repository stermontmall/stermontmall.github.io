Home solar system in Kenya: how to size it

Updated 29 October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: for lights, a TV, phone chargers and Wi-Fi, a 250 to 300 W panel, a 12 V 150 Ah battery and a 500 W pure sine wave inverter is a typical starting system. Add a fridge and you will need roughly double.

Blackouts, a high KPLC bill, or a home outside the grid: these are the usual reasons Kenyans go solar. The good news is that Kenya gets plenty of sun, so a well-sized system works almost anywhere from Mombasa to Eldoret. Here is how to size one before you spend any money.

The parts of a solar system

Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity. A charge controller protects the battery; MPPT types cost more than PWM but waste less power. The battery stores energy for the evening and night. The inverter turns the battery's DC into 240 V AC for your sockets. Add the right cables, fuses and a sturdy mounting frame.

Worked example: a small home

Multiply each appliance's watts by the hours you use it. Here is a typical small household:

Panel: with about 5 hours of strong sun and some system losses, divide 730 Wh by roughly 3.5 to get about 210 W, so a 250 to 300 W panel gives you a safe margin. Battery: lead-acid and gel batteries should only be drained about halfway, so double the energy to about 1,500 Wh, which is a 12 V 150 Ah battery. Inverter: it must cover everything running at once, so 500 W is plenty for this list.

What about a fridge?

A medium fridge uses around 1 kWh a day and also draws a surge when the compressor starts. Plan for a 600 to 1,000 W inverter and at least double the panel and battery above. Irons, heaters, electric kettles and cookers use too much power for a small system. Keep using gas or charcoal for cooking.

Battery types

Deep-cycle lead-acid and gel batteries cost less up front. Lithium (LiFePO4) costs more but lasts longer, is lighter and can be drained deeper. Never use an ordinary car battery for solar. It is not built for daily deep cycling and will die early.

Choosing well and staying safe

Pick a pure sine wave inverter for TVs, laptops and fridges, because cheap modified-sine units can damage electronics. Ask for the warranty on the panel (often 10 to 25 years), battery and inverter, and keep your receipt and M-Pesa message. For roof mounting or anything bigger than a plug-and-play kit, hire a solar technician licensed by EPRA, and keep batteries in a ventilated place away from children.

Frequently asked questions

What size solar system do I need for a house in Kenya?

Add up your daily watt-hours. A home with LED lights, a TV, phone chargers and Wi-Fi uses roughly 700 to 800 Wh a day, which a 250 to 300 W panel, a 12 V 150 Ah battery and a 500 W pure sine wave inverter can cover.

Can solar run a fridge in Kenya?

Yes, but a fridge runs all day, so plan for around 1 kWh a day and size the whole system up.

Do I need a licensed installer?

For anything beyond a plug-and-play kit, use a solar technician licensed by EPRA.
