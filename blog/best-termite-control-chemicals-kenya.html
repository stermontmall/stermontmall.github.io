Best Termite Control Chemicals in Kenya: Compare Ingredients & Uses

Updated 17 November 2026 · Stermont Arcade buying guide

Quick answer: Choose a termiticide by the exact registered product label and treatment site, not by active ingredient name alone. Imidacloprid has verified Kenyan termite-use entries for products including Premise 200 SC and Mchwatox 350 SC. Other actives, including fipronil, chlorpyrifos and bifenthrin, must be checked product by product against the current PCPB register and label.

Termites (mchwa) can damage foundations, door frames, roof timber, fence posts and stored wood. The right treatment depends on whether the job involves pre-construction soil, an existing building, timber protection, a localised outdoor problem or a monitoring programme. A chemical suitable for one of these tasks is not automatically suitable for the others.

What does soil persistence actually mean?

Soil persistence is not a fixed number that can be copied from one product to another. Soil texture, organic matter, pH, moisture, temperature, microbial activity, formulation and the treatment method all influence how a chemical behaves. A product that binds strongly to soil may remain near the treated zone, but that does not prove a treatment barrier is complete or that a building is protected for a guaranteed period. Avoid marketing claims such as “lasts for ten years” unless the exact product label and warranty terms support them.

For a fair comparison, request the current product label and safety data sheet. Check the registered target pest, permitted treatment site, application method, concentration, personal protective equipment, re-entry directions, environmental precautions and any stated retreatment conditions. Do not publish or use a generic dilution rate for an ingredient family.

Which product suits which situation?

Pre-construction soil treatment

Specify a product whose Kenyan label explicitly covers the intended pre-construction building treatment. The builder or contractor should document the product, batch, treated area and application record. Do not use a wood preservative or a general crop insecticide as a substitute.

Existing buildings and post-construction work

Existing structures may require inspection of slab edges, service penetrations, expansion joints, damp areas and visible termite routes. The selected product and method must match the label and the building construction. Avoid drilling or injecting near unknown services.

Timber and fence posts

Use a timber or wood-treatment product only where its label specifically authorises that timber, site and method. The Mchwatox 350 SC entry on PCPB's public-health list describes it as a wood preservative for subterranean termites; verify the current label for the precise directions.

Monitoring and bait systems

Monitoring stations are not the same as an active pesticide treatment. For example, an in-ground monitoring station can provide an inspection point, while a bait system requires its own compatible bait component and label. Confirm what is included in the pack before buying.

Important Kenyan regulatory checks

Use the PCPB public-health registered products list and the PCPB registered-products portal to check the product name, registration number, active ingredient and authorised use. The FAO pesticide registration toolkit explains that Kenya's authorised labels are published through PCPB resources. The supplied pack label remains essential: never infer an approved use from the active ingredient alone.

For building infestations, request a site-specific assessment, written scope and follow-up plan. A treatment is not a substitute for fixing persistent leaks, poor drainage, timber-to-soil contact or other conditions that attract termites.

Frequently asked questions

Which active ingredient is best for termites in Kenya?

There is no universal best ingredient. Choose only a product whose current PCPB registration and label cover the termite species, treatment site and application method. Imidacloprid products such as Premise 200 SC and Mchwatox 350 SC have specific listed termite uses; do not transfer those uses to another formulation.

Is imidacloprid suitable for soil treatment?

Some imidacloprid products are registered for specific termite uses, but the permitted site and method differ by product. Check the exact PCPB-approved label for the pack you have.

Can I use chlorpyrifos or bifenthrin for building termites?

Only if the exact product is currently registered in Kenya and its label specifically authorises the intended termite treatment site. Do not assume that a crop insecticide or another brand's label covers building soil or timber.

How long does a termiticide remain in soil?

There is no single duration that applies to every formulation or site. Soil texture, organic matter, moisture, temperature, microbial activity and the labelled application method all affect persistence.
