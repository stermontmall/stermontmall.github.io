Termite control at home in Kenya: signs, prevention and options

Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Look for mud tubes, hollow-sounding wood and discarded wings, then reduce moisture and keep timber off the soil. Small, local problems may be handled with a registered product used exactly as the label directs, but soil treatment, mounds and structural damage call for a licensed operator.

Termites can quietly damage roofs, doors, cupboards and wooden furniture long before you notice anything on the surface. In Kenya they are a common problem in both new and old buildings. This guide explains the signs, simple prevention and the difference between products you can use yourself and treatments that need a licensed operator. What are the signs of termites in a house?

Termites hide, so you often see the effects before the insects. Check door frames, skirting boards, roof timbers, cupboards and the base of walls, especially in damp or dark corners.

  • Thin mud tubes running up walls or foundations
  • Wood that sounds hollow when you tap it
  • Discarded wings near windows, lights or door frames
  • Sagging floors, ceilings or bubbling paint
  • Fine powdery debris below damaged timber

Why are termites so hard to remove?

A termite colony can live underground or inside a mound, and the part you see is only a small share of it. The workers you notice in the house are being fed and replaced by a colony you cannot reach with a simple spray.

That is why treating only the visible damage rarely ends the problem. The treatment has to reach the colony, or create a barrier that stops termites travelling between soil and timber.

How can I prevent termites at home?

Termites need moisture and food, which in a house usually means wood and damp. Fixing leaks, improving drainage and keeping ventilation open make buildings less attractive to them.

Keep wooden items off bare soil, clear old timber, tree stumps and cardboard from around the building, and inspect door frames, roof timbers and the base of walls every few months. Catching the signs early makes any treatment simpler and less costly.

Can I treat termites myself or do I need a professional?

Some products are sold for small, localised problems, such as a piece of furniture or a short run of timber. If you use one, buy a product registered for the purpose, read the label carefully and follow it exactly. Never mix or dilute a product in a way the label does not describe.

Soil treatment around foundations, treating mounds and dealing with damage inside walls are normally done by licensed operators with the right equipment and training. Ask a qualified professional if you are unsure which situation you are in.

When should I call a licensed operator?

Call one if you see damage to structural timber, mounds close to the building, repeated returns after treatment, or if you are building a new house and want the ground treated before construction. A professional can inspect, explain what is safe for your household and advise on follow-up.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if termites are still active?

Fresh mud tubes, new piles of debris or live insects when you break open a tube suggest activity. Old, dry, crumbling tubes may be abandoned. If you are not sure, a licensed operator can inspect and tell you.

Can termites damage a house in a short time?

Damage is usually gradual, but it can go unnoticed for a long time because it happens inside timber and behind walls. Regular inspection is the best way to catch it early, before repairs become large.

Do termites only attack old buildings?

No. New buildings can be affected too, especially if timber touches the soil or the ground was not treated before construction. Good drainage and regular checks matter in every building, new or old.

Is a spray enough to get rid of termites?

A surface spray usually does not reach the colony. Effective control normally means treating the soil, the colony or the entry points, which is why professional advice is recommended for anything beyond a small, local problem.

What should I do before treating termites?

Find where the termites are active, fix leaks and damp, and read the product label closely. Keep children, pets and food away from treated areas as the label directs. For larger jobs, ask a licensed operator.

Termites reward early action. Check your building regularly, keep it dry and clear of old timber, and bring in a licensed operator when the problem is bigger than a single piece of wood.
