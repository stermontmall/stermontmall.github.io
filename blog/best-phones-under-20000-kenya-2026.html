Which features matter most at this budget?

RAM: look for physical memory, not just an expanded figure

Budget phones often advertise memory expansion. A phone described as 4GB plus virtual or extended RAM still has 4GB of physical RAM; the extra portion uses internal storage. That can help keep some apps available, but it is not the same as buying a phone with 8GB of physical memory. Read the configuration carefully.

Storage: 128GB is more comfortable for many users

Photos, WhatsApp media, offline videos and app updates all use space. If you keep a phone for several years, 128GB gives more breathing room than 64GB. Check whether the particular model supports a microSD card and whether it uses a dedicated slot or shares space with a second SIM.

Battery and charging

A larger battery can reduce how often you need to recharge, but battery capacity alone does not predict exact screen-on time. Screen brightness, mobile signal, gaming, background apps and battery age all matter. Also check the charging wattage and whether the correct adapter is included in the box.

Display and camera

A 90Hz or 120Hz refresh rate can make scrolling feel smoother, but it does not automatically mean the screen has higher resolution or better brightness. For cameras, look beyond the megapixel number: stabilisation, sensor quality, focus and image processing affect the result. If you take many night photos, look for sample images from the exact model.

How to choose between these five phones

If you mainly use calls, M-Pesa, WhatsApp and light browsing, a lower-priced 4GB/64GB model may be enough. If you save lots of videos or install many apps, consider the Redmi A7 Pro or itel Power 80 with 128GB storage. If battery life is your priority, compare the stated 6,000mAh and 7,000mAh capacities, while remembering that real runtime depends on usage. If you want a high-refresh screen and a 50MP main camera, review the TECNO Spark 50 listing and confirm the exact regional specification.

Checks to make before paying in Kenya

  • Confirm the phone is new or refurbished and get the condition in writing.
  • Ask who provides the warranty, where repairs are handled and how long cover lasts.
  • Check the exact RAM, storage, model number, SIM arrangement and network bands.
  • Ask what is included: charger, cable, case, screen protector and warranty card.
  • Compare the final delivered price, not only the product price.
  • When the phone arrives, dial *#06# and match the IMEI with the box and receipt.

Frequently asked questions

Which phone is best under KSh 20,000 in Kenya?

There is no single best choice. Match the configuration to your priorities: storage, battery, display, camera, network support and warranty.

Is 4GB RAM enough for a budget phone?

4GB physical RAM can handle everyday calls, messaging, browsing and light apps. Heavy multitasking may benefit from more physical memory.

Should I choose 64GB or 128GB storage?

128GB provides more room for apps, photos and downloads. 64GB may suit light use, especially if the phone supports expandable storage.
