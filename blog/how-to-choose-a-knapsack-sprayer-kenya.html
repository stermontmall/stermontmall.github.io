How to choose a knapsack sprayer for your farm in Kenya

Updated 1 November 2026 · Buying guide

Quick answer: Choose a sprayer by the area you need to cover, the tank size you can comfortably carry, the operating method, and the availability of replacement parts. Check that the sprayer is compatible with the product label and follow both the label and the equipment manual.

A sprayer is a practical farm purchase, but the biggest tank or the most expensive machine is not automatically the right choice. A small garden, a greenhouse and a larger field have different needs. Before ordering, think about the size of the job, the distance you will walk, where you will refill and how you will clean and store the equipment.

What is the difference between a hand sprayer and a knapsack sprayer?

A hand-pressure sprayer is a small unit for targeted jobs and limited areas. A knapsack sprayer is carried on the back and has a larger tank, which can reduce the number of refills when covering a wider area. The trade-off is weight: liquid adds weight, so a bigger tank can become tiring when carried for long periods.

How do I choose the right tank capacity?

Start with the size of the area you usually treat and the amount of walking involved. A smaller tank is easier to carry and handle, but may require more refills. A larger tank can reduce refill stops, but it is heavier when full. Do not choose capacity from the farm size alone: consider slopes, narrow paths, distance to clean water and whether you can safely refill near the work area.

For a small garden or a few plants, a hand sprayer may be enough. For larger plots, compare the 16-litre and 20-litre knapsack options and consider who will carry the equipment. If more than one person will use it, check whether the harness and controls can be adjusted comfortably.

Manual, battery or motorised: what should I compare?

Do not assume the operating method from a product photograph. Read the listing and manual to confirm whether the model is manual, battery-powered or motorised. For powered equipment, check the battery or fuel requirements, charging arrangements, maintenance needs and availability of replacement parts. For manual models, inspect the pump mechanism, seals, hose connections and trigger.

Also check the nozzle and spray pattern requirements for your intended work. A nozzle that is damaged, blocked or unsuitable can affect application. Replace worn parts with compatible components recommended for that sprayer rather than forcing a fitting that does not match.

What should I inspect before buying?

  • Tank and cap: check the stated capacity, material and whether the cap closes securely.
  • Harness and comfort: look for adjustable straps and a shape that sits securely when the tank is filled to its working level.
  • Hose and trigger: check the connections, trigger action and whether replacement parts are available.
  • Nozzle: confirm which nozzle is supplied and whether the equipment supports the nozzle you need.
  • Cleaning and storage: ask for the manual and follow its instructions for cleaning, drying and storage.
  • Service: ask whether seals, filters, hoses and other wear parts can be sourced locally.

If a listing does not state a technical detail, ask the seller to confirm it before payment. Do not rely on assumptions based on a similar-looking sprayer.

Can I use the same sprayer for every farm chemical?

No. Compatibility depends on the product label and the sprayer manufacturer's instructions. Some products specify particular application equipment or exclude certain methods. Read the label before buying and make sure the equipment is permitted for that product. Never improvise a mixing rate, use a chemical in a way the label does not allow or mix leftover products together.

Wear the protective equipment specified by the label. Keep children, pets, food and animal feed away from the mixing and application area. Follow the label for cleaning, disposal, re-entry and any waiting period before harvesting. If you are unsure about a crop problem or the correct product, ask a qualified agricultural extension officer.

How should I clean and store a sprayer?

Follow the chemical label and sprayer manual for the correct cleaning procedure. Do not pour rinse water into drains, streams, wells or places where people and animals may be exposed. Inspect the tank, hose, seals and nozzle for leaks or damage, and store the equipment in a secure place away from children, food and animal feed. Keep the manual so the next user can check the manufacturer's instructions.

Frequently asked questions

What size knapsack sprayer should I buy?

Choose capacity based on the area you cover, how often you can refill and the weight you can comfortably carry. A larger tank reduces refill trips but is heavier when full.

Is a 2-litre hand sprayer the same as a knapsack sprayer?

No. A small hand-pressure sprayer is intended for smaller, targeted jobs. A knapsack sprayer is carried on the back and is generally selected for larger areas.

Can I use any chemical in a sprayer?

No. Check that the product label permits the equipment and application method, and follow the label and equipment manual. Never guess mixing rates or combine products.

How do I maintain a sprayer?

After use, follow the product label and sprayer manual for safe cleaning, rinsing, inspection and storage. Check seals, hose connections and nozzle condition before the next job.
