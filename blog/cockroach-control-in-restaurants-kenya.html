Cockroach control in restaurants and kitchens in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Control cockroaches in a restaurant by combining strict cleaning, sealed gaps, dry drains, closed food storage and regular monitoring with a treatment suited to food premises. Use only products labelled for that use, follow the label and bring in a licensed operator for ongoing control.

Cockroaches thrive in warm, damp kitchens with plenty of food crumbs, which describes many restaurants. They contaminate food and surfaces, damage your reputation and can cost you customers or trigger action from health inspectors. This guide explains the daily habits, monitoring and treatment choices that keep a restaurant or commercial kitchen under control.

Why are cockroaches such a problem in food businesses?

Cockroaches carry germs on their bodies and legs and leave droppings and shed skins in food areas. They hide in cracks, behind equipment and in drains during the day and come out at night to feed. A small number seen in daylight often means a larger hidden population.

Restaurants provide everything they need: warmth from cookers and fridges, moisture from sinks and spills, and food from crumbs, grease and waste. Cardboard boxes and deliveries can also bring them in from outside.

What daily habits keep cockroaches down?

Cleaning is the foundation of control, because no product works well in a dirty kitchen. At closing time, clear food scraps, wipe surfaces, clean grease from cookers and floors and mop to remove spills. Empty bins, wash them and keep lids closed. Do not leave dirty dishes or wet cloths overnight.

  • Store food, flour, rice and sugar in closed containers
  • Keep floors, drains and under-equipment areas clean and dry
  • Remove cardboard and paper bags quickly, because they hide eggs
  • Repair leaking taps and pipes
  • Check deliveries before bringing them into the kitchen

How do I find where the cockroaches are coming from?

Inspect at night with a torch, when cockroaches are most active. Check behind fridges and cookers, under sinks, in floor drains, around dishwashers and in wall or floor cracks near warm equipment. Dark pepper-like specks, egg cases and a musty smell show where they live.

Sticky monitoring traps placed along walls and under equipment help you see how many are present and where. Replace them regularly and record what you catch, so you can tell whether control is working. Seal cracks, gaps around pipes and holes in walls with suitable filler or sealant.

Which treatments suit a kitchen?

Gel baits, residual sprays and powders are the common choices, and each suits different places. Gels are applied in small amounts in cracks and hidden spots, which keeps product away from open surfaces. Sprays treat larger areas but need careful use around food. When should I call a licensed operator?

Call a licensed operator when you see cockroaches regularly, when they keep returning, or when your business handles large amounts of food. A professional can identify the species, find hiding places, treat safely in food areas and set up a regular visit schedule. Keep records of inspections, treatments and dates, as customers and inspectors may ask to see them.

Frequently asked questions

Why do cockroaches keep coming back to my kitchen?

Cockroaches return when food, water and hiding places remain, or when they enter again from drains, neighbouring units or deliveries. Treatment removes the ones you have now, while cleaning, sealing gaps and fixing leaks stops new ones settling.

Is it safe to spray insecticide in a kitchen?

Only use products whose label allows use in food premises, and follow the directions exactly. Cover or remove food and utensils, avoid food-contact surfaces and ventilate afterwards. For commercial kitchens, a licensed operator is the safest choice.

How can I tell where cockroaches are hiding?

Check at night with a torch, since cockroaches are active in the dark. Look behind fridges and cookers, under sinks, in drains and in cracks near warm equipment. Droppings that look like pepper or coffee grounds and a musty smell also show where they live.

How often should a restaurant be inspected for pests?

Do a quick daily check of key areas, a thorough weekly inspection and keep a written log. Many food businesses also arrange regular visits from a licensed operator, particularly if they have had problems before.

Should I rotate between different treatments?

Rotation can help avoid pests becoming used to one product, and a licensed operator can plan it for you. If you do your own treatment, follow the label, avoid mixing products and ask for advice before changing methods.
