Is pest control spray safe for babies and pets? What to check

Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: No spray should be assumed safe for babies or pets. Read the label for where and how the product may be used, follow its re-entry and ventilation directions, cover or remove food, bowls and toys, and ask a qualified professional or your vet if you have any doubt.

It is a fair worry. You want the cockroaches or mosquitoes gone, but you also want your baby crawling on that floor and your dog sleeping in that room. The answer is not a simple yes or no. It comes down to the product, how you use it and how carefully you prepare and clean up.

Can I use a pest control spray in a home with babies?

Many products can be used in homes, but only in the places and ways the label allows. Babies spend time on the floor and put their hands and objects in their mouths, so they are more exposed than adults. Where possible, choose the least intrusive approach first: cleaning, sealing gaps and trapping often cut the need for sprays. If you do spray, keep babies and pets out of the area during and after treatment as the label directs.

What should I look for on the label?

The label is your most reliable guide. Look for the PCPB registration, the target pests, the approved places of use, safety and first aid instructions, and any waiting time before people and pets can return. Our guide on how to read an insecticide label explains each part. If a label does not mention homes, children or pets, do not guess: ask a professional.

How do I prepare the room before spraying?

Remove or cover food, utensils, pet bowls, toys, baby items and bedding. Take pets, birds and fish tanks out of the room, and open windows for ventilation. Apply the product only where the label says, never on surfaces that babies or pets touch or lick, and keep the container out of reach. Wear any protective gear the label recommends.

When can people and pets go back in?

Follow the re-entry time on the label, then ventilate the room well. Wipe surfaces that children and pets touch, wash floors where they crawl or lie, and launder soft items that may have been exposed. Keep pets and babies away until surfaces are dry and any smell has cleared. If the label gives no waiting time, ask a professional before you let them back in.

What if someone is exposed by accident?

Move the person or animal to fresh air, wash skin with soap and water, rinse eyes with clean water, and contact a doctor, clinic or vet straight away with the product label in hand. Frequently asked questions

Is there a spray that is completely safe for babies?

No product should be assumed completely safe. Pest control products are made to kill living things, so risk depends on the product, how it is used and who is exposed. Follow the label closely and keep babies and pets out of treated areas as it directs.

How long should I stay out of a room after spraying?

Follow the re-entry or waiting time on the product label, which varies by product. Ventilate the room well before people and pets return, and keep babies and pets out until surfaces are dry and any smell has cleared, or as the label directs.

Do I need to cover pet food, bowls and toys?

Yes, it is wise to remove or cover food, bowls, feeding items, toys and baby items before treating, and to wash anything that may have been exposed. Fish tanks and bird cages need special care, so remove them from the room or ask a vet for advice.

What should I do if a child or pet is exposed by accident?

Move them to fresh air, wash skin with soap and water, rinse eyes with clean water, and contact a doctor, clinic or vet straight away with the product label to hand. Do not wait for symptoms to appear before asking for help.

When is a professional a better choice?

If you have a newborn, a sick person, a pregnant family member or pets with health problems at home, or if the infestation is large, ask a licensed operator for advice. They can recommend a plan and tell you how long to stay away.

Careful reading, preparation and cleaning make the biggest difference to safety. When in doubt, ask before you spray.
