Poultry feed premix, lysine and feed phosphate in Kenya: what each one does

Updated 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: A premix supplies vitamins and trace minerals, lysine is an amino acid that poultry and pigs cannot make themselves, and feed phosphate supplies phosphorus and calcium for bones and eggshells. Use the type made for your animals, follow the label or your nutritionist's formula, and store them dry and off the floor.

Feed is usually the biggest cost in a poultry or pig business, so it pays to understand what you are buying. Farmers who mix their own rations often hear three names: premix, lysine and feed phosphate. This guide explains what each one is for in plain words, and what to check before you order.

What is a premix and why is it used?

A premix is a blend of vitamins and trace minerals that is mixed into a ration in small amounts. Maize, soya and other feed raw materials do not supply everything a bird needs, and a premix fills those gaps. Premixes are made for a particular type of animal, so a layer premix is meant for layer rations. Our layer premix for poultry feed (25 kg) is sold for mixing layer rations. Always use the mixing rate printed on the label, because too little or too much can both cause problems.

What does lysine do in poultry and pig feed?

Proteins are built from amino acids, and lysine is one that poultry and pigs cannot produce themselves, so it has to come from the feed. Rations based mainly on cereals such as maize can be short of lysine, so feed formulators add it to balance the ration. You can see our lysine feed amino acid (25 kg) for poultry and pig rations. As with premix, the amount to use depends on the ration and the age of the animals, so follow the inclusion rate on the label or your nutritionist's formula.

Why do animals need feed phosphate?

Phosphorus and calcium are needed for bones and, in layers, for good eggshells. A feed phosphate supplies both minerals in the ration. We stock Kynofos 21 Elite feed phosphate (25 kg) for poultry, pig and livestock rations. Check the label for the mineral content and the recommended inclusion rate.

What are feed supplements?

Feed supplements such as Gut Bio (100 g) are added to rations in small amounts. Read the label to see what the product is for and how much to use, and ask your vet if you are unsure whether your animals need it.

What should I check before I buy feed ingredients?

Check that the pack is sealed and that the label shows the product name, contents, batch number and expiry date. Choose the product made for your animal type, whether layers, broilers or pigs. Buy a quantity you can use before the expiry date. If you are not sure about the right ration, ask a livestock officer, a vet or a feed nutritionist, because the correct amounts depend on the animal, its age and the other ingredients you use.

How should I store feed ingredients?

Keep bags dry, closed and off the floor, on pallets or racks, in a cool, ventilated store. Damp feed grows mould, and open bags attract rats and insects. If rodents are a problem in your store, read our guide to rat and mouse control in Kenya. Keep all feed ingredients and chemicals away from children and pets.
