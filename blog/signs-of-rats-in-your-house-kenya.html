Signs of rats in your house or shop in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: The clearest signs of rats are dark capsule-shaped droppings, gnaw marks on wood, packaging or wiring, greasy smear marks along walls, scratching noises at night and shredded nesting material. Find where they enter and where they travel before you choose traps, bait or a professional.

Rats are good at staying out of sight, which is why most people notice the damage before they ever see the animal. Whether you run a home, a shop or a store, learning to read the signs lets you act early, protect food and avoid costly damage to wiring and stock.

What are the most common signs of rats?

Droppings are the easiest sign to spot. Rat droppings are dark, capsule-shaped pellets, usually found along walls, behind appliances and near food. Gnaw marks on wood, plastic, sacks and cables are another giveaway, because rat teeth never stop growing and they gnaw to keep them worn down.

  • Droppings near food, bins and along walls
  • Gnaw marks on doors, packaging, pipes and wiring
  • Greasy smear marks where rats rub along regular routes
  • Scratching, squeaking or running sounds in ceilings and walls at night
  • Nests of shredded paper, cloth or sacking in quiet corners

How do I know rats are active now, not long ago?

Fresh droppings are dark and soft, while old ones are dry, grey and crumble easily. To test for current activity, clean an area, sprinkle a thin line of flour or place a sheet of paper, and check it the next day for footprints and tail marks. New gnaw marks look pale and clean, while old ones are darker.

Where are rats getting in?

Rats can squeeze through surprisingly small gaps. Check around pipes and cables that pass through walls, under doors, around roof eaves and ceiling edges, and in drains and broken air vents. Outside, look for burrows along foundations, in compost heaps and under stored items.

Why are rats in my building at all?

Rats come for food, water and shelter. Open bins, spilt grain, pet food left out overnight, leaking taps and cluttered storage give them everything they need. Shops and stores that keep goods on the floor offer hiding places and easy meals, which is why storage habits matter as much as traps.

Start by blocking entry points and removing food and water sources. Frequently asked questions

What do rat droppings look like?

Rat droppings are dark, capsule-shaped pellets, usually larger than mouse droppings. Fresh ones are dark and soft, while old ones are dry and grey. Wear gloves and a mask when cleaning them up, and wash your hands afterwards.

Why do I hear noises in the ceiling at night?

Scratching, scurrying or gnawing in the ceiling or walls, especially at night, is a common sign of rats because they are most active in the dark. Birds, bats or other animals can also cause noises, so look for droppings and gnaw marks to confirm.

Can rats make people ill?

Rats can contaminate food and surfaces with droppings and urine and may carry germs that cause illness. That is why food stores need special care. Clean affected areas carefully, throw away contaminated food and see a doctor if someone becomes unwell.

Should I put out bait as soon as I suspect rats?

Confirm the signs first, work out where rats are entering, and decide which option suits your premises, especially if children or pets are around. If you use bait or traps, follow the label and place them out of reach of non-target animals.

How do I stop rats coming back?

Seal entry holes with durable materials, store food in sealed containers off the floor, manage waste properly and keep vegetation and clutter away from walls. Keep checking for fresh droppings or gnawing so a new problem is caught early.

Spotting rat signs early gives you more choices and lowers the damage. A few minutes of careful checking each week is a small price for a clean, safe building.
