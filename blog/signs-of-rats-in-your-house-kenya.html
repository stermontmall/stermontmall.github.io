Signs of rats in your house or shop in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Common signs of rats are dark droppings along walls, gnaw marks on packaging, wires or wood, scratching noises at night, greasy rub marks along walls and shredded nesting material. Finding fresh droppings and chewed food usually confirms that rats are active nearby.

Rats are careful animals that come out mostly at night, so many homes and shops have them for weeks before anyone sees one. The good news is that rats leave clear evidence. This guide explains the signs to look for, where to look and how to tell whether the rats are still active, so you can act before they damage stock, wiring or food.

What are the most common signs of rats?

Droppings are usually the first clue. Rat droppings are dark, about the size of a large grain of rice, and are found in groups along walls, behind appliances and near food. Gnaw marks are the next sign: rats chew packaging, sacks, soap, wood, plastic pipes and electric cables.

You may also hear scratching, scurrying or squeaking in the ceiling, walls or under the floor at night. Rats use the same routes again and again, so they leave greasy, dark rub marks along the bottom of walls and around holes. Look too for nests made of shredded paper, cloth or sacking in quiet, hidden corners.

  • Dark droppings along walls and near food
  • Gnaw marks on food packs, sacks, wood and cables
  • Scratching or squeaking at night
  • Greasy rub marks along walls and around holes
  • Shredded nesting material in hidden corners
  • Spilled or half-eaten food

Where do rats usually hide and travel?

Rats prefer to run along walls and keep to dark, sheltered paths. In a house, check the kitchen, store room, behind the fridge and cooker, under sinks, in the ceiling and around pipes that pass through walls. In a shop, look in stockrooms, behind shelves, under counters and around the back door and drains.

Outside, check for burrows near foundations, under sheds, in piles of rubbish or timber and along fences. Rats often move between outdoor shelter and the building along the same path every night.

How can I tell if the rats are still active?

Fresh droppings look dark and slightly moist, while old droppings are dry, grey and crumble easily. Clear away the droppings you find, then check the same spots again the next day. New droppings mean rats are still around.

You can also sprinkle a thin layer of flour or talc on the floor along a wall at night, away from children and food. In the morning, footprints and tail marks show where rats are travelling. Keep checking for several days, because rats can be cautious about anything new in their path.

How do rats get into a house or shop?

Rats can squeeze through surprisingly small gaps, often only about the width of a thumb. Common entry points are gaps around pipes and cables, damaged air bricks, holes under doors, broken drain covers, loose roof sheets and spaces where the roof meets the wall. Open doors left unattended during deliveries are another route in busy shops.

Walk around the building and look for gaps, then seal them with strong materials such as cement, metal mesh or metal plates, because rats can chew through wood, plastic and soft sealants.

What should I do once I confirm rats?

First protect your health. Rats can carry germs, so wear gloves when you clean droppings, dampen them before wiping instead of sweeping them dry, and wash your hands afterwards. Move food into closed containers and remove easy sources of water.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell rat droppings from other droppings?

Rat droppings are dark, about the size of a large grain of rice, and found in groups along walls and near food. Mouse droppings are much smaller. Fresh droppings look moist and dark, while old ones are dry, grey and crumbly.

Why do I hear noises in the ceiling at night?

Scratching, scurrying or squeaking in the ceiling or walls at night often points to rats, because they are most active in the dark. Birds, bats and other animals can also cause noises, so look for droppings and gnaw marks to confirm.

Is it safe to clean up rat droppings?

Wear gloves and a mask, dampen the droppings first so dust does not rise, wipe them up with a disinfectant solution and throw the waste in a sealed bag. Never sweep dry droppings, and wash your hands well afterwards.

Can rats be in the house without being seen?

Yes. Rats are mostly active at night and avoid people, so many households only notice the signs, such as droppings, chewed items and noises. Seeing a rat in daylight often suggests a larger problem nearby.

When should I get professional help?

Ask a licensed operator if signs are widespread, if you run a food business, if rats keep returning after you have proofed and treated, or if you cannot find where they enter. Large premises usually need a planned programme.
