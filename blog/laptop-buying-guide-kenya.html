Laptop buying guide for Kenya: RAM, SSD and what to check

Updated 15 October 2026 · 4 min read

The best laptop is the one that fits what you do with it. A student, an office worker and a video editor need different things. Here is how to compare.

1. Start with how you will use it

For typing, browsing, online classes and spreadsheets, a mid-range laptop is enough. For graphic design, video editing or gaming you need a stronger processor, more memory and often a dedicated graphics card, and it will cost more.

2. Processor

Common families are Intel Core (i3, i5, i7) and AMD Ryzen (3, 5, 7). A higher number generally means more power. For everyday work, an i3 or Ryzen 3 can do the job, an i5 or Ryzen 5 is a good all-rounder, and i7 or Ryzen 7 is for heavy work. Newer generations are usually faster and more efficient than older ones with the same label.

3. Memory (RAM)

8 GB is a sensible starting point for most people today. 4 GB can feel slow with several browser tabs open. Choose 16 GB if you edit video, run several programs at once or want the laptop to last longer.

4. Storage: choose an SSD

A solid state drive (SSD) starts up and opens programs much faster than an old-style hard disk (HDD). A 256 GB SSD is fine for basic use, and 512 GB gives more room for files and programs. Check how much storage you will really need before paying for more.

5. Screen, keyboard and battery

A 14 to 15.6 inch screen is the common range. Full HD (1920 by 1080) is sharper than basic HD. If you type a lot, check the keyboard layout and whether it has a number pad. Battery life matters if you move around or face power cuts, so look at the stated battery hours.

6. Ports and extras

Check for USB ports, HDMI for a projector or TV, and Wi-Fi. Ask whether Windows is installed and activated, and confirm what is in the box.
