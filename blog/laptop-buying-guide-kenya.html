Laptop buying guide for Kenya

Updated 29 October 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: get at least 8 GB RAM and a 256 GB SSD, a battery that lasts 6 hours or more, and a written warranty. Skip 4 GB, hard-drive-only machines.

Whether it is for campus, an office job or your small business, a laptop is a big purchase. Here is how to choose well when buying in Kenya.

An SSD makes a laptop feel far faster than an old hard drive. Look for a Full HD screen if you work long hours, and a keyboard that feels comfortable, since you will type on it every day.

New or ex-UK?

Ex-UK and refurbished laptops are popular in Kenya because they cost less. They can be good value if you check them carefully: battery health and charging, screen for dead pixels and scratches, keyboard keys, ports, and whether the Wi-Fi and webcam work. Ask for a written warranty, even a short one. If you cannot inspect the laptop in person, a new laptop with a local warranty is the safer choice.

Battery and power

With blackouts a real possibility, a battery that lasts 6 hours or more is valuable. Kenya uses 240 V power with UK-style three-pin sockets, so make sure the charger plug fits. Use a surge-protected extension to protect your charger and laptop from voltage swings.

Before you pay

Check the warranty length and who services it, the exact model number and specs, and what comes in the box. Keep your receipt and M-Pesa confirmation. We accept returns of faulty items within 48 hours, see our returns policy. Back up your files and set a password on day one.

Frequently asked questions

How much RAM and storage do I need in a laptop?

8 GB of RAM and a 256 GB or larger SSD is a good minimum for school and office work.

Is an ex-UK laptop a good buy in Kenya?

It can be good value, but check the battery, screen and keyboard and ask for a written warranty. A new laptop with a local warranty is safer if you cannot inspect it.

Which laptop is best for students in Kenya?

A light laptop with 8 GB RAM, an SSD and a battery that lasts at least 6 hours.
