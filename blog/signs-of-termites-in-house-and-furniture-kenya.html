Signs of Termites in a House or Furniture in Kenya

Updated 9 November 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: Key signs of termites in a house include mud tubes along walls or foundations, discarded swarmer wings near windows, hollow-sounding wooden furniture or doorframes, blistering paint, and sagging floorboards. Detecting termite damage early helps protect timber structures before severe structural weakness occurs.

Termites can quietly enter residential homes and commercial buildings, feeding on wooden fixtures, built-in furniture, and roof trusses without immediate detection. For property owners, recognizing the early signs of termites Kenya structures display is essential for safeguarding your timber assets and building foundations. Because termites consume wood from the inside out, visible damage often appears only after an infestation is already established.

What are the early signs of termites in a house?

One of the most noticeable indications of termites in house structures is the presence of mud shelter tubes. Subterranean termites require humidity to survive and construct these thin, pencil-sized tunnels out of soil, wood particles, and saliva. These mud lines typically travel vertically along concrete foundations, stone walls, exterior plaster, and pillar bases, allowing termites to move safely between underground nests and wood sources inside your home.

Another prominent indicator is finding discarded transparent wings along window sills, door thresholds, or near light fixtures. During swarming periods, winged reproductive termites leave mature colonies to mate and form new nests. Shortly after landing, they drop their wings in small clusters. Finding shed wings indoors suggests that swarmers have entered your living space or that an active colony exists nearby. How do you identify termite damage in timber and furniture?

Detecting internal termite damage in wooden items requires both visual checks and physical testing. Because termites hollow out timber while leaving the surface veneer or paint intact, damaged wood often looks completely normal from the outside. Tapping door frames, skirting boards, wardrobes, or structural beams with a screwdriver handle produces a hollow sound when internal galleries have been eaten away.

In advanced cases, the integrity of structural timber declines significantly. Thin wood surfaces may crumble under light pressure, revealing hollowed channels filled with mud or dark debris. You may also notice blistering paint or wood veneer that resembles water damage. When termites feed inside window sills and door jambs, the moisture they generate causes timber to swell, making doors and windows stick or jam during operation.

Where should you check for termites around your home?

A thorough property check should cover both interior rooms and exterior surroundings. Indoors, inspect along baseboards, hardwood flooring, staircase supports, built-in kitchen cabinets, and roof trusses. Kitchens, bathrooms, and laundry areas are particularly vulnerable because moisture from plumbing creates favorable conditions for pest activity.

Outdoors, focus on areas where timber touches soil directly. Check fence posts, garden borders, stacked firewood, tree stumps, and exterior wall footings. Pay close attention to damp crawlspaces and drainage areas where mud tunnels remain hidden. Just as you would monitor structural entry points when looking for signs of rats in your house, keeping exterior walls free of damp wood and stacked timber helps reduce pest harborage.

When should you call a licensed pest control operator?

While minor non-structural timber repairs can be managed easily, widespread infestations or structural destruction require professional intervention. If mud tubes cover large sections of wall foundations, or if roof trusses and load-bearing support beams show hollow weakness, calling a licensed pest control operator is recommended. Trained professionals have the specialized equipment required for deep soil barriers and structural treatments around concrete slabs.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell if termites are inside my wooden furniture?

Tap the timber firmly with your knuckles or a tool handle. If it produces a hollow sound or breaks easily under mild pressure, termites may be feeding inside. You might also notice fine soil pressed into joints or blistered wood veneer.

Are discarded wings a definite sign of termites in the house?

Yes, shed transparent wings near windows, doors, or light fixtures indicate that reproductive termites swarmed inside or very close to the building. After flying, reproductive swarmers shed their wings to establish new colonies.

What is the difference between subterranean and drywood termite signs?

Subterranean termites build visible mud tubes along walls to travel safely from soil to timber. Drywood termites live entirely inside dry timber without soil contact, often leaving behind small piles of wood-colored pellets near infested items.

Can termite damage cause floors or ceilings to sag?

Yes, as termites consume structural wood inside support beams, joists, and floorboards, the load-bearing framework weakens. Over time, this leads to dipping floorboards, cracked plaster, or visibly sagging ceiling structures.

What should I do if I find termite mud tubes on my walls?

Avoid scraping or breaking all the tubes immediately, as disturbing them can cause termites to move into hidden areas. Inspect nearby timber, check product labels for guidance, and consult a qualified professional to determine appropriate management steps.

Identifying termite activity early is the best way to protect your house, furniture, and wooden structures from costly damage. Regular checks of wall foundations, wooden fittings, and outdoor perimeters help you catch infestations early.
