Signs of termites in a house or furniture in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: The main signs of termites are thin mud tubes on walls or foundations, wood that sounds hollow when tapped, discarded wings near windows and doors, blistered or sagging boards, and fine dust or small holes in timber. If you find several of these, ask a licensed operator to inspect.

Termites work quietly, often for months, before the damage becomes obvious. In Kenya they can affect roofs, doors, frames, cupboards and stored goods alike. Knowing what to look for helps you catch a problem while it is still manageable.

What are the first signs of termites?

The most recognisable sign is mud tubes: thin, earthen tunnels running up walls, foundations or timber. Termites build them to travel safely between the soil and their food. Other early signs include small piles of discarded wings, which appear after winged termites leave to start new colonies, and wood that looks fine on the surface but sounds papery inside.

  • Mud tubes on walls, pillars, frames and foundations
  • Discarded wings near windows, doors and lights
  • Hollow-sounding, soft or crumbling wood
  • Blistered paint, sagging floorboards or doors that stick
  • Fine dust, small holes or tunnels in timber

How do I check wood and furniture safely?

Walk around the building and inspect the base of walls, door and window frames, roof timbers and any wood touching the ground. Tap suspicious timber gently with a screwdriver handle and listen for a hollow sound. Check the backs and undersides of wardrobes, beds and shelves, where termites often start unnoticed. Use a torch and avoid prying so you do not break weakened wood.

Termites or something else? How can I tell?

Other wood-boring insects can also leave holes and fine dust, so confirm before you act. Mud tubes and discarded wings point strongly to termites, while neat round exit holes with fine powder often come from different wood borers. If you are unsure, take clear photos and ask a licensed pest control operator or a qualified professional to confirm what you are dealing with.

Where do termites usually hide in Kenyan homes?

They favour places that are dark, damp and close to soil: foundations, wooden door frames, skirting boards, roof timbers, cupboards against outside walls and stored cardboard or paper. Leaking pipes, poor drainage and timber left in contact with the ground make a building more attractive to them, so fixing moisture and keeping wood off the soil helps prevention.

What should I do next?

Do not ignore the signs, because damage spreads. Fix leaks, move timber and boxes away from walls, and remove dead wood and debris around the building. Frequently asked questions

What are flying termites and should I worry?

Flying termites are winged reproductives that leave a colony, often after rain, to start new ones. Seeing them near lights or windows, or finding discarded wings on sills, can mean a colony is close to or inside your building, so inspect the area carefully.

How can I tell if wood is hollow from termites?

Tap the wood with a screwdriver handle. Sound timber gives a solid knock, while wood eaten from the inside often sounds papery or hollow. Do this gently, since damaged boards can break, and look for mud tubes or blistered surfaces nearby.

Can termites be in furniture without me noticing?

Yes. Termites often eat wood from the inside and leave a thin surface layer, so damage can go unseen for a long time. Check the back and underside of furniture, and look for fine dust, small holes or soft spots, especially on pieces touching walls or floors.

Is it safe to treat termites myself?

Small, local problems in furniture may be handled with suitable products used strictly as the label says. Colonies in walls, floors or soil are harder to reach, so a licensed operator is usually the safer choice. Never guess at rates: follow the label.

The earlier termites are found, the smaller the repair bill. Make a habit of checking timber at the start of each rainy season.
