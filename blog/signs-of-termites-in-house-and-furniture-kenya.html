Signs of termites in a house or furniture in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: The main signs of termites are thin mud tubes on walls or foundations, wood that sounds hollow when tapped, discarded wings near windows and doors, small pellet-like droppings and paint that bubbles or sags. Check door frames, skirting boards, roof timbers and furniture touching walls first.

Termites eat wood, paper and other plant material from the inside, which is why damage is often serious by the time it becomes visible. Learning the early signs helps you act sooner and protect your house, furniture and stored goods. This guide explains what to look for, where to look and when it is time to call a licensed operator.

What are the first signs of termites?

The most recognisable sign is a mud tube: a thin, earthy tunnel running up a wall, pillar or foundation. Termites build these to travel from the soil to wood without being exposed to light and dry air. You may also find soft or hollow-sounding wood, paint that blisters or sags, and small piles of fine pellets near damaged timber.

After rains, winged termites sometimes swarm. If you find a pile of discarded wings near windows, doors or lights, a colony may be close by or even inside the building.

  • Thin mud tubes on walls, pillars or foundations
  • Wood that sounds hollow or feels soft
  • Discarded wings near windows and doors
  • Small pellet-like droppings below damaged wood
  • Paint or plaster that bubbles, cracks or sags

Where should I look first?

Check wood that touches the ground or sits in damp places. Door and window frames, skirting boards, wooden floors on the ground floor, roof timbers, fence posts and sheds are common targets. Look at the outer foundation of the house for mud tubes, especially near drains and taps.

Inside, check wardrobes, shelves and cupboards that stand against exterior walls, and look behind them. Also examine stored cardboard, books and paper, which termites eat. Use a torch and tap timber with the handle of a screwdriver to listen for hollow sounds.

How do I tell termite damage from other damage?

Termite damage usually runs along the grain of the wood and leaves thin outer layers intact while the inside is hollowed into tunnels, often packed with soil. Wood rot from damp is usually soft and crumbly and may smell musty. Tiny round exit holes in furniture are more often caused by wood-boring beetles than termites.

If you are not sure, take clear photos and a sample of the damaged wood and show them to a licensed pest control operator or an agricultural officer.

What can I do while waiting for professional help?

Reduce moisture first, because many termites depend on it. Fix leaking taps and roofs, clear blocked gutters and keep soil away from wooden frames. Move stored items, firewood and timber off the ground and away from walls. Do not remove large sections of mud tubes without advice, as termites will simply rebuild, but take photos for the inspector.

When should I call a licensed operator?

Call a licensed operator if you find mud tubes on walls, damage in roof timbers or door frames, or activity in a large building. Termite control in buildings often involves treating the soil or walls and is not a job for a quick spray. A professional can also tell you whether the colony is in the house or in the compound.

Frequently asked questions

What do flying termites mean?

Winged termites swarm from a mature colony, often after the first rains. Seeing them indoors, or finding piles of discarded wings near windows and doors, suggests a colony nearby, possibly in the building, so inspect the area carefully.

Can termites be inside walls without being seen?

Yes. Termites avoid light and feed inside wood, behind plaster and under floors, so damage can be advanced before it shows. Hollow-sounding timber, blistering paint and mud tubes are clues that termites are active out of sight.

Are termites the same as ants?

No. Termites have a thick, straight waist and straight antennae, and winged termites have wings of equal length. Ants have a narrow waist, bent antennae and unequal wings. The difference matters because treatment is different.

Does damp wood attract termites?

Moisture helps many termites survive, and damp or decaying timber is attractive to them. Fixing leaks, improving drainage and keeping timber off wet soil reduce the risk, although some termites can still attack dry wood.

When should I call a licensed operator?

Call a licensed operator if you find mud tubes on walls, damage to roof timbers or door frames, or activity in a large building. Termite control in structures often needs soil or wall treatment that should be done by trained professionals.
