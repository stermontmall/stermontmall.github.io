Poultry Feed Basics: Chicks, Layers and Broilers in Kenya

Updated 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Poultry feed needs change as birds grow. Chicks require high protein for development, broilers need energy for fast growth, and layers need specific calcium for egg production. Always follow the manufacturer’s label for age-appropriate feeding and consult a professional nutritionist for specific flock requirements.

Raising poultry in Kenya requires careful management of nutrition to ensure your birds stay healthy and productive. Whether you are keeping layers for eggs or raising broilers for meat, the feed you choose plays a vital role. Using the wrong feed for a specific growth stage can lead to poor performance and unnecessary costs.

Why does the feed stage matter?

Poultry nutritional requirements are not static. A day-old chick has very different needs compared to a bird nearing the end of its production cycle. Feed manufacturers design different products to match these biological changes.

When you buy poultry feed Kenya options, you will notice labels indicating specific life stages. Using 'starter' feed for adult birds or 'layer' feed for chicks can disrupt their growth or health. Always match the feed type to the age and purpose of your birds to support their development efficiently.

How do I read a poultry feed label?

The label on your feed bag is your most important guide. It provides essential information about the nutritional composition, including protein, energy, and mineral levels designed for specific bird types.

Look for the 'intended use' section on the bag. It will clearly state if the product is meant for chicks, growers, layers feed, or broiler feed. If you are unsure about the components, such as when to supplement with poultry feed premix, always speak to a qualified nutritionist or vet before adding anything to the birds' diet.

How can I prevent mould in stored feed?

Feed storage is just as important as the quality of the feed itself. Moisture is the enemy of poultry rations. If feed gets damp, it can quickly develop mould, which is harmful to your flock.

Store your bags in a cool, dry, and well-ventilated area, preferably off the floor using pallets. Keep the storage area clean and ensure it is protected from pests like rats, which can contaminate the feed. If you notice any clumping, unusual smells, or visible mould, do not feed it to your birds.

Which feed is right for my flock?

Choosing between broiler feed and layers feed depends entirely on your farming goals. Broiler feed is formulated to support rapid muscle growth and weight gain, whereas layers feed focuses on sustaining egg production and shell quality.

Frequently asked questions

Can I mix different types of poultry feed?

It is generally not recommended to mix feed types. Each feed is formulated to meet specific nutritional balances for a certain age. Mixing them can lead to an unbalanced diet, which may affect the health and productivity of your birds.

How long can I store poultry feed?

Poultry feed is best used fresh. Over time, the nutritional value can degrade, and the risk of spoilage or pest infestation increases. It is best to buy only what you can use within a reasonable timeframe, keeping it in a dry, airtight container.

Should I add supplements to my bird's feed?

Commercial feeds are typically balanced for the birds' needs. Do not add supplements, vitamins, or minerals unless specifically advised by a veterinarian or a qualified poultry nutritionist, as over-supplementing can sometimes be harmful.

How do I know if my feed has gone bad?

Check for any signs of moisture, mould, or a musty smell. Also, look for signs of pest activity, such as droppings or holes in the bag. If the feed looks or smells different than when you first bought it, do not use it.

Proper feeding is the foundation of a successful poultry project. By following the labels, storing your bags correctly, and seeking professional advice when in doubt, you give your birds the best chance to thrive.
