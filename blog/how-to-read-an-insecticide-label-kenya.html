How to read an insecticide label: PCPB, ingredients and warnings

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Read the label before you buy and again before you use. Look for the PCPB registration, the active ingredient, the pests and places the product is meant for, the directions, the precautions and first aid steps, and the expiry date. If any of these is missing, do not use the product.

Every pest control product sold in Kenya should come with a label, and that label is the most important safety document you have. Many accidents and failed treatments happen because people skip it. This guide walks through the parts of an insecticide label, what each one tells you and what to do when something is missing. We stock products in pest control and fumigation, and the same checks apply to any of them.

What does the PCPB registration on a label mean?

PCPB stands for the Pest Control Products Board, the body that regulates pest control products in Kenya. A registered product shows its PCPB registration details on the pack or label. This tells you the product has been registered for sale, which is one reason to prefer products that show it clearly.

If you cannot find the registration details, ask the seller to show you where they are. If they cannot, or if the label is missing or damaged, choose another product.

What is the active ingredient and why does it matter?

The active ingredient is the chemical that does the work. It is named on the label, usually with its strength. It matters for two reasons: a doctor or vet can use it to give you the right advice after an accident, and it helps you avoid using two products with the same ingredient at the same time.

Keep the label or a photo of it when you use a product, so the name is easy to find if you need medical help.

Which pests and places is the product made for?

A label lists the pests it is meant for and the places where it may be used, such as indoors, in animal houses or on crops. Using a product on a pest or in a place that is not listed can be ineffective or unsafe.

For example, a product made for crops should not be used on food in the home, and a product made for farm structures should not be used on animals. When in doubt, ask an extension officer, vet or pest control professional.

What do the directions and warnings say?

The directions explain how to apply the product, what protective clothing to wear, how long to keep people and animals away and how to ventilate. Many labels also use symbols or a colour band to show the level of hazard. Follow every step exactly, and never mix or dilute a product in a way the label does not describe.

The first aid section tells you what to do if the product touches your skin or eyes, is swallowed or is breathed in. Read it before you start, not after something goes wrong.

What should I do with leftovers and empty containers?

Keep leftover product in its original container with the label intact, in a locked place away from food, feed, children and animals. Never pour it into a drink bottle, and never reuse an empty container for water or food. Dispose of empties as the label and local guidance direct.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use an insecticide on a pest that is not on the label?

It is not advisable. The product was tested and registered for the pests and places named on the label. Using it elsewhere may not work and may be unsafe. Ask a pest control professional, extension officer or vet for a suitable product.

What if the label is torn or faded?

Do not use the product. Without the label you cannot confirm what it is, how to use it or what to do in an emergency. Ask the seller for a replacement or choose another product with a clear label.

Is it fine to use more product to get faster results?

No. Using more than the label allows can harm people, pets and the environment, and it does not make the product work better. Stick to the directions and repeat treatments only as the label permits.

Why does the expiry date matter?

An old product may lose strength or change, so it may not work as intended. Check the date before you buy and before you use it, and store products cool, dry and closed, as the label advises.

Who can I ask if I do not understand a label?

Ask a licensed pest control operator, an agrovet or extension officer, or a vet for animal products. If someone has been exposed to a product, contact a doctor straight away and take the label with you.

A few minutes with the label can prevent most accidents and many wasted treatments. Read it first, follow it exactly, and keep it with the product.
