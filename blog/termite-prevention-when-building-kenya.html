Termite prevention when building a house in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: The cheapest time to stop termites is before the floor goes in. Clear stumps, roots and buried wood from the site, have a licensed operator treat the foundation soil with a registered product, use good barriers and damp-proofing, keep timber off the ground, and inspect the house regularly after you move in.

Termites are common across Kenya, and a new house on cleared land can be attacked within a few years if nothing was done at the building stage. Fixing damage in roofs, doors and furniture later costs far more than prevention during construction. This guide walks through what to do at each stage of the build.

What should I do before construction starts?

Clear the building area of tree stumps, roots, old logs and any buried wood, because these feed termites. Look for mounds on or near the plot; a licensed pest control operator can advise on how to deal with an active colony instead of simply knocking down the mound. Do not bury timber offcuts or wooden shuttering in the ground, and keep stored timber off the soil.

What is pre-construction soil treatment?

It is a treatment applied to the exposed soil in the foundation area, before the slab or floor is laid, to create a protective zone under and around the building. This is best done by a trained, licensed operator using a PCPB-registered product, because the product and method must be right for the site. We do not give mixing rates or amounts here; the label and the operator will specify them. Ask for a written record of the product, the date and the areas treated, and ask what follow-up or warranty applies.

Which building details help block termites?

  • A well-compacted, solid foundation and a properly laid concrete slab with no cracks
  • A damp-proof course that is continuous and not bridged by soil, plaster or paving
  • Sealed gaps where pipes, cables and service lines pass through floors and walls
  • Slopes and drainage that carry water away from the walls, and repairs to any leaks quickly
  • Garden soil, mulch, firewood and stored boxes kept below or away from the damp-proof course so termites cannot use them as a hidden bridge

Which timber should I use?

Use treated timber or naturally durable species for roof trusses, door frames and window frames, and keep wood from sitting directly on the ground or touching damp walls. Treating cut ends and drilled holes matters, because these are where protection is broken. Treated timber works best together with soil treatment and good detailing, not as the only defence.

How do I check for termites after the house is built?

Walk around the house a couple of times a year, and after the rains. Look for thin mud tubes on foundations and walls, hollow-sounding timber, powdery material near wood, and winged insects near lights. Frequently asked questions

When should I treat the soil for termites when building?

Pre-construction treatment is done before the concrete slab or floor is laid, when the soil in the foundation area is still exposed. A licensed pest control operator should apply a registered product and give you a record of what was done.

Do I need to remove tree stumps and roots from the site?

Yes. Buried wood, stumps and roots are a food source for termites. Clear them from the building footprint and do not bury timber offcuts or wooden shuttering in the soil around the house.

Is treated timber enough on its own?

Treated or naturally durable timber helps, but it works best together with soil treatment, good detailing and regular inspections. No single measure is a guarantee, so layer your protection.

Can I treat for termites after the house is built?

Yes, a licensed operator can treat an existing building, but it is harder and costs more than treating the soil before the floor goes in. Pre-construction treatment is the best time.

How often should I inspect a new house for termites?

Look at least a couple of times a year, and after the rains, for mud tubes on walls and foundations, damaged timber, and winged insects near lights. Early signs are much easier to deal with than a long-running attack.

Prevention during the build is cheaper and more reliable than a cure later. Plan it with your contractor from day one and keep the paperwork.
