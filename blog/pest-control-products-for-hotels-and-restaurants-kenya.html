Pest control for hotels, restaurants and rentals in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Start with sanitation, sealing gaps and regular inspections, then add products suited to each area: gel baits and traps for kitchens, targeted products for guest rooms. Keep a record of every treatment, and call a licensed pest control operator for large infestations or whenever you are unsure.

Hotels, restaurants and rental properties share the same problem: pests put off guests, threaten food safety and damage your reputation. One review mentioning cockroaches or bed bugs can travel faster than any advert. The good news is that most pest problems follow a pattern, and a simple routine will catch them early.

Which pests cause the most trouble in food and hospitality premises?

Cockroaches, rats and mice, flies and ants are the usual suspects in kitchens, stores and dining areas, because food, water and shelter meet there. In guest rooms and rentals, bed bugs and mosquitoes are the ones guests notice first. Each pest needs a slightly different response, so identify what you are dealing with before you buy anything.

  • Kitchens and stores: cockroaches, rats, mice, ants, flies
  • Guest rooms and rentals: bed bugs, mosquitoes
  • Bins, drains and compounds: flies, cockroaches, rodents

How do I stop pests before reaching for a product?

Hygiene and building upkeep do most of the work. Clean grease traps and drains regularly, wipe down surfaces after service, keep dry goods in sealed containers off the floor, and empty and wash bins daily. Repair dripping taps and leaks, because many pests need water more than food.

Then close the doors on them. Seal gaps around pipes and door frames, fit door sweeps and window screens, and check deliveries before they go into the store. Our guide on how to get rid of cockroaches goes into the kitchen routine in more detail.

Which product types suit kitchens, stores and guest rooms?

In general terms, gel baits placed in cracks and crevices away from food preparation surfaces are popular for cockroaches. Residual sprays cover harder-to-reach areas, while traps and glue boards help you monitor activity. For rodents, tamper-resistant bait stations placed outside or in store areas are common, and for rooms with bed bugs there are dedicated products.

Whatever you choose, the label decides where it may be used and how. Look for a PCPB registration on the pack and check whether the product is allowed in food areas. What records should I keep?

Write down the date, the area, the pest you saw, what you did, the product name and who did it, plus a follow-up date. A simple notebook or spreadsheet is enough. Over a few months the records show where problems start, and they demonstrate to inspectors and guests that you take hygiene seriously.

When should I call a licensed operator?

Bring in a licensed pest control operator for large or repeated infestations, whole-building treatments, work in areas where food is prepared while you trade, or any product marked for professional use. A professional can also advise on rotating treatments and on what your premises must have in place to stay compliant.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a hotel or restaurant inspect for pests?

Do a quick visual check daily in kitchens, stores and bin areas, and a fuller walk-through of every room and storage space at least weekly. Busy premises or those with a past problem may need more frequent checks. Write down what you find so patterns show up early.

Can I use the same product in the kitchen and in guest rooms?

Not necessarily. Labels state where and how a product may be used, and food areas have stricter needs than bedrooms. Check the label for permitted places, keep products away from food and preparation surfaces, and ask a qualified professional if the label is unclear.

Do I need a licensed pest control operator?

Small, early problems can often be handled in-house with good hygiene and suitable products used per the label. For large or repeated infestations, whole-building treatments, or products meant for professional use, a licensed operator is the safer choice and may be expected by inspectors.

Why do pests keep coming back after I treat?

Products alone rarely fix the cause. Pests return if food, water and hiding places remain available, or if they re-enter through gaps and deliveries. Fix leaks, seal entry points, tighten waste handling and keep inspecting after treatment to confirm the problem has really stopped.
