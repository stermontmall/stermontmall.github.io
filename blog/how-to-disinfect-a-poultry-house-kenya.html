Clean and Disinfect a Poultry House Between Batches

Updated 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer: To clean and disinfect a poultry house between batches, remove all birds and old litter, dry clean dust and organic matter, wash thoroughly with water and detergent, apply an approved livestock disinfectant following the label instructions, and let the unit dry and rest empty before placing new chicks.

Running a successful chicken farm in Kenya depends heavily on strict routine hygiene. When one flock leaves your farm, invisible pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, and parasites can linger in the dust, floor crevices, and drinkers. Giving your poultry structure a thorough cleanout between batches breaks disease cycles and provides your next flock with a fresh, safe environment.

Why is poultry biosecurity important between flocks?

Poultry biosecurity refers to all practical measures taken to keep disease-causing organisms away from your flock. When broilers are harvested or spent layers are culled, the remaining litter, feathers, and manure harbor millions of micro-organisms. If you simply spread fresh wood shavings over old litter and bring in day-old chicks, the new birds face immediate infection risks before their immune systems develop.

A structured downtime period between flocks prevents disease buildup. Taking time to properly clean poultry house surfaces ensures that vaccines and quality feed can do their job effectively. When birds do not have to fight off background farm infections, they grow faster, convert feed better, and suffer lower mortality rates throughout the production cycle.

How do I prepare and dry clean the poultry house?

Preparation begins the moment the old flock leaves the premises. Start by removing all movable equipment, including feeders, drinkers, brooder guards, and laying nests. Take these items outside to a dedicated washing area where they can be soaked, scrubbed, and disinfected separately.

Next, remove all used bedding, manure, and feathers from the house. Bag the old litter immediately and move it far away from the poultry building. Leaving heaps of used manure right next to the coop allows winds or farm workers to carry disease organisms straight back inside.

Once the floor is bare, carry out a thorough dry clean. Use stiff brooms, scrapers, and brushes to sweep cobwebs from the ceiling, brush dust off wire mesh, and dislodge caked manure from concrete floors and corners. Removing as much dry dirt as possible is essential because organic matter rapidly deactivates chemical disinfectants.

  • Take out all movable feeding and drinking equipment
  • Bag and remove old litter to a distant compost area
  • Sweep roofs, beams, netting, and curtain folds
  • Scrape off caked droppings from floors and walls

What is the right way to wash poultry surfaces?

After dry cleaning, the next stage is wet washing. Wetting down surfaces with clean water and a farm detergent loosens greasy deposits, bird oils, and stubborn dirt. Start washing from the top of the house and work your way down to the floor, ensuring water runs away from the building.

Pay close attention to rough timber joints, cracks in concrete, ventilation openings, and drain channels. If you have access to a pressure washer, use it to blast out stubborn grime from hard-to-reach corners. If washing by hand, use stiff scrubbing brushes to scour surfaces until you can see the bare material underneath.

Rinse everything thoroughly with fresh, clean water to remove dirt residues and detergent foam. Excess detergent left behind can neutralize certain sanitizers applied in the next stage. Allow surfaces to drain and dry partially before moving on to disinfection.

How long should the poultry house rest before new chicks arrive?

Applying disinfectant is not the final step; the poultry building must be allowed to dry completely and rest empty. This resting period is known in poultry biosecurity as downtime. Keeping the house empty for at least ten to fourteen days allows remaining pathogens to die off naturally in the dry, aired environment.

During the resting period, keep the poultry house securely locked to prevent wild birds, rodents, dogs, or stray livestock from wandering inside and recontaminating the clean floor. Place rodent control traps or tamper-proof bait stations around the building perimeter to prevent pests from settling in before the next batch arrives.

Two to three days before your day-old chicks or point-of-lay pullets arrive, lay down fresh, dry, dust-free wood shavings. Reinstall the clean, disinfected drinkers and feeders, test your heating equipment, and set up a clean biosecurity footbath at the entrance. Frequently asked questions

Can I spray disinfectant directly over old chicken litter?

No, spraying disinfectant over old litter is ineffective. Manure, feathers, and dust neutralize active disinfectant ingredients before they can reach pathogens. You must remove all litter, sweep, and wash surfaces thoroughly with water before applying any sanitizer.

How long should a poultry house stay empty between batches?

Most poultry specialists recommend an empty resting period of at least 10 to 14 days after cleaning and disinfection. This downtime allows surfaces to dry fully and deprives lingering organisms of a host, reducing disease carryover to the incoming flock.

What personal protective equipment do I need when spraying disinfectant?

Always wear chemical-resistant rubber gloves, gumboots, an impervious apron or overall, protective eye goggles, and a suitable respirator or face mask. This prevents skin contact, eye irritation, and inhalation of airborne chemical droplets while spraying inside enclosed sheds.

Where should I dispose of used poultry litter?

Move used poultry litter well away from the poultry houses immediately after removal. Store it in a covered compost pit or spread it on distant crop fields. Stacking old litter near active chicken houses allows wind and foot traffic to carry pathogens straight back.

How do I clean poultry drinkers and feeders between batches?

Take all feeders and drinkers outside to a wash station. Empty leftover feed and water, scrub them thoroughly with water and detergent to remove slime and feed residues, rinse with clean water, soak or spray with livestock disinfectant, and air dry before reinstallation.

Carrying out a disciplined cleaning and disinfection routine between poultry batches protects your flock from avoidable diseases and helps you achieve consistent farming results. Take time during every turnover to follow each step thoroughly, adhere strictly to product labels, and consult your local extension officer whenever you need professional advice.
