ULV Fogger Maintenance in Kenya: Cleaning, Battery Care and Storage

Pest-control equipment guide · 6 November 2026

Quick answer: Maintain a ULV fogger by following its exact manual: inspect the tank, hose, nozzle and seals; isolate power before cleaning; keep electrical parts dry; and store the unit clean and dry. Never improvise chemical flushing or dismantling instructions.

A ULV fogger is only useful when it is in sound working condition and the liquid pathway is compatible with the formulation being used. Blocked nozzles, damaged seals, weak batteries or residue left in the tank can interrupt a job and may create avoidable exposure risks. A simple inspection routine helps a pest-control business identify faults before taking equipment to a customer's premises.

Start with the manufacturer's manual

Foggers differ in their pump, nozzle, tank material, seals, filters and power system. A cleaning method that is suitable for one machine may damage another. Keep the manual with the equipment and use it for the approved cleaning liquid, flushing procedure, nozzle removal, lubrication and service intervals. If the manual is missing, ask the supplier or manufacturer for the correct version for the exact model.

Do not use solvents, oils, detergents or improvised mixtures unless the manufacturer specifically approves them. Do not open a motor, battery pack or electrical housing as part of routine cleaning.

Pre-use inspection checklist

  • Tank and cap: look for cracks, leaks, swelling, damaged threads and a cap that does not seal.
  • Hose and connections: check for splits, loose fittings, kinks or residue around joints.
  • Nozzle and filter: inspect for visible blockage or damage. Clean only by the method in the manual; never poke a nozzle with wire or enlarge its opening.
  • Power system: check the battery, charger, plug and cable for damage. Do not operate a unit with exposed wires or a swollen battery.
  • Controls and straps: confirm switches, handles and carrying supports are secure and operate as intended.
  • Accessories: confirm that seals, filters, charger and any supplied protective accessories are present.

Cleaning after a job

First, follow the chemical label and the machine manual for safe shutdown, emptying and cleaning. Keep the unit away from people, animals, food and water sources while handling residues. Wear the protective equipment specified for the product. Never pour leftover chemical or rinse water into a storm drain, river, well, kitchen sink or fish pond; follow the label and local disposal requirements.

Disconnect the power source before cleaning any part that the manual identifies as requiring isolation. Keep water away from electrical contacts, the motor, battery and charger. Clean the tank and liquid pathway only as directed. Allow the parts to dry fully before reassembly or storage, and label the machine if it is reserved for a particular approved formulation.

Battery and charging care

For a cordless fogger, use only the charger specified for that model. Charge in a dry, ventilated place away from heat, water and combustible materials. Do not leave a damaged, unusually hot or swollen battery in service. Avoid storing a battery fully exposed to heat or moisture, and follow the manufacturer's guidance for long-term charge level and periodic checks.

Record charging problems and runtime changes. A battery that suddenly loses operating time may need inspection or replacement; do not attempt a homemade repair. For the VectorFog DC20, the supplier listing states up to 40 minutes of continuous operation and about three hours to charge, but actual runtime varies with battery condition and operating conditions.

Storage between jobs

After the approved cleaning procedure, store the fogger dry, secure and out of direct sunlight. Keep it away from food, feed, drinking water, children and unauthorised users. Store chemicals separately in their original labelled containers, not inside the fogger unless the equipment manual and product label expressly require that arrangement. Keep the charger and removable battery protected from damp conditions.

For a business with several machines, label each unit with its model, serial or asset number, last service date and any formulation restrictions. This makes it easier to identify which machine needs attention and reduces the risk of using the wrong accessory.

Keep a simple maintenance record

Record the date, operator, machine ID, inspection result, cleaning completed, parts replaced and any fault reported. A short record can reveal recurring nozzle blockages, battery decline or leaking seals. Remove faulty equipment from service until it has been checked and repaired by a competent person. Do not rely on a fogger that has an intermittent switch, damaged wiring, leaks or abnormal vibration.

Final maintenance reminder

Equipment maintenance does not make an unapproved chemical safe to fog. Use only a formulation whose label explicitly allows the intended ULV application, and follow its PPE, access-control, ventilation and re-entry instructions. If the machine manual and product label appear to conflict, stop and seek clarification from the manufacturer or supplier before using the unit.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I clean a ULV fogger?

Follow the machine manual and product label after each use or whenever the manufacturer requires cleaning. Do not assume one cleaning schedule applies to every model.

Can I rinse an electric fogger with water?

Only clean the parts and pathways using the procedure approved in the manual. Keep the motor, battery, charger and electrical contacts dry.

Can I use a different charger for my cordless fogger?

Use only the charger specified or approved for the exact model. An incompatible charger can damage the battery or create a safety risk.

What should I do if the nozzle is blocked?

Stop using the machine and follow the manufacturer's nozzle-cleaning procedure. Do not force wire or tools into the nozzle or enlarge the opening.
