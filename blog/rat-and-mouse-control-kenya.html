Rat and mouse control in Kenya: a home and farm guide

Updated 15 October 2026 · 4 min read

Rats and mice eat and spoil food, chew wires and carry germs. Whether they are in a house, shop, restaurant or farm store, the plan is the same: find them, block them out and control them safely.

Signs of rodents

Look for droppings, gnaw marks on food packs, wood and wires, greasy marks along walls, scratching noises at night, nests made of paper or cloth and a strong smell.

Step 1: Remove food and shelter

Store food, grain and animal feed in strong, sealed containers. Keep bins closed and clear rubbish, piles of wood and clutter that give them a place to hide. Fix leaking taps and pipes.

Step 2: Block the entry points

A mouse can squeeze through a very small gap. Close holes around pipes, gaps under doors and cracks in walls using cement, wire mesh or metal sheet, because rodents chew through soft materials.

Step 3: Use traps or baits carefully

Snap traps and glue boards can be used along walls, where rodents travel. Rodent baits must be a registered product, used exactly as the label says and placed where children, pets and livestock cannot reach them. Check traps often and remove dead rodents with gloves.

Step 4: Clean up safely

Wear gloves, avoid sweeping dry droppings into the air, wipe surfaces with a disinfectant and wash your hands well. Throw dead rodents away in a sealed bag.

Step 5: Keep monitoring

Check for new signs for several weeks. If rodents keep coming back, look for a missed entry point or food source.

Safety first. Read the whole label. Look for the Pest Control Products Board (PCPB) registration on the pack. Never place baits where children, pets or livestock can reach, and never mix products. For restaurants, warehouses or heavy infestations, use a licensed pest control operator.
