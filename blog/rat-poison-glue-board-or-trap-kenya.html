Rat poison, glue board or snap trap: how to choose in Kenya

Updated 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: Snap traps are a simple choice for a few rats indoors, glue boards catch but raise welfare and safety concerns, and baits suit larger infestations when placed in tamper-resistant stations out of reach. Whichever you choose, keep children and pets away and follow the label.

Rats in a house, shop or store are more than a nuisance: they gnaw wiring, contaminate food and can spread disease. People in Kenya usually choose between three tools, rodent baits, glue boards and snap traps. Each has strengths and risks. This guide compares them so you can choose safely. For signs of rats and basic prevention, read our rat and mouse control guide.

What are the main ways to control rats?

Baits are food-based products designed to be eaten by rodents. They are used in bait stations and must be handled with great care because they can also harm children, pets and other animals. Glue boards are sticky trays that hold a rodent in place. Snap traps are spring traps designed to kill quickly when a rodent triggers them.

Many professionals use traps and baits as one part of a plan that also includes proofing and cleaning, because no single product solves a rat problem on its own.

Which option suits a home with children or pets?

Any bait or trap can injure a child or pet if it is left in the open. Baits should only be placed in tamper-resistant stations, out of reach, as the label directs. Snap traps are better placed inside covered boxes or behind furniture where only rodents can reach. Glue boards can catch pets and wildlife by accident.

If you have toddlers or animals that roam, ask a professional before you place any rodent product, and keep the product label and emergency contact details close by.

Are glue boards a good idea?

Glue boards are popular because they are cheap and simple, but they are also the most criticised option. A trapped rat may suffer for a long time, and boards can catch birds, lizards and pets. Animal welfare groups often advise against them.

If you do use one, check it often, place it where nothing else can reach it, and follow the label and any local rules.

Where should I place traps and baits?

Rats travel along walls and use the same routes night after night. Place traps or stations along walls, behind appliances and near signs such as droppings and gnaw marks, not in the middle of the floor. Check them every day, wear gloves, and dispose of carcasses safely in a sealed bag in line with local guidance.

  • Place devices along walls where you see droppings or gnaw marks
  • Keep baits in covered, tamper-resistant stations
  • Check traps daily and wear gloves
  • Wash your hands after handling anything

When should I call a licensed operator?

Shops, restaurants, stores and farms with a large rat population, or any place where food is handled, are better served by a licensed operator who can inspect, proof and monitor. Remove food sources too: cover grain and feed, store goods off the floor and seal holes, otherwise new rats will replace those you remove.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if my child or pet eats rat bait?

Treat it as an emergency. Contact a doctor, a hospital or a vet straight away and take the product label or packet with you, because it shows the active ingredient. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.

Where do I put rat bait?

Place it in a tamper-resistant station along walls and runways, in places children, pets and livestock cannot reach, as the label directs. Keep it away from food and water, and check the station regularly.

Do traps or bait work faster?

Snap traps can remove a rat on the first night, while baits take longer because rats must eat them first. Speed matters less than safe placement, so choose the option that fits your home and follow the label.

How do I stop rats from coming back?

Seal holes around pipes and doors, store food and feed in closed containers, keep waste covered and keep the compound tidy. Check regularly for fresh droppings or gnaw marks so you can act early.

Can I use rat bait inside the house?

Baits are usually placed in stations, and the label says where they may be used. Indoor use around food and children needs extra care. If you are unsure, ask a licensed operator rather than placing loose bait.

There is no single perfect rat control product. Pick the option that fits your home, place it safely, remove the food and shelter that attract rats, and keep checking until the signs stop.
