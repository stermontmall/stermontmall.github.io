Mosquito control at home in Kenya: simple steps that work

Updated 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: The most effective mosquito control is to stop them breeding. Empty and scrub containers with standing water every week, cover tanks, cut long grass, use screens and a treated bed net, and use a registered mosquito product only as the label directs.

Mosquitoes are more than a nuisance. They can spread diseases such as malaria. The most effective control starts with stopping them from breeding, then protecting your home.

Step 1: How do I remove standing water?

Mosquitoes lay eggs in still water. Once a week, empty and scrub containers, buckets, flower pots, old tyres, tins and blocked gutters. Cover water tanks and drums with a tight lid or fine net, and fill in puddles in the compound.

Step 2: How do I keep mosquitoes out of the house?

Fit mosquito screens on windows and doors where you can, repair holes in nets and close gaps under the roof. Sleep under a treated bed net, especially for children and pregnant women.

Step 3: Why should I tidy the compound?

Cut long grass and thick bushes near the house, because adult mosquitoes rest there during the day. Keep drains clear so water flows.

Step 4: Which mosquito products can I use?

Sprays, coils and other mosquito products can reduce biting around the home. Choose a product registered for mosquito control, use it in the way the label says and ventilate rooms where the label requires it.

Step 5: How do I protect myself and my family?

Wear long sleeves in the evening and use a repellent that is safe for your age group, following the label. If you or your child get a fever, see a health worker without delay.

Safety first. Read the label. Look for the Pest Control Products Board (PCPB) registration on the pack. Keep products away from food, water and children, and never spray directly on fish ponds or drinking water.
