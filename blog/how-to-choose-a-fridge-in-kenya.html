How to choose a fridge in Kenya: size and types

Updated 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: Pick a fridge by household size: roughly 100 to 200 litres for one or two people, 200 to 350 litres for three to five, and 350 litres or more for bigger households. Then choose the type (single door, double door, side by side or chest freezer), frost-free or direct cool, and check the delivery on arrival.

A fridge runs all day for years, so it is worth choosing the right one. This guide covers size, type and the checks to do before and after you buy.

1. What size fridge do I need?

As a rough guide, a small fridge of about 100 to 200 litres suits one or two people, 200 to 350 litres suits a family of three to five, and bigger households or people who buy in bulk may want 350 litres or more. Check the capacity on the label, and measure your space, including room at the back and sides for air to flow.

2. Which type of fridge should I choose?

Single door: compact and budget friendly, with a small freezer compartment inside. Double door (top or bottom freezer): a separate freezer section, which suits families. Side by side: two tall doors, more space and a higher price. Chest freezer: for storing lots of frozen food, such as meat for a small business.

3. Should I choose frost-free or direct cool?

Frost-free fridges stop ice building up, so you do not need to defrost them, and they usually cost more. Direct cool fridges are cheaper but need manual defrosting from time to time.

4. How much electricity does a fridge use?

A fridge uses electricity all day, so check the energy rating if the product page lists one. Power dips and surges can happen, so a fridge guard or stabiliser helps protect the compressor.

5. Which features matter most?

Look at shelf layout, door storage, a vegetable drawer, a lock if you need one, and the noise level. Features you will not use only add cost.

6. What should I check when it is delivered?

Check the fridge for dents or damage and make sure the doors close properly. Leave it standing upright for a few hours before you switch it on, as the maker's manual usually advises, so the oil in the compressor settles.
