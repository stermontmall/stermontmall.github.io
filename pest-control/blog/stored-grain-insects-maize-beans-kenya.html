Stored Grain Insects in Kenya: Protecting Maize, Beans and Cereals

Stored grain pests can damage both the commodity and the value of a harvest. Prevention begins before storage, with clean stores, dry commodities, careful inspection and traceable stock management.

Recognise early signs

Look for live beetles or weevils, larvae, webbing, powdery frass, damaged kernels, holes in bags, clumping and unusual heating. Inspect more than the top layer of a stack; concealed activity can be missed by a quick visual check.

Prepare the store before loading

Remove old grain and residues, clean cracks and ledges, repair leaks and check pallets. Ensure the store is suitable for the commodity and can be kept dry. Do not store new grain on top of old residues or mix suspect deliveries with clean stock.

Manage bags and stacks

Keep bags off the floor and away from damp walls. Leave space for inspection and air movement where appropriate. Identify each lot and use first-in-first-out stock rotation. Repair damaged bags and investigate any recurring signs.

Choose treatment responsibly

A pesticide intended for building surfaces is not automatically suitable for grain. Commodity treatment must be specifically authorised for the commodity and used according to its label and relevant requirements. Specialist fumigation is a controlled activity, not a DIY store spray.

Monitor and keep records

Record supplier, delivery date, commodity, quantity, stack location, inspection findings and action taken. Check for rodents and water ingress as well as insects. A log helps identify whether the issue arrived with a delivery or developed in storage.

When professional support is useful

If infestation is established within stored stock, separate affected lots where practical and obtain a competent assessment before treatment or disposal decisions. Provide commodity, volume, packaging and intended use.
