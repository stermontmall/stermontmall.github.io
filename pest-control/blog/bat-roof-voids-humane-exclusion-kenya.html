Bats in the Roof: Humane Exclusion and Safe Clean-up

Bats may roost in roof voids, eaves and gaps in buildings. Exclusion should be planned carefully so animals are not trapped inside.

Understand the signs

Bats may roost in roof voids, eaves and gaps in buildings. Exclusion should be planned carefully so animals are not trapped inside.

Inspect and identify the source

Look for entry points and signs of activity from outside. Avoid entering a contaminated roof space or touching bats and droppings. A qualified assessment should establish whether the roost is occupied and the safest timing for exclusion.

Prevention starts with the environment

A humane exclusion plan may use one-way exit methods followed by sealing confirmed entry points. The sequence matters: sealing an occupied roost can trap animals inside and create additional hygiene problems.

Choosing an appropriate response

Droppings should be assessed before clean-up. Avoid dry sweeping or vacuuming contaminated material. Use competent remediation with suitable protective measures and local public-health advice.

Monitor and follow up

If a person or pet has direct contact with a bat, seek prompt medical or veterinary advice even if no obvious injury is visible.
