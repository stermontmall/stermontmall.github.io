Termite Warning Signs and Treatment Options for Kenyan Buildings

Termites can remain hidden while damaging timber and other cellulose-containing materials. Early inspection helps establish the extent of activity before repairs become more extensive.

Signs to look for

Possible signs include mud tubes, hollow-sounding timber, blistered paint, discarded wings after swarming, damaged door frames and unexplained timber weakness. Not every damaged board is termite damage, so inspection is important.

Understand the building context

Check areas with soil-to-wood contact, damp foundations, leaking plumbing, garden beds against walls, timber stored beside the building and concealed service penetrations. Moisture problems can increase risk and should be corrected.

Pre-construction and post-construction are different

Pre-construction measures are planned around the building stage and specified soil or structural interfaces. Post-construction work must account for existing occupants, finishes, access and the actual route of termite activity. They should not be described as one identical treatment.

Do not disturb evidence prematurely

Avoid removing every mud tube or applying random chemicals before an inspection. Evidence can help trace the route and distinguish active from old activity. Keep children and pets away from damaged or treated areas.

Ask for a clear treatment scope

A quotation should state the areas inspected, findings, proposed method, preparation, access requirements, limitations, follow-up and any warranty terms. Ask what building repairs or moisture corrections are also needed.

Monitor over time

Termite risk can persist where entry routes or conducive conditions remain. Keep inspection access clear and investigate new swarmers, tubes or timber damage promptly.
