Restaurant Pest Management: A Practical Kenya Guide

Build prevention into kitchen hygiene, stock receiving and daily routines.

Ask SterMont about your pest issue

Restaurants attract pests when food, water and shelter are accessible. Risk points include drains, grease traps, delivery doors, dry stores, gaps under equipment, refuse areas and damaged door seals. Inspection should map these locations and identify supporting conditions.

What to check first

Keep food sealed, clean spills, remove waste on schedule, maintain drains, rotate stock and inspect deliveries. Keep a pest sighting log with date, location and evidence. Never apply pesticides around exposed food or active preparation areas.

Practical next steps

Coordinate work with the person responsible for food safety. Ask for a site-specific plan, treatment records, preparation instructions, restricted-area controls and follow-up findings. Correct sanitation failures and entry points.

When to call a pest professional

Arrange an assessment when pest activity is widespread, keeps returning, affects food or business operations, may involve structural damage, or cannot be identified confidently. A professional should explain the evidence, treatment options, preparation, safety controls and whether follow-up is needed.
