Pest Proofing and Building Exclusion in Kenya

Reduce pest entry into homes, restaurants, warehouses and commercial buildings with practical proofing and exclusion measures.

Request a site-specific quote

Pest proofing addresses the routes pests use to enter or move through a property. It is a key part of long-term pest management because treatment alone cannot correct a gap beneath a door, an unsealed service penetration or a damaged drain cover.

Common entry routes

Inspect gaps around pipes and cables, damaged air vents, roof edges, broken drain covers, door thresholds, loading bays, wall cracks, ceiling voids and vegetation bridging the building envelope. Rodents can exploit surprisingly small openings.

Rodent exclusion

Look for gnaw marks, rub marks, droppings and repeated movement along walls. Repair suitable openings with durable materials compatible with the building, fit door sweeps where appropriate, protect vents and keep loading doors closed when not in use. Foam alone may not withstand rodent gnawing.

Insect proofing

Repair window and door screens, seal suitable cracks, maintain drain traps and remove standing water. For flying insects, review external lighting and entry practices around loading and service doors.

Warehouse and food-site priorities

Protect goods from wall and floor contact, maintain clear inspection lanes, close gaps around conveyors and pipework, keep waste areas clean and ensure pallets and deliveries are inspected. Coordinate repairs with facility maintenance teams.

Treatment plus proofing

If pests are already established, proofing may need to be staged with trapping or treatment so animals are not trapped inside walls or ceilings. The correct sequence depends on pest species and the building layout.

Assessment information

Share building type, floor plan if available, pest evidence, affected elevations and any recent repair work. A useful report separates urgent entry points from longer-term maintenance improvements.

Frequently asked questions

What affects the treatment plan?

The pest identified, extent of activity, property use, access, occupants, food or stock present and the product directions all influence the scope.

Will one visit solve every infestation?

Not always. Hidden harbourage, pest life cycles, adjoining units and unresolved building defects can require monitoring, repeat work or repairs.

What should I send for a quotation?

Share your town, property type, approximate size, pest evidence, affected areas and any sensitive occupants, stock or equipment.
