Almost all spiders eat insects, and a house with a few spiders usually has fewer flies and mosquitoes. People call for help when there are lots of webs, when large spiders appear suddenly, or when a bite or a child's fear makes the problem urgent.

A small number of spiders in Africa can cause medically significant bites. In Kenya these include widow-type spiders, called button spiders, and large burrowing ground spiders sometimes called baboon spiders in dry rural areas. The sensible rule is: do not handle spiders with bare hands, and get medical advice for a painful bite that spreads or causes illness.

Types of spiders found in Kenya

Common house spiders

Small, harmless web-builders in corners, under furniture and around windows. They are mostly a cleaning matter.

Huntsman and wall spiders

Large, flat, long-legged spiders that run quickly on walls and ceilings and do not build webs to catch prey. They look alarming, but are rarely harmful.

Button spiders (Latrodectus)

Dark, round-bodied spiders in the widow group, found in sheltered places such as outbuildings, wood piles and under stones. Their bite can cause pain and illness and needs a medical check.

Baboon spiders and large ground spiders

Large, hairy burrowing spiders found mainly in dry rural areas. They bite when handled or trapped, and a bite should be checked by a health worker.

Signs of spiders

  • Webs in corners, behind furniture, under eaves and in window frames
  • Egg sacs, which look like small silk balls, in corners and sheltered places
  • A large spider running across a wall or floor at night
  • A bite with pain, swelling or a mark, especially after moving stored boxes, firewood or garden items
  • Burrows with silk lining in the soil in dry rural plots
  • Many small flies and insects, which feed spiders and keep them near the house

Health risks and why it matters

Most spider bites are minor and heal by themselves. A bite that is very painful, spreads, causes muscle cramps, sweating, fever or vomiting, or happens in a small child, should be checked by a health worker without delay. Keep the spider if it is safe to do so, or a photo, to help the health worker.

What attracts spiders

  • Plenty of insects as prey, especially around lights
  • Clutter, stored boxes, firewood and garden items that provide hiding places
  • Gaps around doors, windows and vents
  • Long grass, bushes and creepers touching the walls
  • Undisturbed outbuildings, garages and stores

How to prevent spiders

  • Remove webs and egg sacs regularly with a broom or vacuum and empty the bag outdoors
  • Reduce clutter and store items in sealed plastic boxes off the floor
  • Seal gaps around doors, windows and pipes and fit screens
  • Shake out shoes, gloves and clothes that have been left in a store, garage or outside
  • Cut back vegetation from the walls and keep firewood away from the house
  • Switch off outdoor lights when not needed, because lights attract insects and then spiders

Frequently asked questions about spiders

Are house spiders dangerous?

Most are harmless to people and eat other insects. A few species, such as button spiders, need caution, and a painful or spreading bite should be seen by a health worker.

Why do I find spiders in the bathroom?

They come looking for small insects, which are drawn to moisture. Repairing leaks and sealing gaps reduces both.

Do you remove spiders by hand?

We remove webs and egg sacs and treat the hiding places. For large or unusual spiders, we advise you to keep away and call us.

What should I do after a spider bite?

Wash the area and keep it clean. If there is severe pain, spreading redness, cramps, sweating, fever or vomiting, or if the person is a child, go to a hospital or clinic.
