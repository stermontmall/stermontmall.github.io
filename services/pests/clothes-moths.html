Clothes moths are small, buff-coloured moths, around 6 to 8 mm long, that avoid light and run or flutter low in dark cupboards. The adults do not feed. The damage comes from their larvae, which eat natural fibres that contain keratin: wool, cashmere, silk, fur, felt, feathers and hair, and sometimes cotton or synthetic blends soiled with food, sweat or body oils.

Because the larvae live in dark corners, a wardrobe can be affected for months before anyone sees a hole. Woollen suits, school jumpers, blankets, carpets, rugs, upholstered furniture, taxidermy and piano felts are all at risk, especially if they are stored dirty or left undisturbed.

Types of clothes moths found in Kenya

Webbing clothes moth (Tineola bisselliella)

The larvae spin silk webbing and tunnels on the fabric as they feed, leaving patches of damage and silky mats.

Casemaking clothes moth (Tinea pellionella)

The larvae live inside a small silk case that they carry with them as they feed, often seen on walls and fabric.

Pantry moths (for comparison)

Different moths that eat dry food, grains and flour. They need food-store treatment, not clothes treatment.

Signs of clothes moths

  • Irregular holes and thinned patches in wool, silk and fur items
  • Silky webbing, tunnels or small cases on the fabric, in seams and in corners
  • Tiny pale larvae with dark heads, small black droppings and shed skins
  • Small buff moths in wardrobes, which avoid light and flutter low
  • Bare patches in carpets, especially under furniture and along edges
  • Damage in the folds of stored blankets and in clothes that have been left unused for a long time

Why it matters

Clothes moths do not harm people, but the damage to good clothing, school uniforms, blankets and carpets cannot be undone and can be costly. They are more of a problem in cool highland homes where woollens are used, and in stores of second-hand clothes.

What attracts clothes moths

  • Unwashed or lightly soiled wool and silk items stored for a long time
  • Dark, still, undisturbed corners in wardrobes, under beds and in boxes
  • Dust, hair and lint that collect along the edges of carpets and under furniture
  • Second-hand clothes, blankets and rugs brought into the home
  • Cupboards that are rarely opened or aired

How to prevent clothes moths

  • Wash or dry-clean woollens and silks before storing them, because food and sweat stains attract larvae
  • Store clothes in sealed plastic boxes or tight bags, not open cardboard
  • Air, brush and shake clothes and blankets in the sun now and then
  • Vacuum carpets, under furniture and wardrobe floors, paying attention to edges
  • Check stored items every month or two
  • Inspect second-hand clothes and rugs before they go into your wardrobe

Frequently asked questions about clothes moths

Do the moths I see eat my clothes?

No. The adults do not feed. The larvae cause the damage, so finding and treating the larvae and their hiding places matters most.

Does washing remove clothes moths?

Washing and drying at a high heat usually removes eggs and larvae from items that can take it. Dry cleaning is an option for delicate items.

Can they damage carpets?

Yes, especially wool carpets and the areas under furniture and along walls.

Are mothballs necessary?

Our advice is to rely on cleaning, sealed storage and treatment of the cupboard. If you use mothballs, follow the label strictly and keep them away from children and pets.
