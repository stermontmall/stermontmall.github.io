Rats and mice live close to people because we give them food, water and shelter. They breed quickly: a small problem in a roof or a store can become a large one within a few months. They gnaw through wood, plastic and cables, which is a fire risk, and they contaminate food with urine and droppings.

The first step is to find out which rodent you have and how it gets in. Rats need a gap about the width of a thumb. Mice can pass through a gap about the width of a pencil. So the job is two things at once: reduce the number of rodents with bait or traps, and close the gaps so new ones cannot replace them.

Types of rats and mice found in Kenya

Black rat (roof rat)

A slim, agile climber that nests in roofs, ceilings, trees and upper floors. It is the rat most often heard running overhead at night.

Norway rat (brown rat)

A heavier burrowing rat found around drains, rubbish, basements and ground-level gaps, often near restaurants and markets.

House mouse

A small mouse that lives in kitchens, cupboards, stores and furniture, and eats small amounts from many places.

Field rats and mice

In rural areas, multimammate rats and other field rodents live around crops, stores and farm houses, and move indoors after harvest or when fields are cleared.

Signs of rats and mice

  • Droppings along walls, behind appliances and in cupboards, stores and roofs
  • Gnaw marks on food packets, wood, soap and cables
  • Scratching and running sounds at night in ceilings and walls
  • Greasy rub marks along skirting and walls where rats travel
  • Nests of shredded paper, cloth or insulation
  • Burrows beside foundations, compost heaps and drains, and dead rodents with a smell in the ceiling

Health risks and why it matters

Rodents contaminate food and surfaces with urine, droppings and hair. Rats can carry leptospirosis, a bacterial infection spread through urine, and rodents and their fleas have been linked to plague in parts of Kenya in the past. They also chew electrical cables, and gnawed wiring is a recognised cause of fires. A restaurant, shop or warehouse with rodents risks closure and spoiled stock.

What attracts rats and mice

  • Open rubbish, uncovered bins and food waste near the building
  • Gaps around pipes, doors, roof eaves, air vents and drains
  • Food stored on the floor or in open sacks and containers
  • Dense bush, piles of timber or rubble against the walls
  • Cracked or unsealed floor drains and sewer connections

How to prevent rats and mice

  • Store food, grain and pet food in sealed metal or thick plastic containers off the floor
  • Use covered bins and clear rubbish daily
  • Seal gaps with metal mesh, cement or metal plates, since rodents chew soft materials
  • Fit door sweeps and cover vents and drains with mesh
  • Cut back branches that touch the roof, and clear bush and rubble from walls
  • Fix leaks, because rodents need water

Frequently asked questions about rats and mice

Is rat bait safe for children and pets?

Bait can be dangerous if eaten. That is why we use tamper-resistant stations placed along rodent runs and keep bait away from places children and pets can reach. Tell the technician about pets and young children before the visit.

What if a rat dies inside the wall or ceiling?

It can happen, and it may produce a smell for a while. We explain the risk before treatment and sealing gaps where possible reduces it. Good ventilation helps while the smell passes.

Why do rats keep coming back?

Usually because food, rubbish or open gaps remain. Baiting reduces the numbers, but exclusion and clean storage keep new rodents out.
