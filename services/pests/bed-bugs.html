Bed bugs are small, wingless insects that feed on the blood of people while they sleep. An adult is roughly the size of an apple seed, flat, and reddish brown, and it swells and darkens after a meal. They do not live in dirt. They live where people sleep, which is why they turn up in clean homes, boarding school dormitories, hostels, hotels and short-stay rentals as often as anywhere else.

They spread by travelling with us: in luggage, bags, second-hand beds and sofas, bedding and clothes. A single mated female carried into a flat can start an infestation that is noticed only weeks later, when the bites begin. That delay is why early inspection matters more than guessing from bite marks.

Types of bed bugs found in Kenya

Common bed bug (Cimex lectularius)

The species found worldwide. It lives in mattress seams, bed frames, headboards, sofas and cracks close to where people sleep.

Tropical bed bug (Cimex hemipterus)

A closely related species found in warm regions, including parts of Africa. It looks almost the same and is treated the same way, so you do not need to tell them apart before calling for help.

Nymphs and eggs

Young bed bugs are pale and tiny and are easy to miss. Eggs are about a millimetre long and are stuck into seams and crevices, which is why one treatment is often not enough.

Signs of bed bugs

  • Small dark spots on mattress seams, sheets or the bed frame, which are droppings
  • Tiny rusty or red stains on bedding from crushed bugs or bites that bled
  • Pale, empty shed skins and tiny white eggs in seams and under headboards
  • Itchy bites in lines or clusters on skin that is uncovered at night, though some people show no reaction at all
  • A sweet, musty smell in a room with a heavy infestation
  • Live bugs found behind headboards, in curtain folds, inside sofa seams or behind loose wallpaper and skirting

Why it matters

Bed bug bites are itchy and can become inflamed if scratched. Bed bugs are not known to pass diseases from person to person, but the broken sleep, the stress and the cost of replacing furniture are real. For a hotel, hostel, school or Airbnb, a single guest review or parent complaint can do more damage than the treatment costs.

What attracts bed bugs

  • Second-hand beds, mattresses, sofas and clothes brought home without inspection
  • Luggage placed on beds or upholstered chairs in hotels, lodges, buses and shared rooms
  • Shared walls and ceilings in flats and hostels, where bed bugs move between rooms through gaps and pipes
  • Clutter around beds that gives them more places to hide
  • Guests, visitors and new tenants who carry them in without knowing

How to prevent bed bugs

  • Inspect any second-hand bed, mattress or sofa in bright light before it enters your home
  • Keep luggage off beds and upholstered seats when you travel, and check the mattress seams on arrival
  • Wash bedding and clothes hot and dry them on high heat after a trip
  • Use a zipped mattress cover so there are fewer places to hide, and keep the bed a little away from the wall
  • Reduce clutter under and around beds
  • Seal cracks along skirting boards and around sockets in shared walls

Frequently asked questions about bed bugs

How do I know it is bed bugs and not mosquitoes or fleas?

Bite marks alone cannot tell you. Look for dark droppings, rusty stains and shed skins on the mattress seams and bed frame. If you find live bugs, or the signs above, book an inspection and we confirm what it is.

Can I sleep at home after treatment?

The technician tells you the re-entry time for the product used, which depends on the room. Treated rooms are aired, and bedding is put back clean. Follow the instructions you are given on the day.

Do bed bugs spread diseases?

They are not known to pass diseases from person to person. The main problems are itchy bites, loss of sleep and the cost of replacing furniture, so treat an infestation early.

Why do bed bugs come back after treatment?

Usually because eggs hatch after the first visit, or because new bugs arrive in luggage or second-hand furniture. A follow-up visit and the prevention steps above reduce the chance of a repeat.
