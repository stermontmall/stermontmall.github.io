Scorpions are nocturnal predators. They hide under stones, logs, bark, roof tiles, in cracks and in piles of rubble by day, and come out at night to catch insects. In dry and semi-arid parts of Kenya they can enter houses, schools, tents and stores, and they sometimes hide in shoes, bedding and clothes left on the floor.

A scorpion sting is painful, and for most healthy adults the pain and local swelling pass. Some species have venom that is dangerous, especially to small children, so any sting in a child, or a sting followed by sweating, drooling, vomiting, muscle twitching or trouble breathing, needs urgent medical care.

Types of scorpions found in Kenya

Thick-tailed scorpions (Parabuthus)

Dark scorpions with thick tails and slender pincers, found in dry areas of eastern and northern Africa. Some species have venom that is a medical concern.

Burrowing and rock scorpions (Opistophthalmus)

Large scorpions with strong pincers that live in burrows or under rocks. Stings are painful but usually less dangerous than those from thick-tailed species.

Small yellow or brown scorpions

Smaller scorpions that hide in walls, roofs and under objects. Small size does not mean a safe sting, so treat any scorpion with caution.

Signs of scorpions

  • A scorpion seen in a bathroom, on a wall, under a mat, or in a shoe
  • Scorpions hiding under stones, boards, bricks, roof tiles, logs and rubble in the compound
  • A sting at night or in the morning on a hand or foot when putting on shoes or reaching under an object
  • Many insects around the house, which are their prey
  • Cracks and gaps in walls, thatch or roofs that let them in
  • Glowing blue-green shapes under a UV torch at night, which is how they are found in the dark

Health risks and why it matters

Scorpion stings cause immediate pain, swelling and numbness. In children and in some species, they can cause serious symptoms. Take any child, and any adult with unusual symptoms, to a hospital quickly. Do not cut the wound or apply a tourniquet. Keep the person calm and still, and bring the scorpion if it can be done safely.

What attracts scorpions

  • Dry rural and peri-urban plots with stones, bricks, rubble and firewood
  • Gaps in walls, doors and under roofs, and thatched or loose roofs
  • Plenty of insects around the house for them to eat
  • Clothes, shoes and bedding left on the floor
  • Building sites and stores with piles of timber, tiles and stone

How to prevent scorpions

  • Shake out shoes, boots, clothes, towels and bedding before using them
  • Sleep with bedding off the floor and tuck in nets
  • Seal gaps around doors, windows and pipes and fit door sweeps
  • Clear rubble, timber, bricks and rubbish from the plot and keep firewood away from the house
  • Reduce insects by repairing lights, bins and leaks
  • Wear closed shoes and use a torch outdoors at night

Frequently asked questions about scorpions

How serious is a scorpion sting?

Most stings in healthy adults cause pain and swelling only. Stings in children, and stings with sweating, vomiting, drooling, twitching or trouble breathing, are emergencies. Go to a hospital.

Can I find scorpions at night myself?

A UV torch makes them glow, but do not handle them. Keep your distance and call us.

How do I keep scorpions out of my shoes?

Keep shoes off the floor or in a closed box, and shake them out before wearing.
