Flies are quick breeders. A housefly lays its eggs in rotting food, manure or waste. The eggs hatch into maggots within a day in warm weather, and the whole cycle from egg to adult can take roughly a week to ten days. That speed is why a restaurant, dairy, poultry house or market can go from a few flies to a swarm in days.

Different flies breed in different places, so the first job is to name the fly. Houseflies and blowflies come from waste, meat and manure. Fruit flies come from ripe or fermenting fruit and drinks. Drain flies come from the slime inside drains. Stable flies come from wet manure and bedding on livestock farms.

Types of houseflies and other flies found in Kenya

Housefly (Musca domestica)

The common indoor fly. It feeds on food and waste, and carries germs on its feet and body, so it is a food hygiene concern.

Blowflies and flesh flies

Metallic blue or green flies drawn to meat, fish, carcasses and rubbish. A sudden increase indoors can mean something has died in a roof, wall or store.

Fruit flies

Tiny flies that gather around ripe fruit, juice, sugary drinks, bins and sink drains.

Drain and moth flies

Small fuzzy flies that rest on walls near bathrooms and floor drains, and breed in the slime inside pipes and traps.

Stable flies and horn flies

Biting flies around livestock, dairies and stables. They breed in wet manure and bedding and cause stress and reduced milk and weight gain.

Signs of houseflies and other flies

  • Many flies at windows, kitchens, bins and food counters
  • Maggots in bins, drains, manure and under rotting food
  • Fly specks (small dark dots) on walls, ceilings and light fittings
  • A sudden swarm of large blue or green flies indoors
  • Small fuzzy flies near bathroom and kitchen drains
  • Cattle bunching, stamping and flicking their tails because of biting flies

Health risks and why it matters

Flies pick up germs from waste, drains and manure and put them on food, plates and surfaces. They are associated with diarrhoeal illness, especially where hygiene and waste collection are weak. In food businesses, flies lead to complaints and failed inspections. On livestock farms, biting flies reduce comfort, milk yield and weight gain.

What attracts houseflies and other flies

  • Uncovered or overflowing bins and waste areas that are not collected often enough
  • Manure, wet bedding and spilled feed on farms and in stables
  • Dirty drains, grease traps and unsealed floor drains
  • Fruit, drinks and open food left out
  • Open doors and windows without screens, and dead animals in roofs or stores

How to prevent houseflies and other flies

  • Use bins with tight lids, empty them daily and rinse them regularly
  • Clean drains and grease traps often and keep them covered
  • Remove manure and wet bedding from animal houses regularly and keep it dry and covered if it must wait
  • Fit fly screens and self-closing doors on kitchens and food areas
  • Keep fruit and sugary drinks covered or in the fridge
  • If blowflies suddenly appear, check the roof, ceiling and stores for a dead animal

Frequently asked questions about houseflies and other flies

Why are there so many flies after rain?

Warm, wet weather speeds up breeding in waste, manure and drains. A good cleaning routine and covered bins matter even more then.

Why do I suddenly have large blue or green flies indoors?

A dead animal, such as a rat in the ceiling or roof, is a common cause. Look for the source, or ask us to inspect.

Can you control flies on a dairy or poultry farm?

Yes. We combine manure and bedding advice with treatment of resting surfaces. We take farm requests from all 47 counties.

Are fly traps enough?

Traps help monitor and reduce adults, but without removing the breeding source flies keep coming.
