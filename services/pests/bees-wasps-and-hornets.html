Stinging insects build nests under roofs, in eaves, in wall cavities, in trees, on gates and inside meter boxes, and they defend them. A nest the size of a fist can hold dozens of wasps, and a bee colony in a wall can hold tens of thousands. Most stings happen when someone disturbs a nest without knowing it is there, for example while pruning, painting or cleaning gutters.

Not every stinging insect is a pest, and some, such as honey bees, are useful. African honey bees are known for defending their nest fiercely and can attack in numbers, so a wall colony needs care. Where it is possible, the better outcome is to move the bees alive with a beekeeper, and we say so when that is the case.

Types of bees, wasps and hornets found in Kenya

Paper wasps (Polistes)

Slim, long-legged wasps that build open, umbrella-shaped paper nests under eaves, beams, window frames and shelters. They sting when the nest is disturbed.

Mud daubers and potter wasps

Solitary wasps that build small mud tubes or pots on walls and ceilings. They are not aggressive and are mostly an untidiness problem.

Honey bees (Apis mellifera)

Colonies in hollow walls, roof spaces, water meter boxes and trees. African honey bees can react strongly if disturbed, and a swarm should be left alone and reported.

Carpenter bees (Xylocopa)

Large, shiny black or blue-black bees that drill round holes in dry timber to nest. They are seldom aggressive but can damage fascia boards, pergolas and beams.

Large hornet-like wasps

Big, strongly marked wasps that people often call hornets. They build large paper or ground nests and should be left to a trained person to handle.

Signs of bees, wasps and hornets

  • A steady flow of wasps or bees going in and out of one spot in a wall, roof or tree
  • A visible paper nest, mud tubes or round holes in timber
  • A buzzing or humming sound inside a wall
  • Wax or honey stains seeping through plaster or ceiling boards
  • Dead bees or wasps on the floor near a window or light
  • Stings to people or pets near the same place on more than one occasion

Health risks and why it matters

A sting is painful and for most people the pain and swelling fade within a day. Multiple stings can cause serious illness, and some people are allergic. Swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing, dizziness or collapse after a sting are emergencies, so get to a hospital right away. Children, older people, and people with allergies are the most at risk.

What attracts bees, wasps and hornets

  • Sheltered, undisturbed spots under roofs, eaves and in quiet corners
  • Small gaps in walls, roofs and fascia boards that give access to cavities
  • Flowering plants, fruit trees and water sources nearby
  • Untreated dry timber, which is attractive to carpenter bees
  • Old nests that attract new queens in the next season

How to prevent bees, wasps and hornets

  • Inspect eaves, sheds and gutters regularly, and remove small new nests early in the season by calling us, not by yourself
  • Seal gaps and holes in walls and roofs and paint or treat exposed timber
  • Keep bins covered and clean up fruit that has fallen
  • Do not leave sugary drinks open outdoors
  • If you find a nest, keep children and pets away and put up a warning
  • Wear protective clothing for any garden work around trees and eaves

Frequently asked questions about bees, wasps and hornets

Is it safe to remove a wasp nest myself?

It is risky. Wasps defend the nest, and a disturbed nest can cause many stings. Keep away and call us.

What should I do after a sting?

Wash the area, remove the sting if it is in the skin and apply a cold compress. If there is swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing, dizziness or many stings, go to a hospital immediately.

Will you kill the honey bees in my wall?

Where possible we recommend relocation by a beekeeper. If that is not possible, we discuss the options and the need to remove the comb so that it does not attract other pests.

Do carpenter bees harm my timber?

They drill round holes and tunnels in dry timber, which can weaken beams over the years. Treating and painting the wood helps stop them.
