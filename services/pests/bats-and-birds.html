Bats roost in roof spaces, ceilings, wall cavities and under eaves. Pigeons, sparrows, starlings and other birds nest in gutters, ledges, signboards, air-conditioner housings and roof voids. Both leave droppings that stain and corrode surfaces, block gutters and make noise and smell, and the droppings can be a health hazard when dry dust is breathed in.

Bats are wild animals, and many eat large numbers of insects. The responsible approach is exclusion: closing the gaps so they cannot come back, at a time when young are not present, rather than poisoning them. For birds, we use physical barriers, netting, spikes and sealing, along with cleaning of the areas that are affected.

Types of bats and birds found in Kenya

Insect-eating bats

Small bats that roost in roofs and ceilings and fly out at dusk to hunt insects. They make scratching and squeaking sounds and leave small dark droppings that crumble to powder.

Fruit bats

Larger bats that roost in trees and sometimes in roofs and feed on fruit, leaving fruit remains and droppings below the roost.

Feral pigeons

City birds that nest on ledges, balconies, signboards and roofs and leave a lot of droppings and nesting material.

Sparrows, starlings and weaver birds

Smaller birds that nest in gaps in roofs and walls, in eaves and in vents, and can block pipes and gutters.

Signs of bats and birds

  • Scratching, squeaking or fluttering in the ceiling at dusk or dawn
  • Droppings below eaves, on window ledges, balconies and in the roof space
  • Dark stains and a strong smell near a roost entry point
  • Nesting material blocking gutters, vents and air conditioner housings
  • Birds roosting in numbers on ledges and signboards
  • Bat or bird mites biting people in rooms below a roost

Health risks and why it matters

Dry bat and bird droppings can carry fungi that may cause lung illness when the dust is breathed, so do not sweep them dry. Wet them down and wear a mask, gloves and protective clothing, or ask a professional to do the cleaning. Do not handle bats with bare hands, because bats can carry rabies-related viruses. Bird droppings are slippery, acidic, and can damage paintwork and metal.

What attracts bats and birds

  • Small gaps in the roof, eaves, fascia, vents and wall tops
  • Quiet, warm roof spaces and ceilings with no disturbance
  • Ledges, signboards, balconies and sheltered spots for pigeons
  • Food waste, open bins and people feeding birds
  • Nearby trees with fruit or insects

How to prevent bats and birds

  • Seal gaps in the roof, eaves and vents with mesh, once the animals have gone
  • Fit netting, spikes or sloped covers on ledges and signboards used by pigeons
  • Cover vents and air conditioner housings with mesh
  • Keep bins covered and do not feed birds near the building
  • Clean droppings regularly and safely, and wear a mask
  • Check the roof and ceilings after storms and at the end of the rainy season

What we do and what we do not do

We do not poison, trap to kill or otherwise harm bats or birds. We inspect, find the entry points, and install proofing once the animals have left, and we clean and treat the affected areas. If young bats are present, we advise on waiting until they can fly before sealing.

How we deal with bats and birds

We inspect the roof, eaves and ledges, find the entry and roost points, and plan the exclusion with you. We seal entry points, install netting or spikes where suitable, and clean and treat the droppings area. Where there are mites in a roost, we treat the space after the animals have gone.

Because bats and birds return to old roosts, proofing is done thoroughly, and we check the work after the first rains.

Frequently asked questions about bats and birds

Will you poison the bats in my roof?

No. We use exclusion: closing the gaps after the bats have left, and not when young are present. Poison is inhumane and unsafe.

Is bat or bird dropping dust dangerous?

It can be if breathed in. Wet it before cleaning and wear a mask and gloves, or ask us to do it.

Can you stop pigeons from landing on my building?

Netting, spikes and sloped covers on ledges and signboards, together with removing food and nesting material, reduce roosting.

Do you work in shopping centres and hotels?

Yes. We take requests from all 47 counties and can plan work to avoid disturbing customers.
