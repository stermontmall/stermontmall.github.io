Carpet beetles are small, rounded beetles, about 2 to 4 mm long. Adults may be mottled, black or patterned and are often found on windowsills, because they are drawn to light and fly outdoors to feed on pollen. The larvae are the pest: they are small, hairy and brownish, and they crawl in dark places eating animal-based materials.

They feed on wool carpets, rugs, blankets, felt, silk, fur, feathers, leather, taxidermy, pet hair and the dried remains of insects. They can also infest stored dry foods such as flour, cereals and pet food. A common hidden source is a dead insect or bird nest in a roof or wall void.

Types of carpet beetles found in Kenya

Varied carpet beetle (Anthrenus verbasci)

A small, round beetle with a mottled pattern. The larvae are hairy and brown with a carrot shape and feed on wool and fur.

Black carpet beetle (Attagenus)

A shiny black or dark brown beetle. The larvae are elongate with a tail of hairs and feed on wool, fur and stored foods.

Furniture carpet beetle

A small beetle whose larvae feed on upholstery, carpets and similar materials in homes.

Signs of carpet beetles

  • Small beetles on windowsills in spring and summer
  • Shed larval skins, which look like tiny, hairy brown tubes, in corners, drawers and under furniture
  • Irregular bare patches or holes in wool carpets and rugs
  • Damage to fur, feathers, felt, leather and stored woollens
  • Skin irritation or small itchy marks from larval hairs, which are sometimes mistaken for bed bug bites
  • Larvae or skins in stored flour, cereals or pet food

Why it matters

Carpet beetles damage valuable items and carpets and can spread through the home along skirting boards and under furniture. They do not spread disease, but some people get itchy skin from larval hairs. Fixing the source, such as a dead insect nest in the roof, is key to stopping repeat outbreaks.

What attracts carpet beetles

  • Dead insects, bird nests and rodent nests in the roof, wall voids and under floors
  • Accumulated hair, lint, dust and pet fur under furniture and along skirting boards
  • Stored woollens, fur, feathers and leather that are rarely cleaned
  • Open windows that let adults in, or flowers brought indoors
  • Contaminated flour, cereals or pet food stored in open packets

How to prevent carpet beetles

  • Vacuum carpets, rugs, upholstery, skirting boards and under furniture regularly
  • Wash or dry-clean woollens and store them in tight containers
  • Store flour, cereals and pet food in sealed containers
  • Clear old bird, wasp and rodent nests from roof spaces and wall voids
  • Screen windows and keep cut flowers out of bedrooms
  • Check stored items regularly

Frequently asked questions about carpet beetles

Are carpet beetles the same as bed bugs?

No. They are different insects. Carpet beetle larvae eat fabric and food, while bed bugs feed on blood. The larval hairs can cause itchy marks, which is why people confuse them.

Where do they come from?

Often from outdoors as adults in flight, or from a hidden dead insect or old nest in a roof or wall void.

Can they damage my carpet?

Yes. Wool carpets and rugs are common targets, particularly under furniture and along edges.

Do I need to throw my items away?

Not always. Many items can be washed, dry-cleaned or sealed. We advise item by item.
