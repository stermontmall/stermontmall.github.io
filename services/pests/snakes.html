Kenya has many snake species. Most are harmless and eat rodents, frogs and insects, but some are venomous, including puff adders, cobras (including spitting cobras), mambas, boomslangs and some vipers. Snakes enter compounds because of shelter, rodents and water, not because they are looking for people.

The safest approach is to reduce the reasons for snakes to be in your compound, avoid handling them and know what to do in an emergency. If a snake is in your house or yard, keep people and pets away, keep it in sight if you can do so from a distance, and call the relevant wildlife or snake-handling service.

Types of snakes found in Kenya

Puff adder

A heavy, well-camouflaged viper that lies still on paths and in grass and often bites when stepped on. Its bite is serious and needs hospital care.

Cobras, including spitting cobras

Snakes that raise their front, can spit venom at the eyes and bite. Flush eyes with plenty of clean water and get medical help if venom is spat in the face.

Mambas and boomslangs

Fast, slender, mostly tree- or bush-dwelling snakes that are highly venomous but usually avoid people.

Harmless snakes

House snakes, rat snakes, sand snakes and pythons are among those that eat rodents and are not a danger of venomous bites. Pythons can still bite and should not be handled.

Signs of snakes

  • A snake seen in the compound, in the roof, near the drain or in a store
  • A shed snake skin, which is a papery tube in the grass or near walls
  • Many rodents, frogs or birds around the house, which attract snakes
  • Piles of rubble, long grass, stacked timber and thick bush near the walls
  • Trails or tracks in loose soil, dust or sand
  • Unexplained disturbances in poultry houses or around rabbit hutches

What to do in an emergency

After a snakebite, move the person away from the snake, keep them calm and still, remove tight rings, watches and clothing near the bite and get to a hospital quickly, ideally one known to stock antivenom. Do not cut the wound, suck the venom, apply a tourniquet or give alcohol. If venom is spat into the eyes, rinse them with a lot of clean water and seek care. A photograph of the snake from a safe distance can help the hospital, but do not delay to take one.

What attracts snakes

  • Rodents, frogs and birds that live around the house and store
  • Long grass, thick bush and rubbish heaps near buildings
  • Stacked timber, bricks, iron sheets and rubble that give shelter
  • Gaps under doors and around pipes, and holes in walls and eaves
  • Poultry houses, rabbit hutches and animal feed stores that attract rodents

How to prevent snakes

  • Control rodents in and around the house and stores, and keep feed in sealed containers
  • Keep grass short and clear bush, rubbish and rubble from the walls
  • Stack timber and bricks away from buildings and raise them off the ground
  • Seal gaps under doors and around pipes and fit door sweeps
  • Use a torch and closed shoes at night, and do not put hands in places you cannot see
  • Keep children and pets from playing in long grass and rubble heaps

Frequently asked questions about snakes

Can you remove a snake from my house?

No. We do not handle snakes. Keep away, keep pets inside and call your local wildlife authority or a trained snake handler.

Do snake repellents work?

We do not recommend relying on repellents. Removing food, shelter and gaps is the dependable approach.

What should I do if someone is bitten?

Keep them calm and still, remove tight items near the bite and get to a hospital quickly. Do not cut, suck or use a tourniquet.

Does rodent control reduce snakes?

It removes a food source and can make a compound less attractive. It is one of several steps.
