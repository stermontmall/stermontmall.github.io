Fleas are small, wingless, blood-feeding insects that jump. Adults live on the animal, but most of the flea population at any time is in the surroundings: eggs, larvae and pupae in carpets, bedding, cracks in floors and dusty soil. That is why treating only the pet, or only the house, so often fails.

In Kenya there is one more flea to know about: the jigger, or sand flea (Tunga penetrans). The female burrows into the skin, usually of the toes and feet, and is common where floors are earthen or dusty. Jiggers need medical care for people and a clean-up of the floors and surroundings.

Types of fleas and jiggers found in Kenya

Cat flea (Ctenocephalides felis)

The flea found most often on both cats and dogs in homes. It bites people on the ankles and lower legs.

Dog flea (Ctenocephalides canis)

A close relative that behaves much the same way and is treated in the same way.

Rat flea (Xenopsylla cheopis)

Lives on rodents and bites people when the rats are gone or crowded. It is the flea linked with plague, which is one more reason to treat rodents first.

Jigger (sand flea, Tunga penetrans)

Lives in dry, sandy or dusty soil and floors and burrows into feet, especially between the toes and under the nails. Infested feet become swollen, itchy and painful and can get infected.

Signs of fleas and jiggers

  • Itchy red bites on the ankles and lower legs, often in clusters
  • A pet that scratches, bites its coat or has black specks (flea dirt) in its fur
  • Tiny dark fleas jumping on white socks or on a light cloth laid on the floor
  • Flea dirt that leaves a reddish smear when wet on a tissue
  • Small black dots with a white centre on the toes or soles, or swollen painful toes, which may be jiggers
  • Fleas in bedding, sofas, carpets and the corners of rooms where the pet sleeps

Health risks and why it matters

Flea bites itch and can become infected if scratched, and some people and pets are allergic. Fleas can carry tapeworm to pets. Rat fleas have been linked to plague. Jiggers cause pain, trouble walking and secondary infection, and in children they can affect school attendance. If you see signs of jiggers on yourself or a child, see a clinic or health worker, because the flea has to be removed safely and wounds cleaned.

What attracts fleas and jiggers

  • Pets that go outside, or visiting pets and stray animals in the compound
  • Rodents in the roof, store or yard
  • Earthen, sandy or dusty floors and compounds, which suit jiggers
  • Carpets, rugs and bedding that are rarely vacuumed or washed
  • Second-hand furniture and bedding that carry eggs and pupae

How to prevent fleas and jiggers

  • Ask your veterinarian or agrovet for flea treatment for pets and follow the label for the species and weight
  • Wash pet bedding hot every week and vacuum carpets, sofas and floor edges often
  • Keep floors smooth and swept, and consider cementing earthen floors, which is a long-term answer to jiggers
  • Keep pets and children from sleeping on bare dusty floors
  • Control rodents in the roof and yard
  • Keep the compound swept and free of piles of animal bedding and dry waste

Frequently asked questions about fleas and jiggers

I treated my dog and still have fleas. Why?

Most of the flea population is in carpets, bedding and floors as eggs, larvae and pupae. The home needs treatment and cleaning at the same time as the pet.

How do I get rid of jiggers?

A health worker should remove them safely and clean the wounds. Then the floors and surroundings need cleaning and treatment so they do not return. Smooth, swept floors help a great deal.

Can fleas live on people?

They bite people but do not usually stay on them. They live on animals and in the home surroundings and jump to people from there.

Is the treatment safe for pets?

Remove pets and their bedding before treatment and bring them back when the technician says the room is ready. Pet flea treatment itself should come from your vet.
