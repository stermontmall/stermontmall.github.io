Ants are social insects, so what you see on the counter is only a few workers from a much bigger colony somewhere nearby. Spraying the ones you see usually kills only the workers, and the colony sends more. Good ant control follows the trail to the nest, and uses baits that workers carry home.

In Kenyan homes you meet a handful of types again and again. Tiny ants swarm over sugar and fat in the kitchen. Larger black ants may nest in damp timber. After heavy rain, long columns of safari ants can march through a compound at night. Each needs its own approach.

Types of ants found in Kenya

Pharaoh ants

Tiny yellow to light-brown ants that nest in walls, behind skirting and in warm spots. Spraying can scatter a colony into many new nests, so baits are used instead. They are a particular worry in hospitals and food premises.

Sugar and kitchen ants

Small ants that follow trails to sugar, syrup, fat and pet food. They usually nest outdoors and enter through cracks, windows and door frames.

Carpenter ants

Large black or dark ants that make nests in damp or rotting wood. They do not eat the wood, but the tunnels weaken it, and they leave coarse sawdust-like debris.

Safari (driver) ants

Species of Dorylus that move in long columns with large soldiers on the sides. They are nomads, so they pass through a compound in a day or two, but they can overrun poultry houses, kennels and small penned animals and give painful bites.

Signs of ants

  • Steady trails of ants along walls, windowsills and pipes to food
  • Ants gathering around sugar, honey, fat, pet bowls and rubbish bins
  • Small piles of sawdust-like debris under timber, which can mean carpenter ants
  • A column of large ants entering a house or a livestock shed at night
  • Winged ants emerging in numbers from the same spot, which shows a mature nest nearby
  • Ants inside electrical sockets, appliances or wall cavities

Why it matters

Most ants are a nuisance and a food hygiene problem rather than a danger. Pharaoh ants can spread germs in hospitals and care homes. Carpenter ants can weaken damp timber. Safari ants can overwhelm chicks, rabbits and other penned animals and bite people who stumble into a column, so farms and homes with livestock should act as soon as a column is seen.

What attracts ants

  • Food crumbs, spills, sweet drinks and unsealed sugar and pet food
  • Gaps in window frames, door frames, walls and around pipes
  • Damp or rotting timber that suits carpenter ants
  • Plants and mulch touching the walls, which give trails a bridge indoors
  • Heavy rain that floods nests and drives safari ant colonies on the move

How to prevent ants

  • Wipe counters, sweep floors and seal sugar, honey and pet food in tight containers
  • Fix leaks and replace rotten timber
  • Seal cracks around windows, doors and pipes
  • Trim plants and branches that touch the walls
  • Do not spray a pharaoh ant trail, since that can scatter the colony
  • Keep poultry houses and kennels clear and raised, and tell us quickly if a safari ant column approaches

Frequently asked questions about ants

Why do ants come back after I spray them?

Spraying kills the workers you see but not the nest. Baits that workers carry home, with trail and entry-point treatment, are more effective for most household ants.

Are safari ants dangerous?

Their bites are painful and a column can overrun small animals. They usually move on within a day or two, but if they are inside a livestock shed or house, move people and animals clear and call us.

Do carpenter ants damage my roof?

They tunnel in damp or rotting timber and can weaken it over time. We look for the moisture problem as well as the nest, because dry sound timber is much less attractive to them.

Can you treat ants in a restaurant or hospital?

Yes. We use baits and crack treatment away from food surfaces and give you a dated service report for inspections.
