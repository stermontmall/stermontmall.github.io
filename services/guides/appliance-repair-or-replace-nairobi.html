Appliance Repair or Replace? A Nairobi Guide for Fridges and Washing Machines

Common faults and what they mean

A fridge that runs but does not cool may have a failed fan, thermostat or sealed system problem. A washing machine that will not drain often has a blocked pump or hose. Microwaves that run but do not heat can have a failed component that needs a technician, never a home fix.

The repair-or-replace test

Ask what the repair costs compared with a new unit, how old the appliance is and whether parts are available. If the repair is a small part on a well-made machine, repair wins. If the repair is a major part on a very old machine, replacing may save money.

Extend the life of your appliances

Clean fridge coils and door seals, run washing machine cleaning cycles, avoid overloading and use a voltage protector if your area has power surges.

Questions to ask before deciding

How old is the appliance? What is the fault, and is the part available? What does the repair cost against a new unit of similar quality? Has the appliance had repeated repairs? If the answers point to an old appliance with a major fault and a repair price close to a new unit, replacing makes sense. If the appliance is fairly new and the fault is a small part, repair it.

Why diagnosis comes first

Many faults look alike. A washing machine that does not spin may have a belt, a motor, a control board or simply an unbalanced load. A fridge that does not cool may have a failed fan, a thermostat or a sealed system problem. Paying for the wrong part wastes money, so we diagnose first, then quote.

A simple repair or replace rule of thumb

If the repair costs more than about half the price of a comparable new appliance, or the appliance is old and has failed repeatedly, replacing it often makes more sense. A diagnosis, a written quote and the age of the appliance help you decide. Ask whether spare parts are available before you agree to a repair.

Before you call a technician

Check the plug, the socket and the breaker. Read any error code on the display and note when the fault happens. For fridges, check the door seals and the vents. For washing machines, check the drain filter. A clear description saves time and can reduce the diagnosis cost.

Questions people ask

Do you charge for diagnosis?

We confirm any diagnosis charge in your quote before the visit.

Can I fix a microwave myself?

No. Microwaves store dangerous electrical charge even when unplugged, so leave repairs to a technician.

Is it worth repairing a microwave?

Often the repair price is close to the price of a new basic microwave, so ask for the quote and compare. Never open a microwave yourself.

What spare parts do you use?

Genuine spare parts where they are available. If only a compatible part can be sourced, we tell you before fitting it.
