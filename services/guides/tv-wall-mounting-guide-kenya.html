Check the wall first

Concrete and block walls hold heavy screens with the right plugs. Drywall partitions need special fixings and sometimes a support board. Drilling blindly can hit wires or pipes, so we check before we drill.

Pick the right bracket

Fixed brackets sit flat and suit eye-level viewing. Tilting brackets help when the TV is higher. Full motion brackets let you swing the screen but need a stronger wall. Always match the bracket to the screen size and weight.

Height and hidden cables

Mount the centre of the screen close to eye level when seated. Hide cables in the wall or in trunking to keep the room neat and prevent tripping or tugging on the connectors.

Choosing a bracket by TV size

Check the screen size, the weight and the VESA pattern, which is the spacing of the mounting holes on the back of the TV, then pick a bracket rated above the weight. Large, heavy screens need a stronger bracket and a wall that can carry the load. Full motion arms put more strain on the wall than fixed brackets.

What a good mounting job includes

A good job includes checking the wall, drilling in the right places, using the correct plugs, levelling with a spirit level and testing that the bracket holds before the TV goes on. Cables should be tidy and the connectors should not be stretched. We run the TV, the decoder and the sound together before we leave.

Pick the right bracket before the technician arrives

Check your TV size, weight and VESA hole pattern, usually printed in the manual or on the back of the set. Choose a fixed bracket for a flat finish, a tilting bracket to reduce glare or a full-motion bracket for corner rooms. A bracket rated well above the weight of your TV is safer than one at its limit.

Wall type matters

Concrete and brick walls need masonry plugs. Drywall and partitions need heavy-duty anchors or a fixing into the studs. Tell us the wall type when you book. Hiding cables inside the wall or in a trunking channel gives a cleaner look, so ask about both options.

TV mounting for hotels, offices, schools, hospitals and tenders

Bulk installations need a clear scope: the number of screens, their sizes, the bracket type, cable concealment and a handover checklist. We quote per screen with labour and brackets itemised so procurement committees can compare bids.

Questions people ask

Can any TV be wall mounted?

Most modern TVs can, if they have mounting holes on the back. Check the VESA pattern and weight before buying a bracket.

How long does mounting take?

A standard TV takes about an hour. Concealed cabling and sound setup add time.

Do I need to buy the bracket first?

You can, if you know the size, the weight and the VESA pattern. Otherwise tell us the TV model and we advise on a suitable bracket.

Can I mount a TV above a cooker or a fireplace?

Heat can damage electronics, so avoid a position above a heat source such as a cooker or a working fireplace. We can suggest a safer position.
