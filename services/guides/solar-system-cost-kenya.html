Solar System Cost in Kenya: What to Budget for Home and Farm

Quick answer: A solar installation in Kenya ranges from about KSh 15,000 for small setups to KSh 250,000 and more for large homes, farms and commercial systems. Battery capacity and system size drive most of the cost.

Start with what you want to power

List your appliances, their watts and the hours you use them each day. Lights and a TV need little. A fridge runs all day, and pumps, irons and water heaters draw heavy power. This list sets the size of the panels, the inverter and the battery.

Where the money goes

Batteries are usually the largest cost, followed by panels and the inverter. Labour, mounting frames, cables, breakers and earthing complete the bill. A system built with protection and good wiring costs more upfront but avoids fires, failures and early battery damage.

Grid-tied, hybrid or off-grid

Off-grid farms need enough battery to run through the night and cloudy days. Hybrid systems use the grid and batteries together, which suits homes with frequent outages. We explain which type fits your budget before you commit.

Keeping output high

Dust on panels, loose connections and tired batteries lower output. Cleaning and a yearly check keep the system healthy.

Questions people ask

Can I start small and add later?

Often yes, if the inverter and wiring are chosen with growth in mind. Tell us your future plans and we design for them.

How long does installation take?

Small systems can be installed in a day. Larger systems take longer depending on mounting and wiring.
