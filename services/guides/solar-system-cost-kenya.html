Solar System Cost in Kenya: What to Budget for Home and Farm

Quick answer: A solar installation in Kenya ranges from about KSh 15,000 for small setups to KSh 250,000 and more for large homes, farms and commercial systems. Battery capacity and system size drive most of the cost.

Start with what you want to power

List your appliances, their watts and the hours you use them each day. Lights and a TV need little. A fridge runs all day, and pumps, irons and water heaters draw heavy power. This list sets the size of the panels, the inverter and the battery.

Where the money goes

Batteries are usually the largest cost, followed by panels and the inverter. Labour, mounting frames, cables, breakers and earthing complete the bill. A system built with protection and good wiring costs more upfront but avoids fires, failures and early battery damage.

Grid-tied, hybrid or off-grid

Off-grid farms need enough battery to run through the night and cloudy days. Hybrid systems use the grid and batteries together, which suits homes with frequent outages. We explain which type fits your budget before you commit.

Keeping output high

Dust on panels, loose connections and tired batteries lower output. Cleaning and a yearly check keep the system healthy.

A simple way to budget

Write down three numbers: the watts you need at the same time, the hours you need power, and how many hours of backup you want when the grid is off. Those numbers, with the type of system, set the price. A small system for lights, phone charging and a TV sits near the low end of our range. A home that runs a fridge, a pump and a water heater, or a farm with a borehole, sits much higher.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not buy panels first and design later. Do not choose an inverter that is too small for the pump or iron you plan to run, and do not skip earthing and surge protection. Cheap cable and loose joints cause heat and failures. Ask for the equipment brands, specifications and warranty terms in writing.

Questions to ask before you buy a solar system

Ask for a load calculation, not a guess. A good installer lists your appliances, their daily hours of use and your peak load, then sizes the panels, inverter and battery to match. Ask which brands are used, what the panel, inverter and battery warranties cover, who handles service after installation, and whether the quotation separates equipment from labour and mounting.

Solar for schools, clinics, farms and tenders

Institutions often buy solar through formal procurement. Prepare your list of loads, the roof or ground space, your daily energy use and your budget, then request an itemised quotation. A written scope with equipment, labour and commissioning shown separately lets evaluation committees compare bids line by line.

Questions people ask

Can I start small and add later?

Often yes, if the inverter and wiring are chosen with growth in mind. Tell us your future plans and we design for them.

How long does installation take?

Small systems can be installed in a day. Larger systems take longer depending on mounting and wiring.

Will solar work on cloudy or rainy days?

Panels still produce power under cloud, but less than in full sun. A properly sized battery and a hybrid design cover cloudy spells so you are not left without power.

Is solar worth it if I already have Kenya Power?

Often yes when outages are frequent or token costs are high, but it depends on your use. We compare your usage with the system cost before you decide.
