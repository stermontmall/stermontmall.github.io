Diagnose before you spray

Yellow leaves can come from disease, insects, nutrient shortage or too much water. Spraying a fungicide on an insect problem wastes money. A farm visit or clear photos help identify the cause.

Follow the label

The label gives the dose, the crops the product is meant for, the mixing method and the days to wait before harvest. Using more does not work better and can burn the crop or leave residues. Wear gloves, a mask and long clothing when mixing and spraying.

Time your spray

Spray early in the morning or late afternoon when wind is light and it is cooler. Avoid spraying before rain, which washes the product off. Rotate products with different modes of action to slow resistance.

Protective gear and safe handling

Wear gloves, a mask, goggles and long clothing when you mix and spray. Mix in a ventilated place away from water sources and never use kitchen utensils for chemicals. Keep children, animals and people without protective gear away from the area during spraying.

Keep a spray record

Write down the date, the product, the dose, the crop block and the weather. This record helps you work out waiting periods before harvest, avoid repeating the same product too often and see what worked. We record what we apply on our jobs, and you can ask us for the details.

Before you spray: scout, identify, decide

Walk the field early, look under the leaves, note which pest or disease you see and how widespread it is, and spray only when the problem needs it. A wrong diagnosis wastes money and can harm the crop, so identify the problem before you choose a product.

Safe spraying habits

Read the label for the dose, the crop, the waiting period before harvest and the re-entry time. Wear gloves, a mask, goggles and long clothing. Mix in a ventilated place, never eat or smoke while spraying, and spray when it is calm rather than windy or about to rain. Rinse equipment away from water sources and store chemicals locked and out of reach of children.

Questions people ask

Can I mix two chemicals in one tank?

Only if the labels allow it. Some mixtures damage crops or reduce effect, so ask us before mixing.

Do you supply the chemicals?

Yes. Our catalogue includes crop protection products and plant inputs, and we recommend what suits your crop.

What if it rains right after I spray?

Heavy rain soon after spraying can wash off some products. Check the label for any rain-free period, and ask us whether the spray should be repeated.

Where should I store leftover chemicals?

Keep them in the original labelled container, closed tightly, in a locked place away from food, feed, children and animals.
