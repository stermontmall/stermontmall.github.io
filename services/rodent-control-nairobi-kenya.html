Rat and mouse control for homes, warehouses and food premises

Rodent activity can contaminate stored food, damage packaging and wiring, and create recurring complaints when entry routes remain open. A lasting programme combines inspection, proofing, sanitation, monitoring and carefully selected control devices. We assess droppings, gnaw marks, runways, burrows, roof voids, loading bays, refuse areas and gaps around doors or service penetrations.

Our enquiries cover homes, apartment blocks, warehouses, shops, restaurants, hotels, food stores and other commercial premises in Nairobi and elsewhere in Kenya. For multi-site operations, share each site location and the nature of the activity so the scope can be planned accurately.

Tamper-resistant bait stations and rat blocks

Lockable or tamper-resistant bait stations help keep rodent bait contained and positioned at mapped monitoring points. They should be secured, labelled, inspected on a documented schedule and placed where children, pets, livestock and non-target wildlife cannot access them. Bait stations are not simply left unattended: records should show location, bait condition, activity and servicing date.

Rodenticide blocks and other bait formats differ by active ingredient, formulation and label restrictions. Use only products approved for the intended site and target species. Never transfer bait into food containers or leave loose bait where people, animals or food could contact it.

Tracking powder: strict site and safety controls

Tracking powders can present inhalation and contamination risks and are not appropriate for every site. They must only be considered by trained pest-management personnel where the product label expressly permits the use and placement. They must not be used on food-contact surfaces, in occupied living areas without label authorisation, or where dust can be disturbed and carried into food, feed or air-handling systems. Safer alternatives such as proofing, mechanical traps and contained bait stations may be preferable.

Warehouse and food-business rodent programmes

For warehouses and food operations, the service plan can include a site map of monitoring points, proofing priorities, housekeeping recommendations, reporting intervals and escalation steps when fresh activity is detected. Keep baiting and trapping records available for internal audits and food-safety inspections. Any fumigation or pesticide use must be separately assessed and follow the relevant product label and site controls.
