What a typical farm visit covers

We walk the rows or the greenhouse, look at the leaves, stems, roots and soil, and ask about the planting date, recent rain and what you have already sprayed. That history often explains the problem as much as the plant does.

Then we write down what we found, what we recommend and what it costs. You can take the advice only, or ask us to supply the products and apply them.

Safe handling and storage on the farm

Chemicals belong in their original labelled containers, in a locked place away from food, feed, children and animals. Wear gloves, a mask, goggles and long clothing when mixing and spraying, and wash your hands and clothes afterwards.

Do not rinse sprayers or empty containers in rivers, dams or near boreholes. Follow the label for how to dispose of empties, and respect the waiting period before harvest so produce is safe to sell and eat.

How much does farm spraying cost in Kenya?

Do you follow the product label?

Yes. Doses, mixing and waiting periods before harvest come from the manufacturer label. We never guess rates.

Can you diagnose a crop problem from photos?

Photos help us give a first opinion over WhatsApp, but a farm visit gives a reliable diagnosis because many problems look alike.

Do you also advise on livestock?

Yes. We advise on animal health products, disinfection and housing for dairy, poultry and other livestock.

How soon can I harvest after spraying?

The waiting period is printed on the product label and differs by product and crop. We write it into your spray schedule so you know the earliest safe harvest date.

Yes. Tell us the size of the plot and the crop. Our range starts at KSh 4,500 and we confirm the exact figure after the visit.
