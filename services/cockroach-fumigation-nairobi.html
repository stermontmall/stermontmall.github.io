Cockroach gel bait vs residual spray: which is suitable?

Targeted gel baiting

Gel bait is placed in small, targeted points near cockroach harbourages and travel routes. It can be useful in kitchens and sensitive operating areas where broad surface spraying is undesirable. Bait must be placed where children, pets, food-contact surfaces and routine cleaning will not disturb it. Do not apply sprays over bait placements unless the product label and treatment plan specifically allow it.

Residual surface treatment

Residual sprays may be appropriate for selected cracks, crevices, void edges and non-food-contact surfaces when the label permits that site. They require careful preparation, protection of food and utensils, and adherence to drying and re-entry instructions. A residual spray is not a substitute for correcting leaks, waste handling or sanitation gaps.

The best approach depends on cockroach species, infestation pressure, building design and label directions. A combined integrated pest-management plan may include monitoring, proofing, cleaning and targeted application rather than repeated blanket spraying.

Restaurant and hotel treatment planning

Food businesses need treatment timing that protects customers and staff while maintaining hygiene controls. We discuss kitchen shut-down windows, food and utensil protection, drains, delivery zones, waste rooms and follow-up monitoring. Hotel and apartment programmes can coordinate inspections by room, floor or unit to reduce reinfestation from untreated adjoining areas.
