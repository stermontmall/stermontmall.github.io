Buying Veterinary Products in Kenya: A Responsible Guide

Kenya buyer guidance · Updated 18 November 2026

Veterinary products are not interchangeable: the right choice depends on animal species, age, weight, condition and diagnosis. Medicines and vaccines should be selected with a veterinary professional, while supplements and routine care products still need clear instructions and suitable storage. Do not treat an online description as a substitute for a veterinary diagnosis or prescription where one is required.

Confirm the animal and purpose

Identify the species, age group, approximate weight and health concern. A product suitable for poultry may not be suitable for cattle, goats, sheep, dogs or cats. Seek veterinary advice to confirm the diagnosis and whether a medicine, vaccine, supplement or husbandry change is appropriate.

Read the label and product details

Check active ingredient, concentration, dosage form, species, route, expiry date, storage requirements, warnings and withdrawal period for food-producing animals. Confirm any prescription or professional-use requirement before ordering. Never calculate a dose from another animal’s treatment.

Check cold-chain and storage needs

Some vaccines and biological products require a controlled cold chain. Confirm storage and delivery arrangements before purchase and do not use products stored outside their required conditions. Check packaging integrity and expiry information on receipt.

Compare pack sizes and keep treatment records

Compare exact concentration, volume, number of doses and intended species. A cheaper pack may contain a different strength or fewer doses. For food-producing animals, follow label and veterinary instructions for withdrawal periods and record product, batch, date and treatment.

Before you order: buyer checklist

  • Species, age, weight and condition identified
  • Veterinary advice obtained where needed
  • Concentration, route, expiry and withdrawal checked
  • Storage and cold-chain requirements confirmed
  • Batch and treatment records planned
