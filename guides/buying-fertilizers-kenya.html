How to Choose and Buy Fertilizers in Kenya

Kenya buyer guidance · Updated 18 November 2026

Choosing fertilizer starts with the crop and condition of the soil, not a brand name. Different crops and growth stages need different nutrient balances, while soil pH and existing nutrient levels affect what is useful. Use soil-test results or advice from a qualified agricultural extension professional whenever possible.

Start with soil and crop requirements

Identify the crop, field history, planting stage and expected yield. Soil testing can reveal pH and nutrient gaps, reducing guesswork and unnecessary spending. Ask an extension officer or agronomist to interpret results and recommend a suitable nutrient programme.

Understand the grade on the bag

Read the guaranteed analysis and N-P-K grade. Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium have different roles, and secondary or micronutrients may also matter. Compare stated nutrient content rather than judging by bag colour or marketing language. Confirm whether the product is a compound fertilizer, straight fertilizer, top-dressing product or soil amendment.

Match fertilizer to application stage

Basal fertilizers, top-dressing fertilizers and specialty products are not interchangeable. Check crop and timing instructions on the product information. Follow recommended placement and application rates; placing fertilizer too close to seed or plant roots can cause damage.

Compare pack sizes and storage

Check whether the bag is 1 kg, 5 kg, 25 kg or 50 kg and compare cost per kilogram and nutrient content. Calculate the amount needed for your acreage before selecting a pack. Keep fertilizer dry and protected from contamination, children and animals.

Before you order: buyer checklist

  • Crop, field and growth stage identified
  • Soil test or qualified recommendation considered
  • N-P-K grade and guaranteed analysis checked
  • Basal or top-dressing use confirmed
  • Bag weight, cost per kg and storage compared
