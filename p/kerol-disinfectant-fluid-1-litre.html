What the rideal-walker coefficient means

The Rideal-Walker coefficient is a traditional way of comparing the germ-killing strength of a disinfectant with phenol, which is given a value of 1. A coefficient of 18 to 20 means the fluid is many times stronger than phenol when tested under standard laboratory conditions, which is why it is described as a powerful disinfectant. Real results depend on dilution, contact time and how clean the surface is.

Where it is used

  • Livestock houses: cattle sheds, milking parlours, goat and sheep pens and pig sties
  • Poultry houses: brooders, layer and broiler houses, and equipment
  • Domestic use: floors, drains, toilets, bins and other places that need disinfecting

Why disinfection matters

Germs build up where animals live and where waste collects. Regular cleaning and disinfection lowers the risk of disease spreading in a herd or flock, protects young animals, and keeps houses fresher and smell-free. In the home, it helps keep drains, bathrooms and bins clean.

How to use it

Dilute with water as directed on the label, using the correct amount for the job. Always clean first: remove manure, bedding, feed and dirt, wash with water and allow to drain, because disinfectants work best on clean surfaces. Then apply the diluted fluid by spraying, mopping or scrubbing, and give it enough contact time as stated on the label before rinsing, if required. Let houses dry and rest before animals return, following the label.

Good practice

  • Use clean water for dilution
  • Do not mix with other chemicals or cleaners
  • Disinfect feeders and drinkers only as directed on the label, and rinse them well
  • Repeat between batches of animals

Safety

Read the label before use. Wear gloves, goggles and protective clothing, and avoid contact with eyes and skin. Keep away from children, pets, food and drinking water. Do not let large amounts reach streams or ponds.

Storage

Store in the original closed bottle in a cool, dry, locked place away from food and feed.
