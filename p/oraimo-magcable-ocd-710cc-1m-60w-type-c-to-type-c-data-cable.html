Buying guide & product checks

Check the charging port on your device and the connector on your charger before ordering. A Type-C cable cannot be plugged directly into a Lightning or Micro-USB port without an appropriate compatible adapter.

Product details

The Type-C connector still needs to be inserted into the device's charging port for a wired connection. The product is also promoted for stain and wear resistance, high-speed data transfer and broad compatibility with USB-C devices. Data-transfer performance depends on the connected devices and the cable's supported data standard.

Do not assume that every Type-C cable supports the same transfer speed or video output. When disconnecting, hold the connector body rather than pulling the cable itself. Do not force a connector into a port if it does not fit smoothly.

Some high-performance laptops require a higher-wattage charger and may charge slowly or not at all with a 60W supply. Check the device manufacturer's recommended adapter rating before relying on this cable for computer charging.

Safety information

To use the cable, connect one end to a USB-C charger or power bank and the other to a compatible USB-C device. Insert the connectors straight and avoid twisting them under pressure. Avoid sharp bends, tight knots, crushing under furniture or pulling the cable around corners.

Keep it away from hot surfaces and liquids. Coil it loosely for storage and avoid wrapping it tightly around a charger in a way that stresses the connector ends. If the outer insulation splits, wires become visible or a connector is loose, stop using the cable.

Care, cleaning & storage

If charging or data transfer becomes intermittent, inspect the cable ends and device ports for lint, moisture or physical damage. Good cable care can extend its useful life. Stop charging if a connector becomes unusually hot or if there is visible damage.
