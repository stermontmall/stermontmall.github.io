Decide whether a dry iron suits your clothes-care routine

The ElectroMate listing identifies a 1,000 W dry iron with a non-stick soleplate and five temperature settings. Unlike a steam iron, this model has no water tank or steam function, so choose it if dry ironing meets your routine rather than expecting steam features. The listing also mentions an indicator light and a one-year warranty.

Check the care label on each garment before selecting a temperature. Different fabrics tolerate different levels of heat, and printed, coated or delicate materials may need special care. The product description mentions settings for fabrics such as cotton and linen, but it does not give a full temperature chart. Use the markings on the actual iron and its manual rather than guessing a setting for an unfamiliar fabric.

  • Confirm the five-setting control dial and indicator light on the supplied unit.
  • Check the plug, cable condition, warranty terms and included documentation.
  • Use a stable ironing board with enough space to place the iron safely between garments.

Care for the soleplate and store the iron safely

Move the iron steadily and avoid dragging it across zips, buttons or other hard fittings that could scratch the non-stick coating. If a garment needs a lower setting, allow the iron to cool as directed before changing to it. Follow the garment label and the manufacturer's instructions whenever the two appear to differ.

Keep the hot soleplate away from skin, children, pets and fabrics that should not be heated. Do not leave the iron connected to power unattended. When you finish, switch it off, unplug it and place it in its designated safe position while it cools. Clean the soleplate only with the method recommended in the manual; do not immerse the appliance in water. Store it only after it has cooled fully, with the cable arranged so it is not sharply bent.
