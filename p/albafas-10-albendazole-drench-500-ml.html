Why worm control matters

Internal parasites quietly drain animals. Heavily infected cattle, sheep, goats and camels grow slowly, lose condition, produce less milk and meat, and become more open to other diseases. Young animals are often the most affected. Regular, well-planned deworming protects growth and productivity and can save the cost of treating sick animals later.

How to use it

Albafas is given by mouth as a drench. The dose depends on the animal species and body weight, so follow the dose on the label and use an accurate drenching gun. Weigh or estimate weight carefully, and dose the heaviest animal in a group at its own weight to avoid under-dosing. Under-dosing is one of the main causes of worms becoming resistant to dewormers. If you are not sure about the dose, the correct time to treat, or the withdrawal period for milk and meat, ask your vet or animal health provider.

Good practice

  • Deworm on a programme agreed with your vet, rather than only when animals look sick
  • Keep clean water and good pasture management to reduce re-infection
  • Rotate dewormers from different groups when advised, to help slow resistance
  • Keep records of dates, products and doses

Safety and storage

For veterinary use. Follow the label dose and withdrawal periods. Wear gloves when handling and wash your hands afterwards. Keep out of reach of children. Store the bottle tightly closed in a cool, dry place away from direct sun and food. Do not let rinse water reach streams or ponds.
