About Delta Insect Monitoring Trap – Reusable

The Delta Insect Monitoring Trap is a reusable triangular trap housing designed for monitoring selected flying moth pests and other target insects in greenhouses, crop fields and horticultural plots. Its folded delta shape provides a sheltered space for a replaceable sticky insert, while a compatible species-specific pheromone lure can attract the target insect into the adhesive surface. This is a monitoring device; the correct lure and liner must be selected for the pest being monitored.

How a Delta Trap works

A delta trap uses a triangular cover with an adhesive liner positioned at the base. When used with a suitable pheromone dispenser, the lure releases a species-specific scent that attracts target insects, often male moths, towards the trap. Insects entering the housing become stuck on the adhesive liner and can be inspected, counted and identified. Koppert's Deltatrap instructions describe folding the trap into a triangle, placing the sticky insert with the adhesive facing upwards and positioning the compatible Pherodis capsule on the liner. Do not assume that every pheromone lure works with every pest species.

Select the correct pheromone

The lure is essential for species-specific monitoring. Different moth pests respond to different pheromones, and the wrong lure may produce little or misleading information. Before ordering a dispenser, identify the target pest and confirm the correct lure with the supplier or crop adviser. Keep lure packages sealed until use and avoid cross-contamination between different pheromone types. Do not touch the lure or the inside of its packaging unnecessarily. Follow the lure's storage temperature, replacement interval and handling instructions.

Where to position the Trap

Delta traps may be used in greenhouses, crop fields, orchards and horticultural production areas where moth activity needs to be monitored. Koppert recommends hanging the trap approximately 10cm above the crop in a location where it can be inspected easily, such as a greenhouse aisle. Distribute traps evenly enough to reduce interference between lures. The exact number of traps depends on the target pest and monitoring protocol. Koppert provides different recommendations for moth monitoring and mealybug monitoring; follow the guidance for the exact species rather than applying a generic rate.

Preparation and installation

Fold the trap into its triangular shape and secure the base flaps according to the product instructions. Place a clean sticky insert at the bottom with the adhesive surface facing upwards. Position the correct pheromone dispenser as directed. Hang the trap securely so it does not touch plants, irrigation equipment or walkways. Keep it accessible for inspection and replacement. If the trap is damaged, distorted or cannot hold the liner securely, replace the housing or relevant component.

Inspection and record keeping

Inspect traps at regular intervals and record the location, crop, lure type, installation date and number of insects caught. Replace the sticky insert when it is largely covered with insects, dust or debris. If a moth is captured, identify it where possible rather than assuming every catch is the target pest. A rising catch may indicate a change in pest pressure and should prompt closer crop scouting. Trap counts should be interpreted alongside plant inspections and agronomic advice.

Use with integrated pest management

Delta traps support early detection and population monitoring. They can help growers time crop inspections and assess whether pest activity is increasing or decreasing. They do not replace crop sanitation, removal of infested plant material, weed management, biological control or other suitable measures. If a pesticide is considered, confirm that it is registered and labelled for the crop and target pest, and follow application directions and pre-harvest intervals.
