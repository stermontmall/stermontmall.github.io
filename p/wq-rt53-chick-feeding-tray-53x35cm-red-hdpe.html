Care, cleaning & storage

Its open shape makes it useful for early feeding management, but it does not replace the need to provide enough feeder space for the whole flock. The stated 20-to-35-chick capacity should be treated as a supplier guide rather than a strict stocking rule. Setting up the tray Place the tray on a level, dry section of the brooder where chicks can approach from more than one side.

Keep it away from drinkers because spilled water can wet feed and bedding. Cleaning and maintenance Remove leftover feed and loose litter before washing. Clean the tray using a poultry-equipment cleaning method suitable for plastic, rinse it thoroughly and allow it to dry before adding fresh feed.

Inspect the base and edges for cracks, warping or sharp damage. Remove a damaged tray from service because rough edges can injure birds and cracks can trap dirt. The supplier notes that trays can be stacked for storage.

Stack them only when empty and dry, and keep them in a clean area away from rodents, moisture, chemicals and direct contamination. Use a starter ration appropriate to the bird type and age, following the feed manufacturer's instructions. Provide clean drinking water continuously and make sure drinkers are accessible without wetting the feed area.

Maintain suitable brooding temperature, ventilation and dry litter. Check the flock regularly for uneven access, spilled feed, wet bedding or signs that some chicks are not eating. Remove spilled feed that could attract rodents or insects, and store feed in sealed, dry containers.

For larger flocks, distribute feeding points so birds do not have to travel far or compete at a single location. The tray should be positioned where the keeper can inspect it easily and clean around it. Adjust the number of trays as the flock grows or when birds are moved to another pen.

Safety information

Avoid placing it directly beneath perches or in a spot where droppings can fall into the feed. Spread a thin layer of suitable starter feed rather than filling the tray deeply. Avoid placing heavy objects on top if they could deform the plastic.

Keep feeding equipment separate from sprayers, pesticide containers and chemical-mixing tools.
