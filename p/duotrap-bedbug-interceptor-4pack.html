About DuoTrap Bed Bug Interceptor Cups – 4 Pack

Product overview

DuoTrap bed bug interceptor cups are passive monitoring devices designed to sit beneath suitable bed or furniture legs and help detect crawling insects moving between the floor and the furniture. The recessed cup arrangement is intended to create a difficult route out once an insect enters the inner well. Interceptors can help a homeowner, hotel team or pest technician monitor bed bug activity over time, but they are not a pesticide and do not treat an infestation by themselves. This listing is for a four-pack of interceptor cups. Interceptors are most useful when the furniture is configured so that every relevant leg rests securely inside a cup and the bed does not touch nearby walls, curtains or other furniture that could create an alternative route. Before installation, inspect the bed frame and surrounding area, move the bed into a suitable position and check that the floor is stable. If the bed has a central support, unusual leg shape or wheels, confirm that the cup can support it safely. Do not use an interceptor that rocks, cracks or cannot hold the furniture securely. The purpose of the cup is monitoring. Insects attempting to climb onto or leave the bed may encounter the interceptor surface, allowing the user to inspect for activity. Check the cups regularly and record the date, room and any insects found. A captured insect should be identified carefully; other crawling insects may also enter a monitor. If a bed bug is suspected, retain a sample or clear photograph for a qualified identification rather than relying only on an assumption based on bites or marks. Prepare the installation area by vacuuming and cleaning the floor around the bed legs. Place a cup beneath each suitable leg and ensure the furniture weight is supported in the centre of the cup as directed by the product design. Do not place the cup on a thick rug or uneven surface if that prevents stable contact. Keep bedding, bed skirts and electrical cables from touching the floor in a way that bypasses the monitor. If the bed is pushed against a wall, insects may use the wall as an alternative route and the monitoring result may be misleading. Inspect the cups on a planned schedule, such as during routine room checks or pest-management visits. Look for insects, dust, debris, cracks and any movement of the furniture that could dislodge the cups. Clean or replace the device according to its material and manufacturer guidance. Avoid applying oil, pesticide or powder to the interceptor unless the product instructions specifically permit it; altering the surface can affect the way insects move through the device and can compromise monitoring. Interceptors work best as part of a wider bed bug inspection. Check mattress seams, the bed base, headboard fixings, screw holes, bedside furniture, upholstered chairs, skirting and nearby cracks. Mattress encasements can simplify inspection of the mattress exterior, while laundering and appropriate treatment may be needed for affected items. Do not assume that an empty interceptor proves there are no bed bugs, because insects may be hiding elsewhere or using another route into the bed. For accommodation providers, record each interceptor's room and position so that catches can be compared over time. If a room changes occupancy, keep the monitor in place and check it during the room-turnover process. When a positive catch is found, follow the property's reporting and pest-management procedure. Avoid moving potentially infested furniture into another room without inspection. In multi-unit buildings, consider adjacent rooms and shared service routes when planning follow-up checks. The four-pack format is intended for a bed or other furniture arrangement with up to four suitable legs, but the actual number required depends on the furniture design. Confirm cup dimensions, opening size, weight-bearing suitability and compatibility with the leg shape before purchase. A bed with more than four support points may need additional monitors or a different monitoring arrangement. Do not use the cups as furniture risers unless the manufacturer states that they are designed for that load. For Kenyan homes, furnished rentals, hotels, guest houses, hostels and short-stay accommodation, bed bug interceptors can support routine room inspection and early detection. They are especially useful when paired with staff training, mattress inspection, clear guest reporting and a documented follow-up plan. Keep monitors clean and accessible, and avoid placing them where children or pets may disturb them. If infestation is confirmed, arrange a suitable treatment programme rather than relying on passive monitoring alone. The DuoTrap four-pack is listed at KSh 1,200. Confirm the exact cup count, dimensions, material and weight suitability before checkout. Mattress encasements, bed frames, chemical treatments and professional services are not included. These devices monitor movement; they do not kill every bed bug in a room or guarantee a pest-free bed.
