CROP AND DISEASE CONTEXT Downy mildew and blight diseases can cause significant damage to susceptible horticultural crops. Disease development is influenced by weather, humidity, crop density, irrigation practices, planting material and field sanitation. Regular scouting helps identify early symptoms and determine whether a fungicide intervention is appropriate. Blight symptoms can overlap with other disorders, so seek a reliable diagnosis before treatment. The supplier describes Skipper 72 WP for downy mildew on roses and early and late blight on tomatoes; use beyond those stated uses must be supported by the actual Kenyan label. INTEGRATED DISEASE MANAGEMENT A strong disease-management programme does not depend on one fungicide. Use clean planting material, resistant or tolerant varieties where available, crop rotation, adequate spacing, balanced nutrition, drainage and irrigation practices that avoid unnecessary leaf wetness. Remove diseased plant material where recommended and manage crop residues appropriately. Monitor weather and crop conditions, and keep field records of disease pressure and treatments. These steps help reduce infection pressure and support more responsible pesticide use. MIXING AND SPRAY APPLICATION Follow the product label for the exact mixing rate, water volume, spray interval, maximum number of applications and crop-specific instructions. Do not use a generic rate taken from a different brand, formulation or pack. Use a calibrated sprayer and prepare only the amount needed for the labelled treatment. Ensure appropriate coverage without creating excessive runoff or spray drift. Do not apply in windy conditions or when rain is likely to compromise the treatment, unless the label provides specific directions. Never increase the dose or apply more frequently than permitted. Wear the protective equipment specified by the label when opening, measuring, mixing and spraying. Avoid breathing powder during handling. Add and mix the product only in the sequence stated on the label, and keep bystanders away from the mixing area. Clean equipment safely after use and prevent wash water from reaching drains, wells, streams or ponds. RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT Repeated use of fungicides with the same modes of action can contribute to reduced sensitivity in pathogen populations. Follow the label's resistance-management instructions and relevant FRAC recommendations. Alternate fungicide groups when appropriate, and do not assume that products with different brand names necessarily have different modes of action. Integrate cultural and physical controls, and record active ingredients rather than only trade names. If disease control is poor, review diagnosis, spray coverage, timing, weather and resistance risk with a qualified agronomist rather than automatically increasing the dose. WORKER AND CONSUMER PROTECTION Read the full label before use. Observe all restrictions for workers, harvesters, consumers, livestock and nearby residents. Follow the stated re-entry period and pre-harvest interval exactly. Do not harvest earlier than the label permits. Keep children and animals away from the product and treated areas. Do not eat, drink or smoke while handling pesticides. Wash hands and exposed skin after handling, and launder contaminated clothing separately as directed. If the product enters the eyes, is swallowed or causes suspected poisoning, follow the label's first-aid directions and seek prompt medical help. ENVIRONMENTAL PRECAUTIONS Prevent the product and spray mixture from contaminating surface water, groundwater, drains, irrigation channels, food and animal feed. Follow label restrictions that protect pollinators and beneficial organisms. Avoid spraying flowering crops when prohibited by the label. Prevent drift onto neighbouring properties and sensitive areas. Dispose of leftover spray, rinsate and empty packaging exactly as instructed on the label and under applicable Kenyan requirements.
