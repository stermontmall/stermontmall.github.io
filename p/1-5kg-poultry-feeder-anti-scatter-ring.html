WHY ANTI-SCATTER MATTERS

Feed is the largest running cost in poultry keeping. Birds, especially chickens, tend to scratch and flick feed out of open troughs, where it is lost in bedding or soil and can attract rodents and wild birds. A ring around the tray makes it harder for birds to push feed over the edge and helps keep more of it available to eat.

Less waste means lower feed costs and a cleaner house.

SETTING UP THE FEEDER

Fill the feeder through the removable top and close it firmly. Place it on a clean, level, dry surface, or raise it slightly on a block so that the tray is at about the height of the birds' backs. Having the tray at back height helps keep litter out of the feed and reduces waste.

Keep the feeder out of the rain and away from the drinker so that feed does not get wet. Put enough feeders in the house that all birds can eat without crowding, especially younger birds.

CLEANING AND HYGIENE

Empty and clean the feeder regularly. Wash it with clean water and a mild detergent, rinse well and let it dry completely before refilling. Damp or old feed can grow mould, which is harmful to birds.

Removing the top makes cleaning easier. Check for cracks and replace any damaged feeder.

WHAT IT SUITS

The 1.5 kg size suits small flocks, chicks and growers, ducks and turkeys in small numbers, and as an extra feeder in a larger house. For bigger flocks you will need several feeders or a larger model. Feeding space per bird depends on the type and age of the birds, so ask your poultry advisor or feed supplier for guidance.

PLANNING YOUR SETUP

Count your birds and decide how many feeders the house needs. As a rule of thumb, it is better to have slightly more feeding places than you think you need, because shy or younger birds are pushed away from a single feeder by stronger ones. Spread feeders around the house, keep them off wet ground and refill them on a regular schedule so the birds always have clean, dry feed.

Ask your feed supplier about daily feed amounts for your breed and age.
