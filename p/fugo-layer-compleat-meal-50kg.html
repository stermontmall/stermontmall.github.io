About Fugo Layer Compleat Meal 50kg

Product overview

Fugo Layer Compleat Meal 50kg is a formulated poultry feed for laying hens from approximately 18 weeks of age and throughout the laying period. It is designed to provide a complete daily meal for birds that have reached point of lay and are producing eggs. A consistent layer ration helps supply the energy, protein, minerals and amino acids that hens need for egg formation, body maintenance and continued activity. The 50kg bag is suited to farms that feed a regular flock and want to manage feed purchasing in practical bulk quantities. A laying hen needs a balanced ration because egg production places continuing demands on the bird. Nutrients are used for maintenance, feather condition, body reserves, egg white and yolk formation, and the mineral structure of the shell. If feed quality or intake is inconsistent, production and egg quality can be affected. A formulated layer meal is intended to simplify the daily ration by providing a planned blend of ingredients rather than requiring the farmer to mix separate ingredients without a reliable formulation. The product information describes Fugo Layer Compleat Meal as containing protein, energy, minerals and amino acids to support laying performance and general wellbeing. Balanced minerals are particularly important in a layer ration because hens need calcium and phosphorus for normal skeletal function and eggshell formation. The exact nutrient percentages should be confirmed from the current bag label, as formulations and guaranteed analyses can change. Use the label as the reference when comparing feeds or discussing a ration with a poultry nutrition adviser. The supplier's feeding guidance is to offer the meal dry and ad libitum to birds from 18 weeks of age and during the laying period. The stated average intake is about 140 grams per laying hen per day. Actual consumption can vary with breed, body size, temperature, production level, feeder design and the quality of the water supply. Monitor the flock's daily feed disappearance, egg output, body condition and behaviour. Do not assume that every flock will consume exactly the same amount. Introduce a new feed gradually where possible. Sudden changes in ingredients, texture or feeding schedule can affect intake. If birds are being moved from a grower ration to a layer ration, follow the supplier's recommended transition and the age guidance on the bag. Avoid feeding a layer meal to young chicks as a substitute for starter or grower feed. The nutrient balance for laying birds differs from the requirements of young growing pullets, and the correct feed should match the bird's stage. Provide clean water at all times. Hens cannot maintain normal feed intake without dependable access to water, especially in hot weather. Check drinkers daily for blockages, leaks and contamination. Keep the feeding area dry and clean, and remove wet or mouldy feed. Feed should be stored in a cool, dry place, away from rain, direct sunlight, rodents, birds and household chemicals. Close the bag after each use and avoid leaving feed exposed overnight. Use clean, suitable feeders that allow birds to eat without excessive spillage. Adjust feeder height as the flock grows so that birds can reach the feed comfortably while reducing scratching and contamination. Provide enough feeder space to prevent dominant hens from excluding smaller birds. Observe the flock during feeding periods; uneven access can create differences in body condition and production. Keep the floor litter dry and remove feed that has fallen into wet areas. Good layer management combines feed with lighting, ventilation, housing, biosecurity and routine health care. Sudden drops in egg production may be related to disease, parasites, heat stress, water interruption, poor lighting, moulting or changes in feed intake. Do not respond to every production drop by increasing feed. First inspect the birds and the environment, then seek veterinary or extension advice where the cause is not clear. Keep a record of daily egg numbers, feed used and any management changes. The 50kg bag is useful for farms with a regular laying flock. Estimate weekly feed requirements from the number of hens and observed intake so that you can plan purchases without keeping excessive stock for long periods. For a small backyard flock, consider whether a smaller bag would be easier to store and use while the feed remains fresh. For a larger unit, maintain a clean feed store and use stock rotation so older bags are used first. Inspect the packaging before opening. The bag should be dry and intact, with the product name, batch details and feeding guidance readable. Do not use feed that smells musty, appears mouldy or has been contaminated by water, pests or chemicals. If there is a quality concern, keep the batch details and contact the supplier rather than mixing questionable feed with a fresh bag. Proper storage and stock rotation help protect the value of the ration. Fugo Layer Compleat Meal 50kg is intended for laying hens from the recommended stage and should be fed according to the current label. Pair it with clean water, appropriate housing, good biosecurity and regular flock observation. For the best results, keep feeding consistent, track egg production and body condition, and obtain advice when a flock has unusual nutritional needs. A suitable layer ration supports the production programme, but it works best as part of a complete and well-managed poultry system.
