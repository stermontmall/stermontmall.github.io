WHY SELECT AN 8-LITRE MANUAL SPRAYER?

A smaller tank can be easier to carry and position for short, targeted jobs. It may be appropriate when treating a few garden beds, individual plants, a greenhouse section or selected outdoor surfaces. It is also useful where a full 16- or 20-litre knapsack would add unnecessary weight. The trade-off is more frequent refilling if the treatment area is large. Consider the job size and the operator's ability before choosing. An 8-litre capacity is not a measure of coverage by itself: the liquid volume required depends on the treatment label, application rate, nozzle and target.

HOW THE MANUAL PRESSURE SYSTEM WORKS

The AC-8L uses a hand-operated pump to build pressure inside the tank. When the trigger is operated, liquid travels through the hose and lance to the nozzle. The operator may need to pump periodically to maintain the working pressure specified by the equipment instructions. Do not over-pressurise the tank or attempt to force the pump if it becomes difficult to operate. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for filling, pumping, spraying, releasing pressure and opening the cap. Always depressurise the sprayer using the prescribed method before inspection, cleaning or storage.

TANK MATERIAL AND CHEMICAL COMPATIBILITY

The supplier identifies the tank as high-density polyethylene. HDPE is widely used for liquid containers, but this does not mean every pesticide, solvent, concentrate or cleaning chemical is compatible with the tank, seals, hose and nozzle. Check the sprayer manual and the product label before mixing. If compatibility is not clearly stated, ask the supplier or manufacturer. Do not use the sprayer for drinking water, food, beverages or household liquids. Keep the tank cap and seals clean, and stop using the sprayer if the tank is cracked, distorted or leaking.

HOSE, LANCE AND ADJUSTABLE NOZZLE

The listing specifies an approximately 1.2-metre hose and an adjustable nozzle on the spray lance. The hose provides the connection between the tank and the lance, while the nozzle shapes the output. A fine mist may be suitable for some label-approved applications, while a more concentrated stream may be useful for directed treatment where permitted. Do not assume that a particular pattern is suitable without checking the chemical label. Inspect the hose for splits and the nozzle for blockages. Do not clear a nozzle by blowing through it with your mouth or using a sharp pin that could damage the opening.

POSSIBLE APPLICATIONS

The AC-8L may be considered for garden care, greenhouse maintenance and selected outdoor liquid treatments. It can be used for pest-management applications only when the chosen product is labelled for the specific pest, surface and application method. The sprayer itself is not a pesticide or disinfectant, and its small tank does not make a chemical safer. Agricultural products are not automatically approved for indoor use, food areas, animal housing or public places. Check product registration and label instructions relevant to Kenya, and use a qualified operator where the task requires professional pest-control services.

PLANNING A SMALL TREATMENT

Identify the target and read the product label before preparing any mixture. Calculate the required dilution and quantity using the label and a suitable measuring method. Prepare only the amount needed for the planned area. Protect food, utensils, animal feed, water sources and sensitive plants. Avoid spraying in strong wind or when runoff could carry product into drains or waterways. Keep children, pets and other people away from the treatment area for the period specified on the label. A clean-water test before mixing helps identify leaks and confirms the spray pattern.

SAFE HANDLING

Wear the protective equipment specified by the chemical label. Depending on the product, this may include chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection, protective clothing, boots and respiratory protection. Do not eat, drink or smoke while mixing or spraying. Keep the lance directed away from people and animals. Never point a pressurised nozzle at skin. Release pressure before opening the tank or disconnecting fittings. Store chemicals in their original labelled containers and keep them separate from food, water and household items. Do not leave a filled sprayer unattended where children or animals can reach it.

CLEANING AND

Storage & handling

After application, follow the label and equipment instructions for handling leftover mixture, rinsing and disposal. Do not pour pesticide residue or rinse water into drains, streams, ponds, wells or storm-water channels. Clean the tank, pump, hose, lance and nozzle using the approved procedure. If changing between products, follow the required decontamination instructions to avoid unwanted chemical mixing. Empty, clean and dry the sprayer before storage. Keep it upright in a secure, cool and dry location away from direct sunlight and children.

ROUTINE INSPECTION AND CARE

Before each use, inspect the tank, cap, shoulder strap, hose, lance, nozzle, pump handle and seals. Check that all connections are secure and that the nozzle adjustment moves as intended. Replace worn seals and damaged hose with compatible parts. Do not improvise repairs with tape or household glue on chemical-contact parts. If the sprayer cannot hold pressure, leaks around the cap or produces an uneven spray, stop and identify the fault before use. Store the unit empty and avoid leaving chemical residue inside for extended periods.
