**Why growers use drip tape**

Product details

Drip irrigation applies water through outlets along a line placed near the crop rows. This can reduce the amount of water lost to wind drift and surface evaporation compared with some overhead watering methods, particularly when the system is correctly designed and operated. Delivering water near the root zone can also help keep paths and non-cropped areas drier. These are potential system benefits rather than guaranteed yield increases: actual performance depends on soil, crop, climate, filtration, pressure, emitter spacing and irrigation scheduling.

Product details

A 1,000m roll gives a grower material for longer rows or several shorter laterals, depending on the field plan. It can be used in suitable vegetable production, greenhouse beds, fruit rows, orchards, flower production and other crops where the chosen emitter spacing suits the planting pattern. Common applications include tomatoes, capsicum, cabbage, chilli, onions, herbs, cucumbers and other row crops. Select spacing according to the crop, soil texture and distance between plants; do not choose a spacing solely because it is the most common option.

**Planning an irrigation layout**

Product details

Before buying, measure the field and decide the number and length of crop rows. Check the distance between beds, the number of drip lines required per bed, the available water volume and the elevation changes across the plot. A drip tape roll is only one part of an irrigation system. Most installations also require a suitable mainline or sub-main, connectors, take-off fittings, end closures, valves, filtration and a source of pressure. The correct filter grade and pressure arrangement depend on the water source and the emitter specifications.

Product details

For a greenhouse, consider the bed length, crop spacing and the way the tape will be secured. For open-field production, plan for access paths, field slope, flushing points and protection from damage by tools, livestock and machinery. If a pump or elevated tank supplies the system, check that it can provide the flow and pressure required by the number of lines operating at the same time. A qualified irrigation installer can help size the system where the layout is large or the water source is limited.

**Installation guidance**

Product details

Lay the tape along the prepared crop row with the emitter outlets positioned as recommended by the manufacturer. Use compatible 16mm fittings and avoid stretching, twisting or sharply kinking the line. Flush the mainline and laterals before connecting or closing the ends, and flush the system periodically according to the irrigation design. Test the system at operating pressure and inspect the full length for leaks, blocked outlets, loose fittings or uneven discharge before planting or routine irrigation begins.

Product details

Do not exceed the pressure rating supplied for the chosen tape. Excessive pressure can damage thin-wall tape or cause uneven operation, while insufficient pressure can result in poor distribution along long rows. Use pressure regulation where required by the system design. Keep the tape away from sharp stones, exposed edges and activities that may puncture it. If the tape is reused between seasons, inspect it carefully for damage, clogging and deterioration before reinstalling.

Product details

**Maintenance and storage**

Product details

Water quality is a major factor in drip irrigation reliability. Install a suitable screen or disc filter and clean it at the intervals required by the water source and system use. Flush the lines when needed and inspect emitters for blockages. Do not use chemicals for flushing or cleaning unless they are compatible with the tape and the irrigation equipment and are applied according to professional guidance. When the roll is not in use, store it out of direct sunlight, away from rodents, sharp objects, oils and sources of heat. Avoid dragging the tape over rough ground.
