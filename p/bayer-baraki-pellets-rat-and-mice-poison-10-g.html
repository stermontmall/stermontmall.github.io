Why rodent control matters

Rats and mice eat and spoil stored food, gnaw wires, packaging and furniture, and spread germs through droppings and urine. A small number can quickly become a large infestation, because they breed fast. Acting at the first signs, such as droppings, gnaw marks, runways or noises at night, saves food and property.

Where to place the bait

Rodents like to move along walls, behind appliances and shelves, and between their nest and their food. Place the pellets near nesting holes, along walls and on rat runs, in spots that children, pets and livestock cannot reach. Use a covered tray or bait station where possible, so the pellets stay dry and away from other animals.

Good practice

  • Keep food in closed containers and tidy up spilled food or grain
  • Seal holes and gaps in walls, doors and roofs after treatment
  • Check the bait regularly and replace it when it is eaten or spoiled
  • Collect and dispose of dead rodents safely, wearing gloves
  • Wash your hands after handling bait

Safety

Check the PCPB registration number on the label, read the label before use, and keep away from children, pets and livestock. Never put bait where food, water or feed can be contaminated. If anyone swallows the bait by accident, get medical help at once and take the pack or label with you. For pets or livestock, call your vet.

Storage

Keep the pack closed in a cool, dry, locked place out of reach of children, pets and food.
