Why Use a Rooting Hormone

Many plants can be multiplied from cuttings, but without help some cuttings root slowly, unevenly or not at all. A rooting hormone encourages the cut end of the stem to develop roots, so more cuttings survive and become healthy young plants. This saves time, reduces losses and gives growers uniform planting material, which matters for commercial flower production and for hobby gardeners alike.

How to Use

Take a healthy cutting from a strong mother plant using a clean, sharp knife or secateurs. Dip the cut end of the cutting into the powder, tap off any extra and plant it straight away into a clean, well-drained rooting medium. Water lightly and keep the cuttings in a warm, shaded and humid place until roots form. Pour out a small amount of powder into a separate container for dipping rather than dipping into the main tub, to avoid contaminating the rest.

Nursery Tips

Use clean tools and clean trays, choose healthy cuttings with good leaves and avoid very soft or woody stems. Keep the medium moist but not waterlogged and protect the cuttings from direct hot sun until they have rooted.
