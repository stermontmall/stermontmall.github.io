WHY ZINC AND BORON MATTER

Zinc and boron are micronutrients. Crops take them up in much smaller quantities than nitrogen, phosphorus or potassium, but a shortage can still limit growth and production. Zinc is involved in plant enzyme activity and normal shoot growth. Boron is closely linked to flowering, pollen development and fruit set, which is why products like Bonzin are aimed at crops that flower and bear fruit. Deficiencies are more common in sandy or heavily leached soils, in soils with a high pH, and in fields that have been cropped intensively for many seasons. A soil or leaf test is the most reliable way to know whether your field actually needs these nutrients.

WHEN FOLIAR FEEDING MAKES SENSE

Foliar feeding does not replace a sound basic fertiliser programme. It is best used to correct or prevent a micronutrient shortage quickly, or to support the crop at demanding stages such as before and during flowering. Because the nutrients go directly onto the leaf, the crop can respond faster than it would to soil application alone. If your soil test already shows adequate zinc and boron, extra spraying may bring little benefit, and boron in particular should never be over-applied because the gap between enough and too much is narrow.

How to use

Follow the pack label first. As a general guide, the supplier recommends 20 to 30 ml of product in every 20 litres of clean water. Measure carefully with a clean measuring cup instead of estimating. Fill the sprayer about half full with water, add the product, stir, then top up with water. Spray evenly so the leaves are wetted without heavy run-off. Early morning or late afternoon is usually better than the heat of midday, because leaves stay wet longer and the risk of scorch is lower. Do not spray when rain is expected soon after application. If you plan to tank mix with another product, check compatibility on both labels first and test a small volume before mixing a full tank.

HOW FAR ONE LITRE GOES

At 20 to 30 ml per 20 litres, a 1 litre pack makes roughly 33 to 50 knapsack tanks of spray solution. How much area that covers depends on your crop, plant spacing and sprayer calibration, so use your own sprayer output to plan quantities. Growers with larger plots or several spray rounds may want more than one pack.
