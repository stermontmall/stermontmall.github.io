WHY DAILY SUN PROTECTION MATTERS Exposure to ultraviolet radiation can contribute to sunburn, premature visible skin ageing and the appearance of dark spots or uneven skin tone. Sunscreen helps reduce the amount of UV radiation reaching the skin when applied generously and reapplied as directed. It should be used alongside other sun-protection habits such as seeking shade, wearing protective clothing and avoiding prolonged direct exposure during the strongest sunlight hours. No sunscreen provides complete protection, and SPF should not be treated as permission to remain in the sun indefinitely. HOW TO APPLY Apply the sunscreen generously and evenly to the face and other exposed skin as the final step of your morning skincare routine. Use it before makeup, allowing the product to settle before applying other cosmetics. Apply before going outdoors, following the timing specified on the product label. Reapply at least every two hours during sun exposure, and after swimming, sweating heavily or towel drying, or as directed on the packaging. Applying too little sunscreen can significantly reduce the protection achieved, so use an adequate amount and cover exposed areas evenly. Remember the areas that are often missed, such as the sides of the face, ears, hairline and neck. Avoid direct contact with the eyes. If contact occurs, rinse thoroughly with clean water. Sunscreen should be used daily when exposed to daylight, even when the weather is cloudy, because UV radiation can still reach the skin.
