Where & How to Use in Kenya

For a young plant, prepare the destination before removing it from its nursery container. Choose a site with adequate space for the mature plant and avoid places where water routinely pools around the root zone. Keep the root ball intact during handling and do not leave exposed roots in direct sun or drying wind.

Set the plant at the same general soil level at which it was growing in its container, backfill gently and water to settle the soil. Use mulch where appropriate while keeping it clear of direct contact with the stem. Check moisture, weeds, physical damage and signs of stress regularly.

The exact irrigation frequency, spacing, pruning schedule and nutrition programme depend on species, variety, soil, rainfall, altitude and local conditions; obtain crop-specific advice rather than applying a universal schedule.

Kenyan farms vary between highland, mid-altitude, coastal, arid and semi-arid settings. Soil texture, rainfall pattern, water access, exposure and the intended crop system can change what is appropriate. Treat general product information as a starting point, not a substitute for a site-specific recommendation.

Safety, Storage & Handling Guidelines

Inspect the plant on receipt and contact the seller promptly if it is damaged, severely wilted or does not match the agreed description. Keep seedlings upright during transport and protect them from crushing, prolonged heat and drying. Do not leave a newly delivered plant inside a hot vehicle.

Store it temporarily in a sheltered place with suitable light and moisture while preparing the planting site. Keep young plants away from grazing animals, accidental trampling and mechanical damage. Use clean tools when opening a nursery bag or trimming damaged material.

If a plant shows unusual symptoms, isolate it from other nursery stock and seek advice from a qualified local horticulture professional; do not apply pesticides or fertilisers without identifying the issue and confirming label suitability.
