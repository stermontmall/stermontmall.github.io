Where & How to Use in Kenya

Poultry farmers can use plastic mesh to form partitions, separate sections within a poultry unit or create protective boundaries around a run. The 1-metre height can be useful for low-level barriers and pen divisions, subject to the bird type, enclosure design and required containment height. It can also be considered for small livestock areas, garden protection and general farm screening where the mesh opening and strength are appropriate.

It is not a substitute for a properly designed predator-proof enclosure where small openings, buried edges or rigid structural support are required. The material may be useful in damp farm environments where rust on metal mesh is a concern. It can be easier to handle than some wire products, but it still needs adequate support and secure fixing.

Do not assume the roll is suitable for every poultry species or predator risk: inspect the mesh opening size and physical strength before installation. INSTALLATION GUIDE Measure the run or partition before cutting the roll. Plan the support posts or frame first, and use suitable ties or fasteners that do not cut through the plastic strands.

Keep the mesh evenly tensioned without stretching it beyond its natural limit. Secure the top, bottom and vertical edges so birds cannot push underneath or force open a loose section. For a longer fence, add intermediate supports to reduce sagging.

Trim cut edges neatly and inspect for sharp or protruding fasteners. BEFORE YOU BUY Confirm the opening size, roll dimensions and intended use with the supplier. The listing states a 25m × 1m roll and 400 GSM weight, but does not provide a mesh-aperture measurement or a published tensile-strength value.

If the mesh will be used to keep out rodents, snakes or other predators, compare the aperture size with the animal you need to exclude. For exposed outdoor installations, ask about UV stabilisation and expected service life in direct sunlight. Also check whether the supplier's current roll is the same green 400 GSM specification shown on the page.

CARE AND MAINTENANCE Inspect the mesh regularly for cuts, loose ties, stretched sections and damage caused by animals or farm equipment. Replace or repair damaged sections promptly so gaps do not develop.
