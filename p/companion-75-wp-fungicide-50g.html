DISEASE IDENTIFICATION AND FIELD PLANNING Successful disease management begins with correct diagnosis. Fungal symptoms can resemble nutrient deficiencies, bacterial infections, viral diseases or physical damage. Inspect the crop and, where necessary, obtain a diagnosis from an agricultural extension officer or qualified crop adviser. Compare the identified disease with the approved crop use on the label. Plan treatment with attention to weather, crop stage, spray coverage and pre-harvest intervals. Avoid unnecessary spraying and do not increase the rate to compensate for poor coverage or late treatment. Use integrated disease management practices such as clean planting material, crop rotation where appropriate, field sanitation, removal of heavily infected residues, good spacing and ventilation, and resistant varieties when available. RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT AND COMPATIBILITY Repeated use of fungicides with the same mode of action can select for resistant pathogen populations. Follow label limits on the number and timing of applications, and rotate or combine modes of action only when advised by the approved label or a qualified crop-protection professional. Do not assume that tank mixing is safe because two products are commonly used on the same crop. Check the label compatibility information and perform a small physical compatibility test only where permitted before preparing a commercial spray volume. Do not mix products when the label prohibits it or when crop safety and residue requirements are unclear.
