Product overview

Sheep, Goat and Pig Ear Tags are livestock identification supplies for farmers managing small ruminants and pigs. Individual identification helps keep breeding, health, ownership and movement records organised, especially where animals have similar coats or are managed in several pens and grazing groups.

How to use & application

A visible ear tag can make routine identification easier during feeding, weighing, vaccination, treatment, breeding checks and sales. The supplier lists sheep, goat and pig ear tags within its livestock equipment range. For animals being raised for sale, tag numbers can help link weights, feed records and treatment history to the correct animal. Use a tag applicator made for the chosen tag system and follow the manufacturer’s placement instructions.

Buying guide & product checks

The reference image shows yellow numbered tags with matching fasteners, but the exact product supplied may vary in colour, size, numbering and attachment style. Confirm the pack quantity, tag dimensions, numbering format and compatibility with your applicator before purchase. A pack may support a new group of young animals or help standardise an existing herd. Before ordering, estimate how many animals need tags and check whether the supplier sells the tags as individual pieces, pairs or complete sets with pins. Also confirm whether the tags are blank for farm numbering or supplied pre-numbered. Ear tags are identification aids, not substitutes for statutory animal movement documentation, veterinary certification or official traceability requirements where those apply.

Uses & applications

Some livestock tag systems are designed for specific species or ear sizes; do not assume that one tag size is suitable for every lamb, kid, adult goat and pig. A good identification system starts with a clear record plan. If a tag is lost, update the records carefully and replace it with a number that does not create duplicate identities in the herd or flock register. This product can be useful to goat and sheep keepers, pig farmers, breeding projects, livestock groups, cooperatives, schools and agricultural training centres. Farmers should follow relevant Kenyan livestock identification and movement rules and keep their farm records accurate. Customers searching for sheep ear tags Kenya, goat identification tags, pig ear tags, livestock numbering tags or small ruminant farm supplies can use this listing to identify the intended species and product category.

Product details

Assign each tag number to one animal and record the species, sex, approximate age, breed or type, date of tagging and any other details relevant to your farm. For breeding animals, the record may include mating dates, kidding or farrowing information, offspring identification and health events. This makes it easier to identify an individual animal during routine checks and reduces confusion when animals are moved, sold or combined with another group.

Safety information

Tagging should be carried out by a competent person using calm, secure animal handling. Correct placement helps reduce the risk of unnecessary injury, irritation and tag loss. Avoid applying tags through damaged or infected tissue. If you are unfamiliar with tagging or are unsure about the correct position for a particular species, ask a veterinarian or trained livestock technician to demonstrate the procedure. Keep tags and pins together in their original pack until use to reduce the risk of losing small components. For group management, record tag numbers alongside pen or paddock details, breeding records and health events.

Care, cleaning & storage

Inspect newly tagged animals after fitting and continue checking tags during routine handling. Watch for swelling, bleeding, discharge, irritation or signs that the tag is catching on fences or feeders. Seek veterinary advice if infection or significant injury is suspected. Keep the tagging equipment clean and maintain appropriate hygiene between animals.

Features & specifications

Small ruminants and pigs may require different tag dimensions and fastening arrangements, so confirm the exact type before applying a tag across mixed species. Young animals have smaller ears than mature stock; the selected tag should be appropriate for the animal’s size and the manufacturer’s stated application.
