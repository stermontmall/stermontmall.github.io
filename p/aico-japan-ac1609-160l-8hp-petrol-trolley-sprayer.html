WHY A 160-LITRE TROLLEY UNIT?

A large tank can reduce the number of refills during a planned spraying operation. This may be useful on extensive outdoor sites where moving a smaller knapsack sprayer back and forth would interrupt the work. The trolley format also avoids carrying the full tank on an operator's back. However, a 160-litre capacity means the machine becomes very heavy when filled. The operator must plan a stable parking position, safe route, ground bearing capacity and a suitable refill point. Do not attempt to pull or push a full machine across unsuitable slopes, steps or soft ground. Use the trolley only within the manufacturer's load and handling guidance.

ENGINE AND POWER SYSTEM

The supplier lists an 8HP four-stroke petrol engine. Four-stroke engines normally use petrol in the fuel tank and engine oil separately in the crankcase. Check the engine manual for the correct fuel, oil grade, capacity and service interval. Never assume that two-stroke premix is appropriate. Check the oil level before starting, inspect for leaks and operate the engine in a well-ventilated outdoor location. Keep the engine away from dry vegetation, fuel spills, open flames and other ignition sources. Turn it off and allow hot components to cool before refuelling or carrying out maintenance.

TANK AND LIQUID MANAGEMENT

The listing describes a 160-litre chemical-resistant tank. Chemical resistance is not universal: compatibility depends on the formulation, concentration, temperature and contact time. Read the chemical label and ask the equipment supplier if compatibility is uncertain. Use the tank only for approved liquid applications and never for drinking water, food or beverages. Fill through a suitable filter if the manual recommends one, avoid overfilling and secure the cap. Calculate the required solution from the product label and calibrated application rate rather than filling the tank automatically to capacity. Prepare only the amount needed for the planned job.

HOSE, REEL AND SPRAY GUN

The supplier lists a 50-metre reinforced high-pressure hose and adjustable brass spray gun. The hose provides reach around the work area, while the gun directs the liquid toward the target. Confirm whether a hose reel is included; it is not assumed from the listed specifications. Inspect the hose for abrasion, bulges, cracks and damaged couplings before every use. Keep it away from sharp edges, vehicle wheels and hot engine parts. Never search for a pinhole leak with bare hands while the system is pressurised. Shut down and depressurise according to the manual before checking fittings. The spray gun should be operated only within its rated pressure range.

PLANNING A LARGE SPRAYING OPERATION

Survey the site and choose a level, stable position for the trolley. Plan the hose route to avoid walkways, vehicle lanes and public access. Identify water sources, drains, ponds, livestock enclosures, food stores and sensitive crops before mixing. Use the product label to calculate dilution and application rate. Check wind and weather conditions, and postpone work when drift or runoff could cause harm. Divide the site into sections and maintain a record of the treatment product, batch, dilution, date, operator and areas covered. Ensure that workers and visitors remain outside the treatment zone until the label's re-entry conditions are met.

OPERATOR AND ENVIRONMENTAL

Safety information

Read the machine manual and chemical label before starting. Wear all label-required PPE, which may include chemical-resistant gloves, protective clothing, boots, eye protection and respiratory protection. Keep the spray gun pointed away from people and animals. High-pressure liquid can penetrate skin and cause serious injury; never touch a suspected leak or direct the spray at a person. Stop the engine and release pressure before servicing the pump or hose. Refuel only with the engine switched off and cool, away from flames and sparks. Prevent chemical drift and runoff, and never discharge residues into drains, streams, wells or ponds.

CLEANING, SERVICE AND

Storage & handling

After use, follow the chemical label and equipment manual for emptying, rinsing and disposal. Do not release concentrated product or rinse water into the environment. Clean the tank, filters, pump, hose and spray gun using the approved procedure. Inspect the trolley frame, wheels, hose connections, clamps, seals and tank for damage. Maintain the engine according to the service schedule, including oil, air filter and spark plug checks where specified. Replace hoses and fittings only with pressure-rated compatible parts. Store the unit clean, empty and dry in a secure, ventilated location. Keep petrol in approved containers and away from ignition sources.
