About Multiple-Catch Live Mouse Trap

The Multiple-Catch Live Mouse Trap is a reusable mechanical device designed to capture more than one mouse between inspections. The product reference image shows a Ketch-All-style metal enclosure with a clear viewing lid, but the exact make and supplied accessories should be confirmed with the supplier. Multiple-catch traps provide a non-rodenticide option for selected indoor rodent-monitoring situations. They should be checked frequently and managed in accordance with animal-welfare guidance.

How a multiple-catch Trap works

A multiple-catch trap uses an entry tunnel and an internal mechanical mechanism to retain mice inside a holding compartment. Manufacturer information for Ketch-All-style traps describes a trigger or paddle-wheel mechanism that activates when a mouse enters. Some designs use the mouse's natural curiosity rather than requiring bait, while other live-capture traps may have different operating methods. Confirm the exact mechanism for the model supplied and follow its instructions before setting it.

Where to position the Trap

Mice often travel along edges and sheltered routes rather than crossing open floor areas. Inspect the premises for droppings, gnaw marks, tracks, damaged packaging and other signs before choosing a position. Manufacturer instructions for Ketch-All-style traps recommend placing the trap close to and parallel with a wall, with an entry opening facing the wall. Follow the instructions for the exact trap and ensure the unit is stable, protected from disturbance and accessible for inspection. Do not place it where children, pets or non-target animals can reach it.

Bait and setting requirements

Some multiple-catch mouse traps rely on an internal mechanical trigger and may not require bait, but this should not be assumed for every product. Read the supplied instructions to determine whether bait is needed and how the mechanism is set. Do not modify the trap or add chemicals. Keep fingers clear of moving components during setting and emptying. If the trap uses a spring or rotating mechanism, follow the manufacturer's method rather than forcing the mechanism by hand.

Inspection and animal welfare

A live-capture trap must be inspected frequently and should never be left unattended for long periods. Check the trap at the interval required by the manufacturer and local animal-welfare guidance. If a mouse is captured, follow the appropriate handling and release procedure for the location. Do not leave a captured animal without water or care for an extended period. If the trap is damaged or a captured animal appears injured or distressed, seek appropriate professional advice. Local rules may govern release locations and methods.

Cleaning and reuse

After emptying, clean and disinfect the trap according to the manufacturer's instructions before reuse. Wear suitable gloves and avoid direct contact with droppings or urine. Do not use strong chemicals that may damage the mechanism or leave residues that deter future entry unless the manufacturer permits them. Allow the trap to dry fully and check the trigger, entry doors, lid and viewing panel for damage. Store it in a clean, dry location when not in use.

Reduce Mouse access and food sources

Trapping is more effective when combined with prevention. Store food and animal feed in sealed containers, keep refuse covered, clean crumbs and spills promptly and remove clutter that offers nesting material. Inspect gaps around doors, pipes and service penetrations and seal suitable openings. Monitor new deliveries and storage areas for signs of activity. If mice continue to appear, review the site for access routes and harbourage rather than relying on a single trap.
