About Koppert Horiver Insect Monitoring Traps – 10 Pack (Blue or Yellow)

Koppert Horiver Insect Monitoring Traps are sticky cards designed to help growers detect and monitor selected flying insects in greenhouses, nurseries and other crop-growing environments. This listing identifies a 10-piece pack with a choice of blue or yellow. The colour selection matters because different insect groups respond differently to coloured monitoring cards. Choose the card colour for the target pest and confirm whether the supplied version is the dry-glue Horiver Drystick product before use.

Blue and yellow card selection

Koppert's product information describes blue Horiver cards primarily for monitoring thrips. Yellow cards are used for whiteflies and, depending on the specific Horiver formulation, other flying pests such as fungus gnats and leaf miners. The dry-glue Horiver Drystick range also identifies yellow cards for monitoring Nesidiocoris tenuis and white cards for Lygus species. This listing offers blue or yellow, so customers should select the colour that matches the crop-monitoring objective and the exact product variant. Do not assume that every colour captures the same pest group.

How the sticky cards work

Insects are attracted to the colour of the card and become caught on the adhesive surface. The captured insects can then be counted or examined during crop inspections. Koppert offers both wet-glue and dry-glue Horiver products. The wet-glue version has an adhesive surface that may transfer if touched, while the dry-glue version is designed to reduce glue staining on hands and crops. Check the product packaging to confirm which version is supplied. The cards are monitoring tools; they do not replace crop inspection or provide a complete pest-control programme.

Where to place the cards

Koppert recommends placing cards in areas with a higher risk of pest entry or activity, such as near doors, gable ends and lateral ventilation openings. The cards should be attached securely to wires or sticks and positioned above the crop. The recommended height should be adjusted as plants grow. Avoid placing cards directly between plants where beneficial parasitic wasps may also be trapped. Follow the crop-specific placement instructions and any advice from your local Koppert representative or agronomist.

Monitoring and catch records

For useful comparisons, record the card colour, location, crop, installation date and inspection date. Count insects consistently and note whether the card is becoming covered with dust or debris. Replace cards when they are largely covered or when the supplier's instructions indicate replacement is due. A change in catch numbers may show that pest pressure is changing, but it should be interpreted alongside visual crop scouting. Sticky cards do not always distinguish similar-looking species, so obtain identification support when necessary.

Using cards with biological control

Sticky cards can support biological-control programmes by helping growers observe pest activity and evaluate trends. However, the card colour and placement should be selected carefully to reduce unnecessary capture of beneficial insects. Koppert's instructions specifically caution against placing cards between plants where parasitic wasps may be trapped. If you release beneficial insects or use biological-control agents, follow the supplier's placement advice for the exact crop and product.

Storage and handling

Keep unused cards in their original packaging in a cool, dry place and follow the storage temperature and best-before guidance on the pack. Handle adhesive surfaces carefully and keep cards away from children, pets, clothing and non-target wildlife. Do not use cards that are damaged, contaminated or past the supplier's recommended storage period. Dispose of used cards responsibly.
