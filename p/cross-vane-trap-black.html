About Cross Vane Beetle Monitoring Trap – Black

The Cross Vane Beetle Monitoring Trap – Black is a specialist interception trap for monitoring selected flying beetles, including bark beetles, ambrosia beetles and longhorn beetles. It is used in forestry, plantations, timber-related monitoring and specialist pest-surveillance programmes where the target insects are known and an appropriate lure can be selected. The cross-vane design creates a broad interception surface; a funnel and collection container direct insects into a catch chamber for inspection.

How the Cross-Vane design works

The trap uses intersecting vertical panels arranged in a cross or X shape. Flying beetles collide with or land on the panels and are directed down towards a funnel and collection cup. The trap may be used with a species-specific pheromone or other attractant where required. The correct lure is essential: different beetle species respond to different attractants, and a generic lure should not be assumed to work for every target. Confirm the recommended lure, dispenser and collection method before deployment.

Targets and applications

Cross-vane traps are used in monitoring programmes for bark beetles, ambrosia beetles, longhorn beetles and other wood-boring insects, depending on the trap and lure combination. They can support surveys in forests, tree plantations, timber yards and research settings. The presence of a trap does not prove that a particular beetle species is present; captured insects should be identified by someone competent to distinguish target species from non-target insects. Follow local forestry or plant-health guidance when monitoring potentially invasive pests.

Selecting a location

Choose a location based on the target species, survey objective, vegetation, access and lure instructions. The trap should hang securely and vertically, with enough clearance for the vanes and collection container. Avoid placing it where people, livestock or machinery may strike it. In a plantation or survey grid, record each trap location so catches can be compared across sites. Do not position traps close to susceptible trees or timber stock without checking the survey guidance, because some lures can attract insects towards the trap location.

Lure handling and collection

Use only a lure recommended for the target beetle and trap system. Keep lures in their original packaging until needed and avoid cross-contamination between lure types. Follow the supplier's instructions for attaching, replacing and disposing of lures. Some monitoring programmes use a dry collection cup; others use a wet collection method or an approved preservation liquid. This listing does not specify which lure or collection liquid is included, so confirm the required accessories separately. Do not add insecticide, antifreeze or other liquid unless the trap instructions and programme guidance permit it.

Inspection and data quality

Inspect the trap at the interval required by the survey protocol and record the date, location, lure type, weather or site observations and insects captured. Remove specimens carefully and preserve or label them according to the identification programme. Check that the vanes remain aligned, the funnel is clear and the collection cup is attached securely. Replace damaged components and remove accumulated debris. Consistent servicing is important because a blocked funnel or missing lure can make monitoring results unreliable.

Product details and storage

This listing identifies one black cross-vane beetle monitoring trap. Supplier listings describe a panel-and-funnel design with a collection container; exact dimensions, material, included hanger, cup and assembly accessories should be confirmed from the current pack. Store the trap clean and dry when not in use and keep lure components separate from the trap body where required to prevent unwanted odour transfer.
