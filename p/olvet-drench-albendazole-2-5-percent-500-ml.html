Why Deworming Is Important

Worms drain animals of blood and nutrients. Infected cattle, goats and sheep may lose weight, produce less milk, grow slowly, develop diarrhoea and become weak and more open to other diseases. Young animals are especially at risk. Regular, well-planned deworming protects growth, milk yield, fertility and the value of your animals.

Good Deworming Practice

Dose each animal by body weight as shown on the label, because under-dosing can lead to worms becoming resistant to the medicine. Use a clean drenching gun, restrain animals gently, and avoid choking the animal when drenching. Rotate pastures where you can, avoid overstocking and keep water troughs clean. Talk to your animal health provider about the best deworming schedule for your area, especially in wet seasons when worm burdens rise.
