About Long Heavy-Duty Chemical-Resistant Rubber Gloves

Long Heavy-Duty Chemical-Resistant Rubber Gloves are protective handwear intended for selected cleaning, handling and maintenance tasks where a longer cuff and chemical-resistant glove may be required. The extended cuff can help cover more of the wrist and lower forearm than a short disposable glove. However, the term chemical-resistant does not mean the glove is compatible with every chemical. Protection depends on the glove material, thickness, lining, length, chemical concentration, temperature and contact time.

Why glove material compatibility matters

Different glove materials resist different chemicals. A glove that performs well against one cleaning solution may degrade, swell or allow permeation when exposed to another substance. The correct selection should be based on the manufacturer's chemical-resistance chart and the safety data sheet for the product being handled. Do not choose gloves by colour or thickness alone. If the exact material composition is not stated on the pack, ask the supplier to confirm it before using the gloves for a chemical task.

Long-cuff design

A longer cuff can provide additional coverage around the wrist and lower forearm, particularly during wet cleaning or handling tasks where splashes may occur. The cuff should be long enough for the task and should be worn in the way recommended by the manufacturer. Avoid folding or rolling the cuff if doing so could allow liquid to enter. Check that the glove fits securely and does not restrict hand movement so much that tools or containers become difficult to control.

Select the right size

Choose a glove size that allows the wearer to grip equipment and perform the task without excessive tension. A glove that is too tight can tear or cause fatigue; one that is too loose may reduce dexterity and increase the chance of snagging. Check finger length, palm width and cuff fit. If gloves are purchased for a team, confirm sizes individually. Inspect both gloves before use for holes, cracks, weak seams, sticky areas or other signs of deterioration.

Use in cleaning and maintenance

Long rubber gloves may be useful for suitable wet cleaning, wash-down and maintenance tasks when the glove material is confirmed compatible with the cleaning product. They can help reduce direct skin contact with water, detergents and selected chemicals. Do not assume that the gloves protect against concentrated acids, alkalis, solvents, pesticides or other hazardous products unless the manufacturer's chart specifically confirms compatibility. For pesticide handling and fumigation, follow the product label and safety data sheet, which may specify a particular glove material and thickness.

Inspection, cleaning and storage

Follow the manufacturer's instructions for cleaning and reuse. Some heavy-duty gloves are reusable, while others may be intended for limited use. Rinse or clean only using a method that does not damage the glove material. Allow reusable gloves to dry fully before storage and keep them away from direct sunlight, heat, sharp objects and chemicals that could degrade them. Store gloves in a clean, dry place and do not store contaminated gloves with clean PPE. Replace gloves if they become punctured, swollen, brittle, sticky, discoloured or otherwise damaged.

Safe removal and hygiene

Remove gloves without touching the contaminated outer surface with bare skin. Follow the workplace procedure for disposal or cleaning. Wash hands after removing gloves, even when the gloves appear intact. Do not use a damaged glove for a task involving hazardous chemicals. If chemical contact or glove failure occurs, follow the substance's first-aid instructions and workplace incident procedure.
