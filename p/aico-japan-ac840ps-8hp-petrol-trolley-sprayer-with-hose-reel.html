WHY A TROLLEY SPRAYER?

A trolley format keeps the main engine and pump on a wheeled frame instead of placing the machine and liquid tank on the operator's back. The operator can position the unit at a suitable point and move the hose toward the work area. This can be helpful around larger gardens, estates, farms, orchards, landscaped grounds and other sites where a hose can reach several treatment sections. The 50-metre hose listed by the supplier may reduce the need to move the trolley frequently, but actual reach depends on the route, terrain, hose condition and operating requirements. A hose should never be stretched, kinked or dragged over sharp edges.

UNDERSTANDING THE ENGINE AND PUMP

The AC840PS is listed with an 8HP 170F four-stroke petrol engine. Four-stroke engines normally use petrol as fuel and a separate crankcase oil supply. Read the supplied manual for the correct fuel, oil grade, oil level, starting procedure and service intervals. Do not mix two-stroke oil into the petrol unless the exact engine manual specifically requires it. The listed triple-plunger brass pump is intended to provide pressurised liquid delivery. Pump performance depends on correct priming, clean filters, sound seals, adequate liquid supply and operation within the manufacturer's limits. Never run the pump dry unless the manual expressly allows it.

THE HOSE REEL AND 50-METRE HOSE

A manual steel hose reel helps organise the delivery hose between jobs and can reduce tangling when the hose is stored correctly. Before operation, unwind enough hose to reach the work area without sharp bends. Check the hose outer surface, couplings, clamps and spray-gun connections for damage or leaks. Do not stand on a pressurised hose or pull the trolley by the hose. When finishing, release pressure as directed by the manual before disconnecting or winding the hose. Keep the hose away from vehicle paths, hot engine components and sharp corners. The 50-metre length is a supplier-listed specification and should be checked against the actual package.

PRESSURE, FLOW AND APPLICATION CONTROL

The supplier lists working pressure of about 20–35 kg/cm² and flow of approximately 8–10 litres per minute. Treat these as listing specifications, not as a guaranteed output under every condition. Pressure and flow can vary with nozzle selection, hose length, pump condition, engine speed, liquid properties and maintenance. Use a nozzle and application rate suitable for the product label and target. Do not increase engine speed or pressure simply to compensate for a blocked nozzle or unsuitable spray gun. Stop the machine, release pressure safely and inspect the filter, nozzle and connections. Use a clean-water test to confirm operation before preparing a treatment mixture.

SUITABLE WORK CONTEXTS

This type of trolley sprayer may be considered for compatible agricultural spraying, orchard care, estate grounds maintenance and professional outdoor treatment tasks. It can also support pest-management work where a product label specifically permits the intended outdoor application and the equipment materials are compatible. It is not appropriate to assume that every pesticide, herbicide, disinfectant or solvent can be used in the machine. Check the formulation against the pump seals, hose, tank or supply container, spray gun and other wetted components. Follow Kenyan product registration and label requirements, and use a qualified operator for regulated or higher-risk treatments.

PLANNING LARGE-SITE WORK

Map the area before starting and position the trolley on stable ground where the operator can reach the work area without blocking walkways or vehicle routes. Identify water points, drains, ponds, livestock areas, food storage, neighbouring crops and public access. Use the treatment label to calculate the required dilution and application rate. Prepare only the quantity needed for the planned section. Check wind direction and weather, and postpone spraying when drift or runoff could create risk. Keep a record of the product, batch, dilution, treated location, date and operator. For commercial premises, coordinate the work so that staff and visitors remain outside the restricted area until the label permits re-entry.

Safety information

DURING OPERATION

Read the sprayer manual and treatment label before use. Wear all protective equipment required by the label, which may include chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection, coveralls, boots and respiratory protection. Keep the spray gun pointed away from people and animals. High-pressure liquid can cause injury; never use a hand or body part to search for a leak. Switch off the engine and depressurise the system according to the manual before servicing fittings, clearing blockages or disconnecting the hose. Refuel only with the engine off and cool, outdoors or in a well-ventilated area, away from flames and sparks. Do not smoke during refuelling.

CLEANING AND MAINTENANCE

After each job, follow the chemical label and machine manual for safe emptying, rinsing and disposal. Do not discharge pesticide residues or rinse water into drains, streams, wells or ponds. Clean filters, spray gun, hose and pump using the approved procedure. Check the brass pump, seals, hose reel, frame, tyres, fasteners and engine before the next job. Replace worn hoses and seals with parts rated for the machine's pressure. Keep the engine oil at the correct level and maintain the air filter, spark plug and other service items at the intervals in the manual. Persistent leaks, abnormal vibration or pressure fluctuations should be inspected by a competent technician.

Storage & handling

AND TRANSPORT

Release system pressure before storage and wind the hose onto the reel without twisting or forcing it. Empty and clean the liquid circuit as directed. Store the trolley in a dry, secure and ventilated place, away from children, food and animal feed. Handle petrol in approved containers and observe fire-safety rules. During transport, secure the frame and protect the hose, spray gun and pump fittings from impact. Follow the engine manual for long periods without use.
