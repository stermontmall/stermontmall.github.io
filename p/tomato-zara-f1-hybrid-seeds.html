Nursery establishment

To get strong seedlings, sow the seeds in well-prepared nursery beds or trays using sterilized potting media. Keep the media moist and warm throughout the nursery phase. Healthy seedlings give you an even crop after transplanting, and even plants mean even harvests.

Crop management

Good seed performs best with good husbandry. Irrigate on time, fertilize in a balanced way and scout regularly for pests and diseases. Stake and prune as the plants grow, control weeds, and consider protective netting to reduce pest pressure. Follow the seed label and the advice of your local agricultural extension officer for planting guidelines specific to your region.

Harvest and marketing

Firm, uniform fruit is easier to grade, pack and transport, and it sells better at the market. Handle the harvest gently and keep the fruit shaded and cool until it is sold.

Who it suits

  • Commercial tomato farmers and greenhouse growers
  • Small and medium farmers who sell at local markets
  • Farmer groups planning a large planting from one pack
  • Growers who want a variety that is easy to stake, prune and harvest

Storage

Store unopened seed packets in a cool, dry and dark place to protect seed viability and germination. Keep all agricultural supplies out of reach of children and domestic animals.
