About Azolla Starter Culture 1kg

Product overview

Azolla Starter Culture is a live aquatic fern culture used to establish a small Azolla-growing unit for supplementary farm feed. Unlike a conventional dry seed packet, Azolla starter material is living plant biomass that is introduced into a prepared pond, tank or lined shallow bed so it can multiply. Farmers use the harvested fern as a green supplement in integrated livestock systems, particularly where they want to produce part of their own supplementary feed using a small area and a reliable water source. Azolla grows as a floating mat on still or slow-moving freshwater. Under suitable conditions it multiplies quickly, allowing repeated harvesting once the culture has established. The plant is valued for its protein-rich biomass and its ability to host nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria. It can be included in feeding programmes for poultry, fish, rabbits, pigs, goats and cattle, but it should be treated as a supplement rather than a complete ration. Livestock still require species-appropriate feed, forage, minerals and clean water. Prepare the growing area before the culture arrives. A shallow pond, a masonry tank or a lined bed can be used, provided it holds water without leaking and can be reached easily for harvesting. Choose a location that receives partial shade rather than intense all-day sun. The water should be clean and free from detergents, oil, herbicide residues and other contaminants. Chlorinated water should be allowed to become suitable for cultivation before the starter culture is introduced. Do not establish Azolla in a water body that may receive runoff from chemical mixing or pesticide washing areas. A nutrient source is needed to support growth. Farmers commonly prepare the bed with well-decomposed organic manure and suitable mineral inputs, using measured quantities rather than adding large amounts at once. Excess nutrients can foul the water, while too little nutrition can slow multiplication. Maintain a shallow, stable water level and avoid strong currents that push the floating fern into one corner. The culture should spread across the surface without being buried or submerged. Water quality has a direct effect on establishment. Monitor the appearance of the fern and the water regularly. Healthy growth generally forms a dense floating cover; poor colour, unpleasant odour, heavy sediment or sudden die-back can indicate an imbalance or contamination. A practical pH range around 5.8 to 6.2 is used in some production systems, but local water and management conditions matter. If growth stalls, check shade, water depth, nutrient balance and water quality before adding more fertiliser. Harvesting should be regular but not excessive. Remove a portion of the floating mat and leave enough healthy material to cover the growing surface and continue multiplying. Rinse harvested Azolla where necessary to remove pond debris, and introduce it gradually into animal feeding programmes. Sudden large changes to a ration can upset digestion or reduce feed intake. For poultry and fish, use it as one ingredient in a balanced ration; for ruminants, combine it with appropriate forage and concentrates rather than replacing the normal diet. A dedicated Azolla bed can fit into a smallholder farm because it uses a compact space and can be placed near a water point. It may complement poultry, dairy, rabbit or fish enterprises, but the farmer should plan the daily labour required for water checks, cleaning, harvesting and replenishing nutrients. Keep the production bed separate from animal pens so manure, litter and feed waste do not contaminate the culture. Use clean tools and wash hands after handling pond material. Store the starter culture temporarily in a cool, shaded place and establish it promptly after receipt. Do not leave it sealed in a hot vehicle or exposed to drying sunlight. If transport is delayed, keep the material moist and protected from heat. Once established, maintain a small reserve culture in a separate container so that the farm can restart the main bed if contamination or weather damages it. Azolla Starter Culture is suitable for farmers interested in producing a renewable green supplement as part of a broader feeding plan. Results depend on water quality, temperature, light, nutrient balance and routine harvesting. Begin with a manageable bed, keep simple notes on growth and feed use, and expand only after the culture is stable. Use the harvested biomass responsibly and seek local livestock or aquaculture guidance when formulating diets for a particular species or production stage. For farms in warm parts of Kenya, a shaded position can help prevent the pond from overheating during the middle of the day. The bed should still receive enough indirect light for growth. Use a net or simple shade structure where necessary, and protect the water surface from poultry, ducks and other animals that may disturb or contaminate the culture. Keep the pond edges accessible so that harvesting does not require stepping into the water. A practical routine is to check the bed at the same time each day. Look for changes in colour, water level, smell and surface coverage, and remove unwanted leaves or debris before they decay. Replenish water lost through evaporation with clean water, and replace only the portion harvested. If the culture becomes contaminated or stops growing, separate healthy material into a clean container while investigating the cause. Good records of harvesting and feeding help show whether the culture is contributing useful biomass to the farm.
