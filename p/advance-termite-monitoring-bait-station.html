Product Overview & Key Benefits

The Advance in-ground station is a perimeter monitoring unit used in structured subterranean termite inspection programmes. Its durable housing is installed in soil at planned monitoring points, allowing trained technicians to inspect the station and check for termite activity without opening the building fabric. The station supports monitoring and follow-up decisions; it is not, by itself, a complete termite treatment or a guarantee against infestation.

Position and inspect it according to the system instructions and local site conditions. This listing is for the monitoring station hardware; cartridges or bait components may be separate and should be confirmed before ordering.

An in-ground monitoring station is an access point in a planned perimeter inspection system. It allows a technician to check for termite activity at selected locations and document changes over time. The station housing does not itself create a chemical barrier, kill a colony or guarantee that a building is termite-free.

Its value depends on correct placement and routine inspection.

Begin with a site survey of foundations, expansion joints, service penetrations, damp areas, landscaping, timber in contact with soil and locations with previous termite evidence. A trained technician should plan station positions so that each can be located and inspected. Avoid locations likely to be flooded, paved over, disturbed by construction or damaged by vehicle traffic.

Confirm exactly what the package includes. Station housing, inspection inserts, monitoring devices and active bait cartridges may be separate components depending on the system and supplier package. Do not describe the hardware as containing an active ingredient unless the supplied label confirms it.

Check compatibility with the exact Advance system and obtain the current installation instructions.

Prepare a station map and assign each installed point a unique identifier. Record the installation date, location, surrounding conditions and any relevant building observations. During each inspection, note the station condition, moisture, disturbance and evidence of termite activity.

Consistent records help compare visits and identify changes that would be missed by an unrecorded visual check.

Monitoring intervals should follow the manufacturer instructions and the site risk profile. Keep covers accessible and replace damaged components only with compatible parts. A station that has been moved, flooded, blocked or buried under landscaping may no longer provide useful information.

Do not assume that an unattended station provides continuous protection or that absence of activity at one point proves the whole property is clear.

If activity is detected, a qualified pest-management professional should identify the termite and assess the building before selecting a response. Depending on the situation, an integrated plan may involve bait components, targeted treatment, moisture correction, removal of conducive conditions, physical repairs and follow-up inspections. Monitoring hardware alone is not a complete termite treatment.

For homes, hotels, warehouses, schools and commercial properties, explain to the client what the station does and does not do. It can support early detection and evidence-based decisions, but it cannot guarantee that termites will not enter a structure. Document inspections, treatment products, active ingredients, follow-up work and any warranty terms accurately.

A perimeter station is most useful when the monitoring programme has a clear baseline. At installation, record the building condition, visible conducive factors and known termite history. Mark stations on a plan that can be used by different technicians and retain photographs of the initial placement.

This makes later inspections more consistent and helps explain any changes to the property owner.

Do not place stations where routine mowing, excavation, drainage work or landscaping will repeatedly disturb them. Tell the property manager where the units are located and ask contractors to avoid covering or removing them. If a station becomes inaccessible, record the issue and restore access using the installation instructions rather than leaving an uninspected point on the monitoring map.

A monitoring station should be included in a wider maintenance agreement that defines inspection frequency, reporting, escalation when activity is found and responsibility for any separate treatment. Explain whether bait cartridges or treatment materials are included in the service. Clear documentation prevents the customer from mistaking a passive monitoring housing for an active pesticide treatment or a permanent structural warranty.

Safety, Storage & Handling Guidelines

This is a pest-control or agricultural chemical product. Use it only for the crop, pest, site and application method expressly authorised on the current Kenya-approved product label. Read the full label before opening or use.

Follow the label exactly for personal protective equipment, dilution, application, ventilation, re-entry period, pre-harvest interval, environmental restrictions and any other precautions. Do not guess a dose, mix it with another product, or use it on food, animals, people or surfaces unless the label specifically permits that use. Keep the original container tightly closed and locked away from children, unauthorised persons and animals, and separate from food, drink and animal feed.

Prevent spills and contamination of drains, wells, streams and other water sources. Do not eat, drink or smoke while handling; wash hands and exposed skin after handling. Never reuse an empty pesticide container; follow the label and approved local hazardous-waste collection or disposal instructions.

In case of suspected poisoning or significant exposure, seek urgent medical attention and show the product label. Kenya PCPB poison information hotlines listed in its labelling guidance: 0800 720 021 or 0800 730 030.
