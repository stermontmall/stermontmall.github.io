Why rodent and mole control matters

Rats eat and spoil stored food, gnaw wires, packaging and furniture, and spread germs through droppings and urine. They breed fast, so a small problem becomes a large one in a short time. Moles tunnel through lawns, gardens and fields, push up mounds of soil and damage the roots of young plants, and they can be a nuisance on small farms and in kitchen gardens.

How to use it

Follow the label for placement and quantity. For rats, place small amounts of bait in protected spots near nesting holes, along walls and on runs where you see droppings and gnaw marks, using a covered tray or bait station where possible. For moles, follow the label's directions for placing the product in active runs, and avoid spreading it on the soil surface where other animals could find it. Check the bait regularly, replace it if it is eaten or wet, and stop when activity ends.

Good practice

  • Keep food, grain and feed in closed containers
  • Seal holes, gaps and cracks and clear rubbish and long grass near buildings
  • Collect dead rodents regularly while wearing gloves and dispose of them safely
  • Remove leftover bait at the end of the treatment and follow the label for disposal
  • Wash your hands after handling bait

Safety

Check the PCPB registration number on the label, read the label before use, and keep away from children, pets and livestock. Do not place bait where food, feed or water can be contaminated, and keep it away from poultry and other animals. If anyone swallows the bait, get medical help at once and show the pack or label. If an animal eats it, contact a vet immediately.

Storage

Keep the pack tightly closed in a cool, dry, locked place away from children, pets and food.
