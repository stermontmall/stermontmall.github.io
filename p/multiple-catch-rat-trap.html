MULTIPLE-CATCH LIVE RAT TRAP IN KENYA

The Multiple-Catch Live Rat Trap is a reusable mechanical enclosure intended to capture rats without relying on rodenticide bait. A multi-catch design can reduce the need to reset a single-capture device after every capture, depending on the exact mechanism and capacity. This listing describes a metal cage-style trap with a self-setting entry arrangement, but multiple-catch rat traps differ in design.

Confirm whether the supplied model is a live-capture unit, a snap-trap station or another mechanical system before use.

WHY CONSIDER A MULTIPLE-CATCH RAT TRAP

In some sites, rodenticide bait may be unsuitable because of food-safety requirements, non-target exposure concerns or the need to avoid chemical bait in a sensitive area. A mechanical trap provides an alternative that can be selected after a site risk assessment. A multiple-catch device may be useful in warehouses, stores, farms, service areas and other premises where rat activity is recurring.

The exact number of rats a trap can hold depends on its construction and should be confirmed from the supplier rather than assumed.

HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

Multiple-catch rat traps use one or more entry points and an internal mechanism designed to prevent captured rats from leaving. Some products use a live-capture chamber with one-way doors; others contain snap traps or a combination of mechanisms. The current product record identifies a mechanical live-capture enclosure, but the precise trigger arrangement and accessories are not confirmed.

Read the instructions supplied with the exact model before setting it. Do not add poison or modify the mechanism unless the manufacturer explicitly permits it.

SITE ASSESSMENT AND POSITIONING

Inspect the premises for droppings, gnaw marks, rub marks, tracks, damaged food packaging and other evidence of rat movement. Identify likely travel routes, feeding points and shelter areas. Position the trap on a stable surface along a suitable route and keep it away from children, pets, livestock and non-target animals.

Do not obstruct emergency exits or place the unit where it can be struck by vehicles or moved during cleaning. In food premises, follow the site's pest-management and hygiene plan.

BAIT AND SETTING

Some mechanical rat traps require bait; others rely on a trigger or entry mechanism. Confirm the exact model's instructions to determine whether bait is needed and which type is suitable. If bait is required, use only the recommended material and secure it as directed.

Do not use rodenticide bait in a trap unless the manufacturer and site programme expressly allow it. Keep fingers clear of moving parts and use the designated setting method. If the trap does not set or close correctly, do not use it until the fault is resolved.

INSPECTION AND ANIMAL WELFARE

A live-capture trap must be inspected frequently and should never be left unattended for extended periods. Follow the manufacturer's inspection schedule and local animal-welfare requirements. If an animal is captured, handle the trap carefully and follow the permitted procedure for removal or release.

Do not leave a captured rat without appropriate care. If the model uses snap traps rather than live capture, follow the manufacturer's safe servicing and disposal directions. Keep a service record showing location, inspection date and observations.

CLEANING AND REUSE

After service, clean and disinfect the trap according to the manufacturer instructions. Wear suitable protective equipment and avoid direct contact with droppings, urine or contaminated surfaces. Check entry doors, triggers, hinges, handles and the body for damage before reuse.

Allow the unit to dry fully and store it in a secure place away from children and pets.

PREVENT REINFESTATION

Mechanical trapping should be combined with sanitation and proofing. Store food and animal feed in sealed containers, keep refuse covered, remove spills and reduce clutter. Inspect door gaps, wall penetrations, drains and service entries for openings that may allow rats to enter.

Correct leaks and remove accessible nesting material. If rat activity persists, reassess the site and use a documented integrated rodent-management plan.
