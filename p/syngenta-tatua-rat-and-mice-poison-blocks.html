About Syngenta Tatua Rat and Mice Poison Blocks

Syngenta Tatua Rat and Mice Poison Blocks are bait blocks for rat and mice control in stores, farms, poultry houses and other premises, and they are made to be used inside bait stations. In a farm store or a poultry house, rodents eat and spoil feed and disturb the birds. A block is heavy and solid, so it resists damp and handling better than loose bait, and it can be fixed inside a station so that it is not scattered.

Where it helps

Rats and mice contaminate and eat stored food, chew wires, sacks and packaging, and can spread disease. Rodents can carry or gnaw a block, but a block held inside a station stays where you put it. That makes it easier to see which stations are being visited and to replace only the bait that has been eaten.

How to use it

Look for droppings, gnaw marks, runways and burrows to find where rodents travel. Place bait stations along walls, near holes, behind stacked goods and in dark corners, and put the blocks inside so that children, pets and livestock cannot reach them. Check the stations regularly, replace blocks that have been eaten, and move a station if there is no activity at that spot.

Before you start

  • Store grain, feed and produce on raised pallets, away from walls, in rodent-proof containers
  • Seal holes and gaps, and keep drains and doors closed or screened
  • Remove rubbish and clutter, and cut tall grass and bush near buildings
  • Have gloves ready, and plan where each station will stand

Safety

Check the PCPB registration number on the label and read the label before use. Use the blocks only inside bait stations, never on food or in food areas. Collect dead rodents with gloves, dispose of them safely and wash your hands afterwards. Keep the blocks away from children, pets and livestock.

Storage

Keep the blocks in their original pack, dry and locked away from children, pets and food.

Plan rodent management without exposing non-target animals

The catalogue presents Syngenta Tatua as bait blocks for rats and mice, intended for use in bait stations. Rodenticide blocks can seriously harm children, pets, livestock and wildlife if accessed or eaten. Before purchase, verify the product registration and read the current label for approved premises, station requirements, restrictions and disposal. If you cannot keep bait fully secured from non-target animals, use sanitation, proofing and other non-chemical measures or consult a qualified pest-control professional.

Start by removing food and water sources that attract rodents. Store feed and food in secure containers, clear spilled grain, manage rubbish, reduce clutter and close suitable gaps around buildings. These steps improve hygiene and reduce the chance of repeated rodent activity, whether or not a chemical control is legally appropriate.

  • Use only the bait station and security method specified by the label.
  • Never leave bait accessible to children, pets, livestock or wildlife.
  • Keep bait away from food, animal feed and water.

Inspect packaging and maintain secure records

Check that the pack is sealed, clearly labelled and within its expiry date. Store it in the original packaging in a locked cabinet separate from food, feed and medicines. Do not break blocks, transfer bait into unlabelled containers or improvise a station. Follow the label for inspection, replacement, removal and disposal, and keep a record of the product and any professional service.

Do not dispose of unused bait in open areas, drains or ordinary waste unless the label and local authority specifically allow it. If a child, pet or other animal may have accessed bait, treat it as urgent: seek medical or veterinary help immediately and provide the product label or packaging.
