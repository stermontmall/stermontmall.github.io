Why Choose Bait Blocks

Rats and mice contaminate and eat stored food, chew wires, sacks and packaging, and can spread disease. In poultry houses and farm stores, they also steal feed and disturb birds. Bait blocks are heavy, solid and made to resist damp and handling better than loose bait. Rodents can carry or gnaw them, and the blocks can be fixed inside a bait station so they do not get scattered.

How to Place Bait Blocks

Look for droppings, gnaw marks, runways and burrows to find where rodents travel. Place bait stations along walls, near holes, behind stacked goods and in dark corners, where rodents run. Put blocks inside the stations so that children, pets and livestock cannot reach them. Check the stations regularly, replace bait that has been eaten, and move stations if there is no activity at a location.

Prevention Tips

Store grain, feed and produce on raised pallets away from walls and in rodent-proof containers. Seal holes and gaps, keep drains and doors closed or screened, and remove rubbish and clutter. Cut tall grass and bush near buildings. Collect and dispose of dead rodents safely, using gloves, and wash your hands afterwards.
