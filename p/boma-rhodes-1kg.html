About Boma Rhodes 1kg

Product overview

Boma Rhodes 1kg is a pasture grass seed pack for farmers establishing grazing areas or producing hay for livestock. Boma Rhodes is valued for its soft, palatable herbage and its ability to form a persistent grass cover under suitable management. It is commonly used in pasture and fodder systems where farmers want a forage crop that can support cattle, sheep and goats. The 1kg pack is a practical quantity for a small plot, pasture trial or part of a larger field establishment programme. Boma Rhodes is described as suitable for altitudes of approximately 1,000 to 2,500 metres above sea level and is popular in medium-altitude areas. The stated maturity period is around 90 to 105 days under favourable growing conditions. Actual establishment and growth depend on rainfall, soil fertility, temperature, seed quality and management. Use the maturity information as a guide for planning, not as a fixed harvest date. The grass is noted for drought tolerance, but young seedlings still need moisture and protection while they establish. The variety is known for producing soft herbage and bottom tillers that help form a thicker grass stand. A dense pasture can help cover bare soil and provide forage when managed with suitable grazing intervals. Slow growth can be an advantage in systems where a persistent stand is preferred and grazing pressure is controlled. Farmers should still monitor pasture height and ground cover, because even a drought-tolerant grass can be weakened by continuous close grazing. Prepare the field before planting. Remove perennial weeds, clear unwanted shrubs and create a fine, firm seedbed. Break large clods so that small seeds can make good contact with moist soil. Avoid planting into waterlogged ground or on a surface where heavy runoff is likely to wash the seed away. Select a planting window when reliable rainfall is expected. If irrigation is available, plan to keep the seedbed moist during germination and early establishment. Follow the recommended seed rate and planting depth on the current pack label or local extension advice. Small pasture seeds should not be buried too deeply. If broadcasting, divide the seed into two portions and spread them in crossing directions to improve distribution. Lightly cover or press the seed into contact with the soil using a suitable method. Keep the area free from heavy livestock traffic until the seedlings are well established. Weed control during establishment is important. Weeds can compete with young grass for light, moisture and nutrients, creating gaps that later become difficult to repair. Scout the field regularly and use suitable mechanical or approved chemical control methods where necessary. Avoid damaging the new grass during weeding. If the site has a heavy perennial weed problem, plan control before sowing rather than relying on the pasture to suppress established weeds immediately. After the grass has formed a strong stand, manage it with rotational grazing or planned cutting. Avoid allowing animals to graze the pasture too early or too close to the ground. Give the grass time to recover between grazing periods so that it can maintain leaf area and root reserves. The correct rest period varies with rainfall and growth rate. A pasture that is repeatedly grazed before recovery can thin out and allow weeds to take over. Boma Rhodes can also be used for hay production. Cut at a stage that balances forage quality and available biomass, then dry the material properly before baling or stacking. Protect hay from rain and ground moisture. Poorly dried hay can heat or mould in storage, reducing feeding value and creating health risks. Store hay in a ventilated shed and inspect it for moisture or pest damage. Soil fertility has a major effect on pasture productivity. Where soil tests indicate deficiencies, apply suitable nutrients according to local recommendations. Do not assume drought tolerance means the grass requires no fertility management. Organic matter, good drainage and erosion control can help improve pasture establishment. On sloping land, plan field layout to slow runoff and protect the soil surface. The 1kg pack should be stored in its original packaging in a cool, dry place until planting. Protect it from direct sunlight, moisture and rodents. Check the label for seed lot information, purity and germination details before opening. If only part of the pack is used, close the package securely and keep it away from chemicals and fertilisers. Use the remaining seed within the supplier's recommended period. Boma Rhodes is a useful option for farmers seeking a soft, drought-tolerant pasture grass for grazing and hay. The best results come from good seedbed preparation, planting at the right time, early weed control and controlled grazing after establishment. Start with a manageable area, keep records of sowing and pasture performance, and expand when the grass has demonstrated that it suits the farm's altitude, soil and rainfall conditions.
