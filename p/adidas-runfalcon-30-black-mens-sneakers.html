For everyday Kenyan use, consider commuting, school, errands, travel, casual office outfits and the surfaces on which the shoes will be worn. Wet pavements, dusty paths and long periods of standing can place different demands on footwear. A lifestyle sneaker may suit ordinary daily wear but should not replace certified protective shoes in workshops, construction sites or industrial workplaces.

Clean the pair according to the confirmed material. Remove loose dirt with a soft brush or cloth, test any cleaner on a small hidden area and avoid soaking glued components. Knit and mesh panels need gentle cleaning; suede or suede-style surfaces may require a suitable brush.

Allow shoes to air-dry naturally away from direct heat, which can affect adhesives and synthetic materials.

Inspect the outsole, heel counter, stitching, eyelets and upper periodically for separation, worn tread or structural damage. Stop using the pair for demanding activity if damage affects fit or stability. Store footwear in a dry, ventilated place and allow it to dry after exposure to rain.

Rotating pairs between uses can help the lining air out and reduce trapped moisture.
