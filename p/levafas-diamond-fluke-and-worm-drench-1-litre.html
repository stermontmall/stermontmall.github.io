Levafas Diamond Fluke and Worm Drench (1 Litre)

Two active ingredients

  • Levamisole hydrochloride acts against roundworms and lungworms in the gut and the lungs
  • Oxyclozanide acts against flukes, which live in the liver and bile ducts

Combining the two in one drench lets farmers treat several parasites in one dose, which saves time and handling stress during routine deworming.

Why Worm and Fluke control matters

Internal parasites reduce growth, milk and meat production, and fertility. Roundworms and lungworms cause poor condition, diarrhoea and coughing. Flukes damage the liver and can cause weight loss, weakness and, in heavy infections, death. Young animals are the most at risk. Regular, well-timed treatment protects health and productivity and reduces the cost of treating sick animals later.

How to use it

Levafas Diamond is a suspension given by mouth as a drench. Shake the bottle well before use, because the active ingredients settle. Follow the dosage instructions on the label, which are based on the animal's body weight, and use an accurate drenching gun. Weigh the animals or estimate their weight carefully, and dose according to the heaviest animal in a group, to avoid under-dosing. Under-dosing can lead to worms becoming resistant to the drug.

Good practice

  • Deworm on a programme agreed with your vet
  • Treat all animals in a group at the same time
  • Keep new animals separate and treat them before they join the herd
  • Manage pasture and keep animals off wet areas where fluke snails live
  • Keep records of dates, products and doses
  • Rotate dewormers from different groups when advised

Safety and withdrawal

For veterinary use. Follow the label dose and the withdrawal periods for milk and meat. Wear gloves when handling and wash your hands afterwards. Keep out of reach of children. If you are not sure about the dose or timing, ask your vet. Do not let rinse water reach streams or ponds.

Storage

Store in the original closed bottle in a cool, dry place away from direct sun and food.
