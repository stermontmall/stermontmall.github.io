Product overview

WQ CB-2L 2-Litre Calf Nursing Bottle with Silicone Teat and Measurement Marks in Kenya The WQ CB-2L is a two-litre nursing bottle designed to help farmers feed calves with measured quantities of milk or an appropriate liquid feed. The bottle has graduated measurement marks on its body and is supplied with a silicone teat.

Uses & applications

It is intended for dairy farmers, smallholder livestock keepers and calf rearers who want a portable feeding tool that is easier to portion than an ordinary bucket. The stated capacity is two litres and the bottle length is approximately 34 centimetres. Who may find this bottle useful The WQ CB-2L may suit dairy farms, smallholder farmers, calf-rearing units and livestock keepers who need to give measured milk feeds to individual calves.

It can be useful when monitoring the intake of an orphan calf, a calf being raised separately or an animal on a controlled feeding programme.

How to use & application

The teat is described as replaceable, allowing a worn component to be changed without necessarily replacing the entire bottle. Measured feeding for calves The measurement marks allow the keeper to see how much liquid has been placed in the bottle. Accurate portioning can make it easier to follow a feeding plan, record intake and compare the amount offered to different calves.

The correct volume and feeding frequency depend on the calf's age, body weight, health, milk programme and advice from a qualified livestock professional. This bottle is a feeding aid, not a feeding chart. Follow the farm's calf-rearing plan and veterinary or extension guidance rather than using the bottle's full capacity as a target for every calf.

Silicone teat for controlled suckling The supplied teat is made from silicone, according to the supplier. A teat encourages the calf to suck rather than drink from an open container, and it can be useful when feeding an animal that is being reared away from its dam or needs closer observation. Replace the teat when it shows signs of wear and use only a compatible replacement.

Correct feeding position and handling During bottle feeding, hold the bottle in a position that allows the calf to suck in a natural, comfortable posture.

Safety information

The teat should be checked before each use for cracks, splits, blockages or a hole that has become too large. A damaged or worn teat can allow liquid to flow too quickly, making feeding uncomfortable and increasing the risk of coughing or aspiration.

Product details

Do not force liquid into the animal's mouth or squeeze the bottle to make it flow faster. Allow the calf to suck at its own pace and stop if it coughs, struggles or appears distressed. Teat: silicone and replaceable.

Intended animal: calves.

Care, cleaning & storage

If a calf is weak, ill or unable to suck normally, seek veterinary advice instead of attempting to solve the problem by changing the bottle technique. Keep the teat clean and do not share feeding equipment between sick and healthy calves without proper cleaning and disinfection. Cleaning after every feed Milk residue can spoil quickly, especially in warm conditions.

Wash the bottle and teat promptly after each feeding using a suitable cleaning routine. The supplier recommends thorough cleaning with mild dish soap. Use a bottle brush where appropriate to remove residue from the inside, rinse thoroughly so that no soap remains and allow all parts to dry fully.

Pay particular attention to the teat opening and the area where the teat joins the bottle. Store the clean, dry bottle in a protected place away from dust, flies, chemicals and livestock contamination. Replaceable teat and maintenance A replaceable teat is useful because the teat is a working component that experiences repeated pulling and cleaning.

Inspect it regularly and replace it if the silicone becomes torn, hardened, sticky or stretched. Check that the teat fits securely and does not leak around the neck. Inspect the bottle body for cracks and the measurement markings for readability.

The bottle is portable and can be carried between pens, but it should be cleaned between animals according to farm hygiene procedures. If several calves are being fed, organise a routine that keeps each bottle and teat clean and avoids cross-contamination.
