WHY CONSIDER A PETROL-POWERED KNAPSACK SPRAYER?

A powered pump can be useful when the work involves repeated spraying across multiple rows or sections and the operator wants to avoid continuous manual pumping. Because the unit is carried as a backpack, it can move with the operator where a trolley machine may be difficult to position. This format may suit farms with paths, orchard rows, garden beds or uneven areas where mobility matters. The 25-litre tank offers a substantial liquid capacity, but the weight of a full tank must be considered. Plan refilling points and work intervals, and do not assume that a large tank should always be filled to maximum capacity.

UNDERSTANDING THE FOUR-STROKE ENGINE

The AC767-4 is listed with a four-stroke petrol engine. Four-stroke engines generally use petrol in the fuel tank and engine oil separately in the crankcase. This is different from many two-stroke engines that require a petrol-and-oil fuel mixture. Never guess the fuel preparation method: read the engine manual, identify the fuel and oil fill points, and follow the manufacturer's specified grade and service interval. Check the engine oil level before starting and place the machine on a stable surface when refuelling. Switch off and allow hot components to cool before adding fuel. Keep petrol away from flames, sparks and smoking.

PUMP, LANCE AND SPRAY CONTROL

The supplier identifies a high-pressure plunger pump and adjustable nozzle. The pump moves liquid from the tank through the hose and lance. The nozzle controls the delivery pattern within its design range. A suitable pattern depends on the approved product, target, surface and label instructions. A narrower pattern is not automatically better, and a wider pattern does not guarantee even coverage. Check the nozzle for wear or blockage, inspect hose connections and seals, and test the machine with clean water in a safe place before using any treatment mixture. Do not direct a high-pressure stream toward people, animals, electrical equipment or fragile surfaces.

PLANNING A SPRAYING JOB

Before filling the tank, identify the area to be treated and estimate the liquid quantity using the product label and a calibrated application method. Avoid mixing more solution than the job requires. Mark the treatment boundary and keep people, livestock and pets away. Check wind direction and avoid conditions that could carry droplets beyond the target. Protect water sources, drains, food, feed and neighbouring crops from drift or runoff. For a large property, divide the work into manageable sections and keep a record of the product used, dilution, date, area and operator. This makes follow-up and safe management easier.

CLEANING AND ROUTINE MAINTENANCE

Use the approved disposal method. Clean the tank, filter, hose, lance and nozzle as directed, taking care to prevent contact with chemical residues. Inspect clamps, seals, hose condition, straps, tank cap and pump connections. Keep the engine air intake and cooling areas clear in accordance with the manual.
